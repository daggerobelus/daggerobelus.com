HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Bulkeley MS 169, Page 17
===================================================

This is a single hand throughout the page. It is a fairly fluent secretary
hand with italic influences. The writing is relatively small and dense,
with moderate spacing between words. The hand leans slightly forward.
The page appears to be a medical/herbal text about lavender.

Page number "7" appears in the upper right corner.

LOWERCASE LETTERS

a - Two forms: (1) a single-compartment a with an open top, fairly rounded.
    (2) occasionally a more closed form. The scribe is fairly consistent.
    Example words: Lavander, and, that, also
    Confusion risks: o (when closed), u

b - Straight or slightly curved ascender with a round bowl.
    Example words: be, but, blood, before
    Confusion risks: l (ascender), h

c - Small curved stroke open to the right.
    Example words: cold, composition, cause
    Confusion risks: e, r, t

d - Secretary d with looped ascender curving back to the left. Quite
    consistent.
    Example words: distilled, dried, degree
    Confusion risks: cl (the loop)

e - Reversed loop form, fairly small. Sometimes hasty and hard to
    distinguish from o.
    Example words: the, be, degrees, water
    Confusion risks: o (primary confusion), a (when open)

f - Long descending stroke with crossbar.
    Example words: for, flowers, first, falling
    Confusion risks: long-s (the main confusion)

g - Round bowl with descending tail curving left.
    Example words: good, great
    Confusion risks: y (descender)

h - Ascender with curved right stroke that sometimes descends below
    baseline.
    Example words: hot, herbs, hath
    Confusion risks: b, ln combination

i - Simple minim with dot. Dot is sometimes displaced or absent.
    Example words: it, is, in
    Confusion risks: minim confusion (m, n, u)

k - Looped ascender with kick.
    Example words: kindes
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

l - Simple tall stroke. Sometimes difficult to distinguish from other
    ascenders.
    Example words: Lavander, also, all
    Confusion risks: b, tall-s

m - Three minims, fairly evenly spaced.
    Example words: many, much, mixt
    Confusion risks: nn, ni (minim confusion)

n - Two minims.
    Example words: not, nor, nature
    Confusion risks: u, m (minim confusion)

o - Round, closed or nearly closed form.
    Example words: of, or, one
    Confusion risks: e (reversed loop), a

p - Descender with bowl. The descender sometimes has a curve at the bottom.
    Example words: put, passions
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

q - Round bowl with descending tail going right.
    Example words: qualitie
    Confusion risks: p (mirror)

r - Short stroke with shoulder; also 2-shaped variant.
    Example words: are, reason, receipts
    Confusion risks: v (2-shaped), n

s - Long-s (initial/medial) and round s (final).
    Example words: smelt (long-s), passions (round s)
    Confusion risks: f (long-s vs f)

t - Short upright with crossbar.
    Example words: that, the, to, third
    Confusion risks: c

u/v - v at word start, u in middle. Standard.
    Example words: vnto, vpon, use
    Confusion risks: n (minim)

w - Broad multi-stroke letter.
    Example words: water, way, which, without
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

x - Crossed strokes.
    Example words: mixt
    Confusion risks: Rarely used

y - Descending stroke, sometimes dotted.
    Example words: yet, you, your
    Confusion risks: thorn

z - Not clearly visible.

UPPERCASE LETTERS

L - Large decorative form. Seen in "Lavander."

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES

th - Very common, connected. Does not appear to use thorn.
-es graph - Terminal looped downstroke visible at word ends.
-er graph - Hook at end of words.
st - Connected pair.

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)

1. f / long-s -- The most critical confusion point in this hand.
2. Minim confusion (m/n/u/i) -- Dense text makes this worse.
3. e / o -- The small letterforms in this cramped hand make these
   particularly hard to distinguish.
4. c / r / t -- Small letterform confusion.
5. a / o -- When a is closed.
6. d / cl -- The secretary d loop vs c+l.
7. Dense passages -- When the scribe writes quickly, letterforms degrade.
