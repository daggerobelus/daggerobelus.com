HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Brumwich MS 160, Page 10
===================================================

This is a two-page opening with extremely dense text in a small, somewhat
hurried hand. The hand appears to be a secretary/italic mix. The left
page (verso, numbered 6 at top left) and right page (recto, numbered 7
at top right) appear to be in the same hand. The text is very cramped
and the bottom of the left page shows water damage or staining.

LOWERCASE LETTERS

a - Small, fairly round, sometimes open at top. In this small hand it is
    often hard to distinguish from o.
    Example words: and, water, take
    Confusion risks: o (high risk in this small hand), e

b - Short ascender with small bowl. The ascender is sometimes hard to see
    in the dense text.
    Example words: be, but, blood, boile
    Confusion risks: l, h

c - Tiny curved stroke. Very hard to see in cramped passages.
    Example words: cold, cure
    Confusion risks: e, r, t (very high risk in this hand)

d - Secretary d with small looped ascender.
    Example words: and, dried, drinke
    Confusion risks: cl, a (when loop is small)

e - Small reversed loop or simple curve. Very hard to distinguish from
    other small letters in this hand.
    Example words: the, take, one
    Confusion risks: o, c, a (all high risk)

f - Descending stroke with crossbar, but crossbar is sometimes faint.
    Example words: for, from, first
    Confusion risks: long-s (critical risk)

g - Small bowl with descender.
    Example words: good, great
    Confusion risks: y (descender)

h - Small ascender with curved right stroke.
    Example words: the, hath, herbs
    Confusion risks: b, ln

i - Tiny minim, dot often displaced or invisible.
    Example words: in, it, is
    Confusion risks: minim confusion (extreme risk in this small hand)

k - Looped form.
    Example words: take, make
    Confusion risks: Distinctive enough

l - Thin tall stroke.
    Example words: all, till
    Confusion risks: b, i (when short)

m - Three tiny minims.
    Example words: make, them
    Confusion risks: nn, ni, iu (extreme risk)

n - Two tiny minims.
    Example words: and, in, one
    Confusion risks: u, m, ii (extreme risk)

o - Tiny round form.
    Example words: of, one, or
    Confusion risks: a, e (extreme risk)

p - Descender with bowl.
    Example words: put, pound
    Confusion risks: Fairly distinctive

q - Small bowl with rightward tail.
    Example words: quarter
    Confusion risks: p

r - Short form, sometimes 2-shaped.
    Example words: are, red, rose
    Confusion risks: v, n, c

s - Long-s and round s.
    Example words: some (long-s), roses (round s)
    Confusion risks: f (long-s)

t - Small upright with crossbar.
    Example words: the, to, that
    Confusion risks: c, r

u/v - Standard convention.
    Example words: vpon, vnto, use
    Confusion risks: n (extreme minim risk)

w - Multi-stroke broad letter.
    Example words: water, with, well
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

y - Descending letter.
    Example words: you, your
    Confusion risks: thorn, g

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES

th - Connected pair. Very common.
-es graph - Terminal form at word ends.
st - Connected.
ff - Double-f for capital F.

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)

1. Minim confusion (m/n/u/i) -- EXTREME risk in this very small, cramped hand.
   This is the most challenging aspect of this manuscript.
2. e / o / a -- All three small round letters are nearly indistinguishable
   in many instances in this hand.
3. f / long-s -- Standard risk.
4. c / r / t -- Small letterform confusion, high in cramped passages.
5. Water damage -- The bottom portion of the left page has significant
   staining that obscures text.
6. Overall legibility -- Much of the text on both pages is at the edge of
   legibility due to the very small hand size and dense layout.
