HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Jane Jackson MS 373, Page 20
======================================================

This is a two-page opening with a single predominant hand. The hand is a
fairly neat secretary/italic mix, somewhat larger and more legible than the
Brumwich manuscript but still quite dense. The left page (verso) has
significant water/stain damage at the bottom third. The right page (recto)
is numbered 9 (or possibly another number) in the upper right corner.

The hand has a forward lean and is fairly fluent. Word spacing is moderate.
Some passages are more cramped than others.

LOWERCASE LETTERS

a - Open-topped round form. Fairly consistent.
    Example words: and, Take, water, are
    Confusion risks: o (when closed), u

b - Ascender with round bowl. Generally clear.
    Example words: be, but, boile, blood
    Confusion risks: l (ascender), h

c - Small curved stroke open to right.
    Example words: cold, cure, come
    Confusion risks: e, r, t

d - Secretary d with looped ascender curving left. Some italic d forms
    also present.
    Example words: and, dried, distill, drinke
    Confusion risks: cl (the loop)

e - Small reversed loop form. Fairly well-formed in this hand.
    Example words: the, take, one, water
    Confusion risks: o (main risk), c

f - Long descender with crossbar.
    Example words: for, from, first
    Confusion risks: long-s

g - Round bowl with leftward descender.
    Example words: good, great, ginger
    Confusion risks: y (descender)

h - Ascender with curved right stroke, sometimes descending below baseline.
    Example words: the, hath, halfe, herbs
    Confusion risks: b, ln

i - Single minim with dot. Dots generally present.
    Example words: in, it, is
    Confusion risks: minim confusion (m, n, u)

k - Looped ascender with kick.
    Example words: take, make
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

l - Tall single stroke.
    Example words: all, like, till
    Confusion risks: b, tall-s

m - Three minims.
    Example words: make, them, mortar
    Confusion risks: nn, ni (minim)

n - Two minims.
    Example words: and, in, one, not
    Confusion risks: u, m (minim)

o - Round, closed form.
    Example words: of, one, or, on
    Confusion risks: a, e

p - Descender with bowl.
    Example words: put, pound, pepper
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

q - Small bowl with rightward tail.
    Example words: quarter, quantitie
    Confusion risks: p

r - Short form with shoulder; 2-shaped variant in some positions.
    Example words: are, rose, red
    Confusion risks: v, n, c

s - Long-s (initial/medial) and round s (final).
    Example words: some (long-s), roses (round s)
    Confusion risks: f (long-s)

t - Short upright with crossbar.
    Example words: the, to, that, take
    Confusion risks: c

u/v - Standard convention (v initial, u medial).
    Example words: vpon, vnto, use
    Confusion risks: n (minim)

w - Broad multi-stroke letter.
    Example words: water, with, well
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

x - Crossed strokes.
    Example words: [rare on this page]
    Confusion risks: N/A

y - Descending stroke, sometimes dotted.
    Example words: you, your, yet
    Confusion risks: thorn (y=th)

UPPERCASE LETTERS

A - Large form with crossbar. Seen in recipe openings.
T - Large decorated form seen in "Take."
F - Sometimes rendered as ff (double-f).
M - Seen in various recipe titles.

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES

th - Common connected pair.
-es graph - Terminal looped downstroke.
-er graph - Hook form.
st - Connected.
ff - Double-f for capital F.

SPECIAL FEATURES
- The left page bottom has significant water/stain damage.
- Some recipe titles appear to be written in a slightly larger or more
  decorative form of the same hand.
- The right page appears to have slightly different ink in some sections,
  possibly written at a different time.

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)

1. Water damage (left page bottom) -- Text obscured; large portions illegible.
2. f / long-s -- Standard risk, exacerbated by the forward-leaning hand.
3. Minim confusion (m/n/u/i) -- Moderate risk; this hand is clearer than
   Brumwich but still presents challenges.
4. e / o -- Small letterform confusion.
5. c / r / t -- Confusion among small letters.
6. a / o -- When a is closed.
