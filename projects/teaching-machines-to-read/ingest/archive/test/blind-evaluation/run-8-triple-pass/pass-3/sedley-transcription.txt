CONFIDENCE NOTES
================

This manuscript is significantly harder to read than Henslow. The hand is small and compressed, and parts of the text (especially at margins and edges) are partially obscured.

LEFT PAGE (RECIPE XXVII):
1. "[battered?]" — The letterforms show b-a-t-t and then minims. Could be "battered" or "beaten."
2. "[parted?]" — Ambiguous minims; could also be "poured" or "purged."
3. "[agreeable?]" — Long word with several ambiguous letters in the middle.
4. "bolt of [boxe?]" — The last word is unclear. Could be "boxe" (box wood) which would make sense as a stirring implement.
5. "[hoxe?]" — Very unclear word near the end of the recipe.
6. "[fashionably?]" — Long word, partially legible. The f and -ly ending seem clear but middle is uncertain.
7. "[character?]" — Unclear what this word is; the letterforms are ambiguous.

RIGHT PAGE (CONTINUATION + RECIPES XXVIII AND XXIX):
8. "[boate?]" — Unclear; could be "both" or "boate."
9. "ffellones" — On re-examination this reads more clearly: double-ff (= capital F) + ellones = Fellons (abscesses/boils). Common medical term.
10. "[Covill?]" — The name in the recipe XXVIII heading is partially unclear. Could be "Covill" or "Colvill."
11. "[gillyflowers?]" — On re-examination appears to be a single compound word for gillyflowers (carnations/pinks, a common recipe ingredient).
12. "[Sallanders?]" — In Recipe XXIX, this appears to be a plant name but the letterforms are not entirely clear. Could be "Sallanders" or "Sallenders."
13. "for" — On re-examination, what I initially read as "gor" is likely "for" with the f formed in a way that resembles g in this hand.
14. "&c" — The end of Recipe XXIX appears to end with "&c." (et cetera).
