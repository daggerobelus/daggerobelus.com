My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge putt thereto a handefull of violett leaues
[Landegreese?] leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit-
togeather of euerie one a the quantitye . . . . .

. . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handefull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and [rake?] them in the [imbers?] untill they bee well rosted
then take them, and putt awaye the Coleworte leafe, and grounde
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them togeather
then take it and laye it to the pyles withe a [trosser?] and vse this
morninge and evenynge . . . . .

. . . . ffor the flixe . . . . .

Take horsedonge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and putt it in a Clothe and laye itt to the fundamente as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and soe vppon untill you haue ease

. . . . ffor Deafnes . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche growes vppon the flanke
of a sheepe and [tarre?] and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare

CONFIDENCE NOTES
================

Line-by-line notes on uncertain or difficult readings:

1. "Caunterberries" — The letterforms are clear; this is an unusual spelling of Canterbury's. The final -ies is clear.

2. "[Landegreese?]" — This word is partially obscured and the letterforms are not entirely clear. It appears to be a compound plant name beginning with "Land-". Could be "Langdebeefe" (an early modern name for bugloss/ox-tongue) or another plant name. The second half of the compound is uncertain.

3. "[imbers?]" — The first letter(s) are unclear. The word appears to refer to embers (hot coals), consistent with "rosted." The initial letters are partially obscured. Could be "imbers" or "embers."

4. "mollett" — The letterforms spell m-o-l-l-e-t-t. This likely refers to mallow (malva) leaves, but I transcribe what I see.

5. "[rake?]" — The first letter could be r or b. If b, the word is "bake." If r, "rake" (meaning to place in embers). Both make sense with "in the embers."

6. "grounde" — On re-examination, the u appears to be present though lightly formed.

7. "[trosser?]" — The word is partially unclear. I can see t-r and then several minims followed by what appears to be -er. Could be "trosser" (a cloth truss or pad) or "troster."

8. "horsedonge" — The letterforms are clear. This is horse dung, a common remedy ingredient in the period.

9. "vppon" — On re-examination, this reads "vppon" (upon) with doubled p.

10. "haue" — On re-examination, this reads "haue" (have). The minims resolve to h-a-u-e, giving "haue ease" = "have ease."

11. "Deafnes" — On re-examination, the title reads "ffor Deafnes" not "ffor Dishes." The letterforms after the D show e-a-f-n-e-s. The remedy (putting wool and tar into the ear) confirms this reading — it is a treatment for deafness.

12. "[tarre?]" — The word appears to begin with t and contains r. Could be "tarre" (tar) which would make sense as an ingredient mixed with wool for an ear remedy.
