HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Henslow MS 688, Page 12
=================================================

General Observations:
- Clear secretary hand with some italic influence
- Titles written larger with decorative flourishes
- Well-spaced, generally legible
- Consistent hand throughout the page
- Ink is dark and well-preserved

LOWERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

a: Open-topped, formed with a rounded bowl and a short upstroke on the right.
   Examples: "Take" (multiple instances), "handefull", "quantitye"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with u when hastily formed
   Variants: Fairly consistent throughout

b: Formed with a tall ascender and a rounded bowl at the baseline. The ascender is straight, not looped.
   Examples: "Brothe", "before", "Borrrage"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with l in unclear instances
   Variants: One form

c: Small, open curve facing right, similar to modern c but sometimes slightly angular.
   Examples: "Caunteberries", "Consumptione", "Coleworte"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with e or t when small
   Variants: One main form

d: Has a looped ascender curving back to the left (typical secretary d).
   Examples: "lorde", "handefull", "and"
   Confusion risks: The loop could be confused with cl or el
   Variants: One form with characteristic leftward loop

e: Secretary e — a reversed loop form, sometimes open at the top.
   Examples: "the", "before", "leaues"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with o when loop is closed
   Variants: Generally consistent

f: A long descender below the baseline with a crossbar.
   Examples: "for", "full", "flixe"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with long-s (which lacks full crossbar)
   Variants: Double-ff used for capital F ("ffor" = "For")

g: A small bowl at the baseline with a descending tail that curves left.
   Examples: "Borrage", "grounde", "grapes"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with y if tail is unclear
   Variants: One main form

h: A tall ascender with a curved stroke descending below the baseline.
   Examples: "the", "handefull", "them"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with b when descender is unclear
   Variants: Generally consistent

i: Short minim stroke, sometimes with a dot above.
   Examples: "in", "itt", "like"
   Confusion risks: Easily confused with first stroke of m, n, u
   Variants: One form; j used interchangeably at times

k: Formed with a tall ascender and two angular strokes. Fairly elaborate.
   Examples: "Take", "blacke"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with h or b
   Variants: One form

l: A tall ascender, straight or slightly curved, with a small foot.
   Examples: "leaues", "like", "laye"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with b or h
   Variants: One form

m: Three minim strokes connected at the baseline.
   Examples: "them", "morter", "maye"
   Confusion risks: Easily confused with sequence of minims (in, ni, etc.)
   Variants: One form

n: Two minim strokes connected.
   Examples: "and", "one", "new"
   Confusion risks: Easily confused with u, and with minims of m
   Variants: One form

o: Round bowl, closed.
   Examples: "of", "one", "Coleworte"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with secretary e when e's loop is full
   Variants: One form

p: Descender below baseline with a bowl on the right side.
   Examples: "putt", "pyles", "put"
   Confusion risks: The "special p" forms for pre-, pro-, per-
   Variants: Standard p and possibly modified p for abbreviations

q: Small bowl with a descending tail curving right.
   Examples: "quantitye"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with g
   Variants: One form

r: Multiple forms visible. Sometimes like a modern r, sometimes a 2-shaped mark.
   Examples: "Brothe", "roses", "wrappe"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with v in some contexts
   Variants: At least two forms (standard and 2-shaped)

s: Two forms: long-s (resembling f without full crossbar) used initially/medially, round s at end of words.
   Examples: long-s in "savinge"; round s in "leaues", "roses"
   Confusion risks: Long-s easily confused with f
   Variants: Long-s and round-s

t: Short vertical stroke with a crossbar, not much taller than minim height.
   Examples: "the", "Take", "them"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with c or e in some hands
   Variants: One main form

u/v: Used interchangeably per period convention. v at start of words, u medially.
   Examples: "vpon" (upon), "leaues" (leaves), "euerie"
   Confusion risks: u confused with n, v confused with r
   Variants: v initial, u medial

w: Formed with two v-shapes connected, creating a wide letter.
   Examples: "wrappe", "with", "well"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with vv
   Variants: One form

x: Formed with two crossing diagonal strokes.
   Examples: "flixe"
   Confusion risks: Minimal — rare letter
   Variants: One form

y: Descender curving below the line, sometimes with a dot above.
   Examples: "you", "yee", "pyles"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with thorn (representing "th")
   Variants: One form; may also represent thorn

z: Not clearly observed on this page.

UPPERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

B: Large form of b with decorative flourish on the ascender.
   Examples: "Brothe", "Borrage"

C: Large curved letter, clearly distinguishable.
   Examples: "Caunteberries", "Consumptione", "Coleworte"

D: Large looped form.
   Examples: "Dishes" (title at bottom)

F: Represented by "ff" (double-f) per convention or large single F.
   Examples: "ffor" in titles, "For"

M: Large, three-humped form.
   Examples: "My" (opening word)

S: Large round form.
   Examples: "Strawberrie"

T: Large form with extended crossbar.
   Examples: "Take" (at start of each recipe)

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
----------------------------------

th: Very common; formed as connected t + h. Watch for thorn (y-like form = "the").
   Examples: "the", "them", "thereto"

sh: s + h connected.
   Examples: "sheepe"

ch: c + h connected.
   Examples: appearing in various words

ff: Double-f represents capital F.
   Examples: possible instances in text

-es: Terminal -es graph — a looped downstroke at end of words.
   Examples: "leaues", "roses", "grapes"

-er: Hook-shaped upstroke.
   Examples: "morter", "butter"

-tion/-sion: Abbreviated with tilde over vowel.
   Examples: "Consumptione"

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)
---------------------------------------------------

1. e / o — Secretary e's reversed loop can look like o when fully closed
2. long-s / f — Distinguished only by crossbar presence
3. u / n — Both are minim-based; context-dependent
4. minim sequences (m/n/u/i) — Three minims could be m, ni, in, iu, etc.
5. c / t — Small c can resemble t in this hand
6. d / cl — Looped secretary d can look like cl
7. y / thorn — Both have descenders; y may represent "th"
8. h / b — Descender of h can obscure distinction from b's bowl
9. r / v — The 2-shaped r can resemble v
