HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Brumwich MS 160, Page 10
=================================================

This is a two-page opening (verso and recto) with dense, small writing.
The hand is a rapid secretary-italic hybrid. There may be more than one
hand present. The writing is significantly smaller and more cramped than
the previous manuscripts. The pages are numbered (left page appears to
be "6" and right page "7").

The text is arranged in multiple recipe entries on both pages. The left
page has several distinct recipe sections, and the right page also has
multiple entries. Some text appears to be in a different, slightly more
formal hand.

Due to the small size and density of the writing, this manuscript is
significantly more challenging to read than the previous ones. Many
letterforms are compressed and abbreviated.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
======================

a - Small open-topped form. Very compressed.
    Example words: and, a, are, after
    Confusion risks: o, u, e (all very close in this tiny hand)
    Variants: One main form

b - Small ascender with rounded bowl.
    Example words: be, black, butter, boyle
    Confusion risks: h, l
    Variants: One main form

c - Tiny curved stroke.
    Example words: cold, cure, chamber
    Confusion risks: e, r, t
    Variants: One form

d - Small secretary d with leftward loop.
    Example words: drinke, dose, decoction
    Confusion risks: cl
    Variants: One form

e - Reversed loop, very small.
    Example words: the, these, ever
    Confusion risks: o (extremely similar at this size)
    Variants: One form

f - Vertical with crossbar.
    Example words: for, from, first
    Confusion risks: Long-s
    Variants: ff = F

g - Small bowl with descender.
    Example words: good, great, ginger
    Confusion risks: y
    Variants: One form

h - Standard secretary h, compressed.
    Example words: halfe, hott, have
    Confusion risks: b, ln
    Variants: One form

i - Simple minim.
    Example words: it, in, is
    Confusion risks: n, u, m strokes
    Variants: One form

k - Looped form.
    Example words: knowe, kinde
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

l - Single ascender stroke.
    Example words: like, last, lay
    Confusion risks: h, b, tall i
    Variants: One form

m - Three minims, very compressed.
    Example words: make, much, may
    Confusion risks: in, ni (very high risk at this size)
    Variants: One form

n - Two minims.
    Example words: not, new, nor
    Confusion risks: u (identical)
    Variants: One form

o - Round, tiny.
    Example words: of, or, on, one
    Confusion risks: e (extremely high)
    Variants: One form

p - Descender with bowl.
    Example words: put, pound, pint
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: May have special forms

q - Bowl with descender.
    Example words: quarter, quart
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

r - Short r and 2-shaped form.
    Example words: receipt, roses
    Confusion risks: c, v
    Variants: Two forms

s - Long-s and round-s.
    Example words: said, sugar, spooneful
    Confusion risks: f (for long-s)
    Variants: Two standard forms

t - Short crossbar form.
    Example words: take, the, that
    Confusion risks: c, l
    Variants: One form

u/v - Standard minim/angular forms.
    Example words: upon/vpon, use
    Confusion risks: n (for u)
    Variants: v initial, u medial

w - Double-v form.
    Example words: with, water, well
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

y - Descender with dot.
    Example words: you, your
    Confusion risks: g, thorn
    Variants: One form

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
===================================

th - Very common
st - Joined
-es/-er graphs present
& - Ampersand used
ff = F

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (highest to lowest)
===================================================

1. e / o - Nearly indistinguishable at this size
2. n / u - Identical minims, impossibly small
3. m / in / ni / iu - Multiple minim confusion
4. Long-s / f - Crossbar distinction very subtle
5. a / o / u / e - All round forms blur together
6. c / r / t - Overlapping in rapid writing
7. h / b / l - Ascender confusion
8. y / g - Descender confusion
9. d / cl - Loop confusion
10. General: The extreme compression of this hand makes
    almost all distinctions more difficult than normal.
