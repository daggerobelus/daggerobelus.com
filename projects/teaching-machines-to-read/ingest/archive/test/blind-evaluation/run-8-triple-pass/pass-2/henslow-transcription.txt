My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before savinge putt thereto a handsfull of violett leaues
langdebeefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knitt
togeathor of euerie one a like quantitye. . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handsfull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Coleworte
leafe, and rake them in the [imbers?] untill they bee well rosted
then take them, and putt awaye the Coleworte leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them togeathor
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a trusser, and vse this
morninge and eueninge. . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the fflixe . . . . . .

Take horsedeunge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter,
and putt it in a clothe and laye itt to the fundamente as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and lay vppen untill yow haue ease.

. . . . . . ffor Diftues . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche growes vpon the fflanke
of a sheepe and [barne?], and mingell them togeathor
and putt them into the eare.

CONFIDENCE NOTES
================

1. "langdebeefe" - High confidence. The letterforms are clear. This is
   langdebeefe/lang-de-boeuf (ox-tongue/bugloss), a known recipe ingredient.

2. "[imbers?]" - Medium confidence. The first letter is ambiguous - could be
   'i' forming "imbers" or possibly 'e' forming "embers." The word appears to
   refer to embers/coals. The letterforms suggest an initial minim stroke but
   the exact letter is uncertain.

3. "mollett" - High confidence. Letterforms are clear. This likely refers to
   mallow (malva) leaves, rendered as "mollett" by this scribe.

4. "trusser" - Medium-high confidence. The letterforms are readable. Could
   also possibly be "trussee" but the final letter appears to be an -er graph.

5. "Diftues" - Medium confidence. The title word is written in a larger display
   hand. The letterforms suggest "Diftues" but this is an unusual word. Could
   possibly be "Diftnes" - the middle letters are somewhat ornate and ambiguous.

6. "[barne?]" - Medium confidence. The word is partially obscured near the edge
   of the page. Some letterforms visible but the reading is uncertain. Could be
   "barne" (a dialectal word) or another word.

7. "maye suffer ytt" - High confidence. The doubled terminal consonant (ytt)
   is consistent with this scribe's habits (putt, itt).

8. "vpon" - High confidence. Standard early modern spelling with initial v for u.
