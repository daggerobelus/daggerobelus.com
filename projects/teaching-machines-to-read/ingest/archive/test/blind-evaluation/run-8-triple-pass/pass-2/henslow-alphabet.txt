HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Henslow MS 688, Page 12
=================================================

This scribe writes in a careful, consistent secretary hand with well-formed
letterforms and relatively even spacing. The hand is upright with moderate
letter size and clear word separation.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
======================

a - Open-topped form, similar to modern 'a' but sometimes with a wider bowl.
    Rounded and fairly consistent throughout.
    Example words: handsfull, Caunteberries, quantitye, Take
    Confusion risks: o (when hastily formed), u
    Variants: One main form; occasionally slightly more angular

b - Standard secretary form with a tall ascender and rounded bowl.
    The ascender sometimes curves slightly leftward.
    Example words: Brothe, before, Borrrage, blacke
    Confusion risks: h (when descender of h is short), l (at a glance)
    Variants: One main form

c - Small rounded form, open to the right, similar to modern 'c'.
    Example words: Caunteberries, Consumptione, Coleworte, clothe
    Confusion risks: e (the reversed loop of e can look like c), r
    Variants: One main form; majuscule C is larger with a sweeping curve

d - Looped ascender curving back to the left (standard secretary d).
    The loop is relatively large and distinctive.
    Example words: handsfull, and, morninge, fundamente
    Confusion risks: Could potentially be confused with cl ligature
    Variants: One main form

e - Reversed loop form (backward-facing), typical of secretary hand.
    Formed with a small clockwise loop.
    Example words: leaues, before, Strawberrie, eueninge
    Confusion risks: o (very similar when small), c
    Variants: One main form; terminal -e sometimes slightly different

f - Long vertical stroke with a crossbar extending to the right.
    Distinguished from long-s by the presence of the crossbar.
    Example words: for, fflixe, fflanke, full
    Confusion risks: Long-s (very similar without crossbar)
    Variants: ff = capital F (fflixe, fflanke); single f in other positions

g - Rounded bowl with a descender that curves to the left, sometimes
    forming a closed loop below the baseline.
    Example words: Borrage, togeathor, morninge, eueninge
    Confusion risks: y (the descender loops can look similar)
    Variants: One main form

h - Standard secretary h with a descender that loops below the baseline.
    The ascender is tall and the descender curves leftward.
    Example words: handsfull, thereto, the, them
    Confusion risks: b (if descender is not visible), ln combination
    Variants: One main form

i - Simple minim stroke with a dot/tittle above.
    Example words: in, violett, itt, drie
    Confusion risks: First stroke of m, n, u (minim confusion)
    Variants: Sometimes without visible dot when written quickly

k - Elaborate form with loops, as expected in secretary hand.
    Example words: Take, blacke
    Confusion risks: Minimal - quite distinctive
    Variants: One main form; majuscule in Take has additional flourish

l - Single vertical stroke with a small loop at top (ascender).
    Example words: leaues, Coleworte, laye, blacke
    Confusion risks: h (without visible descender), tall i
    Variants: One main form

m - Three minim strokes connected at baseline.
    Example words: morter, morninge, them, mingled
    Confusion risks: in, ni, iu combinations (minim confusion)
    Variants: One main form

n - Two minim strokes connected.
    Example words: one, and, Consumptione, morninge
    Confusion risks: u (identical stroke count; distinguished by context
    and sometimes by approach stroke), m (partial)
    Variants: One main form

o - Round closed loop, generally smaller than modern o.
    Example words: one, of, Coleworte, morter
    Confusion risks: e (very similar in this hand), a (when open)
    Variants: One main form

p - Standard descender below baseline; bowl to the right.
    Example words: pyles, putt, pylesse
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: May have special p form for per-/pre-/pro-

q - Round bowl with descender curving to the right.
    Example words: quantitye
    Confusion risks: Minimal - relatively rare letter
    Variants: One main form

r - Multiple forms: (1) standard short r after other letters;
    (2) 2-shaped r used in some positions.
    Example words: Brothe, thereto, Strawberrie, morter
    Confusion risks: c, v (especially the 2-shaped r)
    Variants: Two forms as noted

s - Two forms: (1) long-s (looks like f without crossbar) used initially
    and medially; (2) round s used at word-end.
    Example words: savinge (long-s initial), leaues (round-s final),
    Strawberrie (long-s initial)
    Confusion risks: Long-s confused with f (check for crossbar)
    Variants: Two forms as noted

t - Short vertical stroke with crossbar. The crossbar often extends
    to the right to join the next letter.
    Example words: Take, thereto, the, putt
    Confusion risks: c (in some positions), l (if crossbar faint)
    Variants: One main form; sometimes ligatures with h (th)

u - Minim-based form, essentially same as n but distinguished by context.
    Example words: putt, untill, upon
    Confusion risks: n (identical minim strokes), m (partial)
    Variants: u/v used interchangeably per period convention:
    v at start of words, u medially (vpon, upon)

v - Angular form used at beginning of words where modern English uses u.
    Example words: violett, vpon
    Confusion risks: u (same letter in this period)
    Variants: See u above

w - Formed with two v-shapes connected, relatively wide letter.
    Example words: wrappe, with, well, water
    Confusion risks: Minimal - quite distinctive
    Variants: One main form

x - Crossed strokes forming an X shape.
    Example words: fflixe
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One main form

y - Descender curves below baseline, often with a dot above.
    Example words: pyles, you, yee, quantitye
    Confusion risks: g (descender loops similar), thorn (y-shaped = th)
    Variants: One main form; thorn (looks like y but means "the")

z - Rare; not clearly present on this page.
    Confusion risks: N/A

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
===================================

th - Very common; sometimes written with thorn (y-shaped character meaning "the").
     The t and h often join smoothly, with t's crossbar extending into h.

sh - s (long-s form) + h; the long-s curves into the h.

ff - Double-f represents capital F. Seen in: fflixe, fflanke

-es graph - Looped downstroke at end of words representing "-es".
            Seen in: leaues, pyles

-er graph - Hook-shaped upstroke representing "-er".
            Seen in: morter, togeathor, after

-ll - Double l appears in: handsfull, full, well, untill

-tt - Double t appears in: putt, itt, hott (common feature of this hand)

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (highest to lowest)
===================================================

1. e / o - Reversed loop of e vs. closed loop of o; very similar in this hand
2. n / u - Identical minim strokes; context-dependent
3. m / in / ni - Minim confusion with multiple strokes
4. Long-s / f - Distinguished only by presence of crossbar
5. c / r - Some forms of r can resemble c
6. h / b - When h's descender is not visible
7. y / g - Similar descender loops
8. l / h - Without clear descender
9. d / cl - Looped ascender of d could look like c+l
10. t / c - In some rapid formations
