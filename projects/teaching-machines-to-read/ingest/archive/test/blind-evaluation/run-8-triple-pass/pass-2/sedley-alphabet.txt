HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Sedley MS 534, Page 13
=================================================

This is a two-page opening (verso and recto). The hand is a relatively
rapid, somewhat informal secretary/italic hybrid. The writing is smaller
than the Henslow MS, with closer word spacing. There appear to be two
columns of text - the left page (verso) contains recipe XXVII and the
right page (recto) contains recipes XXVIII and XXIX, plus continuation
text at the top.

The hand leans slightly to the right. Letters are more compressed than
in the Henslow MS. The scribe uses a moderate number of abbreviations.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
======================

a - Open-topped form, relatively small and rounded. In some positions
    nearly indistinguishable from u.
    Example words: all, and, plaister, Salve, applyed
    Confusion risks: u, o (when hastily written)
    Variants: One main form

b - Rounded bowl with ascender. Generally well-formed.
    Example words: best, but, body, bay
    Confusion risks: h (when descender not visible), l
    Variants: One main form

c - Small curved stroke open to the right.
    Example words: colour, cerecloath, cordial
    Confusion risks: e, r (in some positions)
    Variants: One main form; majuscule C larger

d - Looped secretary form with ascender curving leftward.
    Example words: dust, dryed, drop, and
    Confusion risks: cl combination
    Variants: One main form

e - Reversed loop form, but in this hand often very small and nearly
    round. Hard to distinguish from o in many positions.
    Example words: the, incorporated, there, sweetnesse
    Confusion risks: o (highest risk in this hand), a
    Variants: One main form

f - Vertical stroke with crossbar. Used as long form.
    Example words: for, face, fire, fast
    Confusion risks: Long-s (without crossbar)
    Variants: ff = F (appears limited on this page)

g - Bowl with leftward-curving descender, sometimes open.
    Example words: good, great, ginger
    Confusion risks: y (similar descenders)
    Variants: One main form

h - Ascender with descender looping below baseline.
    Example words: half, houres, halfe, heales
    Confusion risks: b (if descender unclear)
    Variants: One main form

i - Simple minim with occasional dot.
    Example words: it, in, incorporate, is
    Confusion risks: First stroke of m, n, u (minim confusion)
    Variants: j used in Roman numerals (iij)

k - Elaborate looped form.
    Example words: Kidneyes
    Confusion risks: Minimal - distinctive
    Variants: One main form

l - Single vertical stroke with small top loop.
    Example words: lead, leaden, last, laid
    Confusion risks: h, tall i, b
    Variants: One main form

m - Three connected minims.
    Example words: made, much, mortar, mingle
    Confusion risks: in, ni combinations
    Variants: One main form

n - Two connected minims.
    Example words: new, not, nor, next
    Confusion risks: u (identical form in many positions)
    Variants: One main form

o - Round closed form, generally small.
    Example words: of, or, one, oyle
    Confusion risks: e (very high in this hand), a
    Variants: One main form

p - Descender with bowl. Standard form.
    Example words: pint, plaister, plaster, paines
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: May use special p for per-/pre-/pro-

q - Rare on this page. Bowl with rightward descender.
    Example words: quarter
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One main form

r - Multiple forms including standard and 2-shaped.
    Example words: red, remedye, roses, raines
    Confusion risks: c, v (for 2-shaped r)
    Variants: Two forms

s - Long-s (medial/initial) and round-s (terminal).
    Example words: sugar (long-s), sores (round-s), Spanish
    Confusion risks: f (for long-s)
    Variants: Two forms as standard

t - Short stroke with crossbar, often linking to next letter.
    Example words: take, the, that, together
    Confusion risks: c, l
    Variants: One main form

u - Minim form, identical to n graphically.
    Example words: upon, untill, ounces
    Confusion risks: n (highest risk)
    Variants: v at word-initial position

v - Angular form at word beginnings.
    Example words: very, vpon
    Confusion risks: u (same letter)
    Variants: See u

w - Double-v form, fairly wide.
    Example words: with, water, white, well
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One main form

x - Crossed strokes.
    Example words: next
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One main form

y - Descender with dot above.
    Example words: you, your, yet, eyes
    Confusion risks: g (similar descender), thorn
    Variants: One main form; thorn for "the"

z - Rare; not clearly present.

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
===================================

th - Very common; scribe sometimes uses thorn (y-shaped) for "the"
st - s and t frequently joined
-es graph - Looped terminal stroke for -es
-er graph - Hook upstroke for -er
ff - Capital F
&c - et cetera

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (highest to lowest)
===================================================

1. e / o - Extremely similar in this small, rapid hand
2. n / u - Identical minim strokes
3. m / in / ni - Minim confusion
4. Long-s / f - Crossbar distinction
5. c / r / t - Multiple overlapping forms in rapid writing
6. h / b - Ascender confusion
7. a / o / u - In hasty passages
8. y / g - Descender confusion
9. l / h - Ascender confusion
10. d / cl - Loop confusion
