CONFIDENCE NOTES
================

This manuscript is significantly more difficult to read than the Henslow MS.
The writing is dense, the letterforms are smaller, and many words are
challenging to decipher with certainty.

HIGH CONFIDENCE READINGS:
- "Lavander" (appears multiple times, clearly written)
- "hott & drye" (standard humoral description)
- "the third degree" (standard humoral classification)
- "diseases of the head"
- "humors"
- "distilled water of Lavander"
- "the Catalespe" (catalepsy - a medical condition)
- "migram" (migraine)
- "fallinge sicknes" (epilepsy)
- "aboundance of humors"
- "blood"
- "Apoplexie" (apoplexy)
- "admonition"
- "begynninge"

MEDIUM CONFIDENCE READINGS:
1. "[purifyinge?]" - Can see most letterforms but some are ambiguous
2. "[Spirituall?]" - Initial S is clear, rest partially visible
3. "[Vertude?]" - Subheading; could be "Vertude" or "Vertue"
4. "[Certayne?]" - Most letters visible but some ambiguous
5. "[Apotecariest?]" - Long word; most letterforms readable but some uncertain
6. "[compositions?]" - Long word; partially clear

LOW CONFIDENCE READINGS:
7. Several words in the middle section (lines 13-22) are very difficult.
   The scribe's hand becomes somewhat less controlled and the ink varies.
8. "[niffer?]" - Very uncertain; letterforms unclear
9. "[fronsied?]" - Very uncertain
10. "[mashinge?]" - Some letterforms visible but reading not confident
11. "[brisith?]" - Very uncertain; unusual word
12. "[fiuers?]" - Could be "diuers" or "fiuers"
13. Lines 16-22 contain numerous uncertain readings due to the density
    and small size of the writing. Many bracketed readings should be
    treated with significant caution.

NOTE: This page appears to be a medical text about lavender's properties
and proper usage, drawing on humoral theory (hot/dry/cold/wet).
The text warns against improper use of lavender preparations, particularly
in cases where there is an abundance of humors mixed with blood.
