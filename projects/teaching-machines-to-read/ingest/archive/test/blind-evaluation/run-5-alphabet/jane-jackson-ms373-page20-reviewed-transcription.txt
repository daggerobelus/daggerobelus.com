NOTES
================================================================

The first-pass agent was, like the Brumwich manuscript agent, overly
conservative in flagging. Many words that were flagged are in fact
clearly readable in the undamaged portions of the manuscript. The
hand is a reasonably clear italic/secretary hybrid and many common
words (for, the, and, of, Take, etc.) as well as recipe vocabulary
(Elder, Tabaco, mace, oile, dropsey, handfull, gallon, ale) are
readable without difficulty in areas not affected by water damage.

The remaining 12 [word??] flags represent genuinely ambiguous readings:
- Words where minim-based letters create multiple possible readings
  (e.g., "dose" vs other minim combinations)
- Words where the e/o confusion affects meaning
- Words at the edges of water-damaged areas where some letterforms
  are partially obscured
- The repeated "standing" readings where the -ing ending is
  compressed and could be read differently

The large number of [...] illegible markers remains unchanged. These
predominantly occur in the lower third of both pages where heavy
water/brown stain damage has rendered the text unrecoverable from
this image. The review agent's role is to re-examine flagged words
only, not to attempt new readings of illegible passages. The water
damage is genuine and these passages would require examination of
the physical manuscript under specialized lighting conditions.

Two areas of particular note:
1. The "for the dropsey" heading on the left page is clearly legible
   and provides an important recipe title for the collection.
2. The reference to "dried Tabaco" is interesting — tobacco was
   entering English medical recipes in this period and this is a
   noteworthy ingredient reference.
