HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Sedley MS 534, Page 13
================================================================

This page contains two columns of text with at least two different hands visible.
The left column contains recipe XXVII (a salve for sundry sores) in a smaller,
more compressed hand. The right column contains recipes XXVIII and XXIX in a
somewhat larger, clearer hand. Both hands show secretary/italic hybrid features.
The page number "20" appears in the upper left corner (original foliation).

================================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS (a–z)
================================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a', formed with a curved left stroke and
  short minim on right. In both hands it is relatively round.
  Example words: "Salue" (left col, line 1), "and" (throughout), "Take" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'o' when loosely formed
  Variants: Fairly consistent in both hands

b:
  Letterform: Ascender with a rounded bowl. The ascender is straight or
  slightly curved to the right.
  Example words: "best" (left col), "butter" (right col), "bay" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'l' if bowl is small
  Variants: Consistent

c:
  Letterform: Small open curve to the right, like modern 'c'.
  Example words: "colour" (left col), "cordial" (right col), "Claret" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'e' or 't'
  Variants: Capital C is larger with a more elaborate curve

d:
  Letterform: Secretary-style looped ascender curving back left, with round bowl.
  Example words: "Leaden" (left col), "dust" (left col), "and" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Loop can look like 'cl' to modern eyes
  Variants: Consistent secretary d throughout

e:
  Letterform: Small reversed loop or rounded form. In both hands, somewhat
  angular at times.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "sores" (left col), "eye" (right col)
  Confusion risks: HIGHEST confusion with 'o' — both are small round forms
  Variants: Sometimes more open, sometimes closed

f:
  Letterform: Tall with ascender and descender, crossbar visible through stem.
  Example words: "for" (throughout), "face" (left col), "finger" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with long-s (ſ) — look for crossbar
  Variants: Double-ff not prominent on this page

g:
  Letterform: Round bowl with descending loop curving left below baseline.
  Example words: "good" (right col), "Plaister" (left col, g-shape in context)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'y' in some formations
  Variants: Consistent

h:
  Letterform: Ascender on left, curved stroke descending from top to right.
  Sometimes the right stroke descends below baseline.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "houres" (left col), "halfe" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'b' if right stroke is short
  Variants: Consistent in both hands

i:
  Letterform: Single minim, sometimes dotted.
  Example words: "in" (throughout), "itt" (throughout), "it" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with first stroke of 'm', 'n', 'u'
  Variants: Consistent

j:
  Letterform: Not distinctly visible as separate from 'i' on this page.
  Example words: "iuice" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Used interchangeably with 'i'
  Variants: N/A

k:
  Letterform: Ascender with angular strokes at midpoint.
  Example words: "Take" (throughout), "make" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Distinctive form, unlikely to confuse
  Variants: Consistent

l:
  Letterform: Simple tall ascender, slightly curved.
  Example words: "Leaden" (left col), "lead" (left col), "last" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'b' or unlooped 'h'
  Variants: Consistent

m:
  Letterform: Three minim strokes connected at top.
  Example words: "make" (left col), "mingle" (right col), "Morter" (right col)
  Confusion risks: 'm' vs 'in' or 'ni' — count minims
  Variants: Consistent

n:
  Letterform: Two minim strokes with arch at top.
  Example words: "not" (left col), "and" (throughout), "new" (left col)
  Confusion risks: 'n' vs 'u' — arch at top (n) vs curve at bottom (u)
  Variants: Consistent

o:
  Letterform: Round closed loop.
  Example words: "of" (throughout), "oyle" (left col), "one" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'e' — highest risk pair
  Variants: Generally round and clear

p:
  Letterform: Descender below baseline with bowl at upper right.
  Example words: "Plaister" (left col), "pint" (right col), "putt" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with extended 'q'
  Variants: May have special form for "per-" prefix

q:
  Letterform: Round bowl with rightward-curving descender.
  Example words: "quarter" (right col)
  Confusion risks: Distinguished from 'p' by descender direction
  Variants: Rare

r:
  Letterform: Two forms — (1) short stroke with rightward shoulder,
  (2) 2-shaped form after rounded letters.
  Example words: "red" (left col), "reines" (right col), "for" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: 2-shaped r can look like 'z'; standard r can look like 'v'
  Variants: Both forms present

s:
  Letterform: Long-s (ſ) medially/initially — tall like 'f' without crossbar.
  Round-s at word end.
  Example words: long-s in "sores" (left col); round-s in "sores" terminal
  Confusion risks: Long-s confused with 'f' — check crossbar
  Variants: Two positional forms as expected

t:
  Letterform: Short upstroke with crossbar at or near top.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "take" (throughout), "to" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'c' if crossbar faint
  Variants: Consistent

u:
  Letterform: Two minims connected at bottom with curve.
  Example words: "upon" (left col), "untill" (left col), "put" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'n' — curve at bottom distinguishes
  Variants: Used medially; 'v' at word start

v:
  Letterform: Two strokes meeting at a point below.
  Example words: "vppon" (left col), "very" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'u'
  Variants: Used word-initially for modern 'u' sound

w:
  Letterform: Broad letter, double-v form.
  Example words: "water" (right col), "with" (right col), "well" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Distinctive
  Variants: Consistent

x:
  Letterform: Crossing diagonal strokes.
  Example words: Not clearly present on this page.
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

y:
  Letterform: Two strokes meeting above baseline with leftward-curving descender.
  Example words: "you" (left col), "your" (left col)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with thorn (þ)
  Variants: Consistent; thorn may appear in "ye" = "the"

z:
  Letterform: Not clearly present on this page.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Formed as two separate letters. Very common in "the", "that", "them", etc.
    Thorn (þ) appearing as 'y' may be present in some places.

sh: Long-s + h. Visible in words if present.

-es: Terminal -es graph (looped downstroke) may appear.

-er: The -er graph (hook upstroke) appears in several words.

-ll: Double-l common: "all", "well", "till".

-tt: Double-t common: "putt", "itt".

================================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Two-column layout with two distinct but similar hands.
2. Roman numerals (XXVII, XXVIII, XXIX) mark recipe numbers.
3. Recipe titles are in a slightly larger, more formal hand.
4. Left column hand is somewhat smaller and more compressed.
5. Right column hand is slightly larger and more spaced.
6. Both hands use secretary 'd' with looped ascender.
7. Ink is dark and consistent throughout.
8. Some words at line ends are compressed to fit within the column.

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. e / o — HIGHEST RISK
   Why: Both small round forms at x-height. Must look for horizontal stroke
   in 'e' vs. full closed circle of 'o'. Especially difficult in compressed text.

2. long-s (ſ) / f
   Why: Both tall with ascender/descender. Distinguished by crossbar presence
   in 'f'. In these hands the distinction is fairly clear but needs attention.

3. n / u
   Why: Both two-minim forms. 'n' has arch at top, 'u' has curve at bottom.
   In compressed passages, the direction of connection can be ambiguous.

4. m / in / ni
   Why: Three minims can be parsed as m or as i+n/n+i. Must count minims
   carefully and look for connecting patterns.

5. c / t
   Why: Both short. 'c' is an open curve; 't' has a crossbar. If crossbar
   is faint, confusion is possible.

6. r / v
   Why: The standard form of 'r' with a rightward shoulder can resemble 'v'
   in some formations.

7. h / b
   Why: Both have ascenders. Distinguish by whether the right-side stroke
   curves down (h) or forms a closed bowl (b).
