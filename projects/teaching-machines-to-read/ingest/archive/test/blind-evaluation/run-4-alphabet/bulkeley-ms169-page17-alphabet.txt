HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Bulkeley MS 169, Page 17 (page numbered 7)
Scribe: A relatively neat secretary hand with some italic influences. The writing is fairly regular and well-spaced, more formal than some recipe manuscripts.

============================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
============================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with a rounded bowl. Formed by a curved stroke that closes or nearly closes at the top. In this hand, generally well-formed and consistent.
  Example words: "Lavander" (line 1), "and" (throughout), "are" (various), "at" (various)
  Confusion risks: Can resemble 'o' when the closing stroke is complete. Also similar to 'u' when hastily written.
  Variants: Generally one consistent form; slightly larger in initial position.

b:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with a leftward approach stroke, then a bowl formed at the baseline. The ascender loops slightly.
  Example words: "be" (various), "but" (line 8), "blood" (mid-page), "before" (lower page)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'h' and 'l' due to tall ascenders. Distinguished by the closed bowl.
  Variants: Consistent.

c:
  Letterform: A curved open stroke, similar to modern 'c'. In this hand, the curve is clear and distinct.
  Example words: "Composition" (title), "cause" (various), "certain" (lower page), "cold" (mid-page)
  Confusion risks: Can occasionally resemble 't' or 'r' in rapid writing, but in this hand the distinction is usually clear.
  Variants: Uppercase 'C' is larger with a more sweeping initial stroke.

d:
  Letterform: Secretary-style 'd' with a leftward-looping ascender. The bowl sits at the baseline and the loop extends to the left.
  Example words: "distilled" (line 10), "degree" (line 2), "doth" (if present), "admonition" (lower page)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'cl' to modern readers. The loop is the key identifier.
  Variants: Consistent secretary form.

e:
  Letterform: Secretary 'e' — a reversed or backward-facing loop. In this hand, it is small and round, sometimes looking like a backwards 'c'.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "temperance" (title area), "be" (various), "her" (various)
  Confusion risks: High risk with 'o' — both are small round forms. The 'e' usually has a slight opening or rightward tail.
  Variants: Fairly consistent.

f:
  Letterform: A long descending stroke with a crossbar. The crossbar is usually visible cutting through the main stroke.
  Example words: "for" (various), "of" (throughout), "forehead" (mid-page), "flowers" (lower)
  Confusion risks: With long-s (ſ) — the crossbar distinguishes 'f'. In this hand, the crossbar is usually visible.
  Variants: 'ff' = capital F at start of words.

g:
  Letterform: A rounded bowl at the line with a descender that loops to the left below the baseline.
  Example words: "good" (if present), "great" (if present), "migram" (mid-page)
  Confusion risks: The bowl can resemble 'q' but the descender direction differs. Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

h:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with an open body — the right side curves down and sometimes dips slightly below the baseline.
  Example words: "hot" (line 2), "her" (various), "hath" (if present), "herbs" (lower page)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' (both have tall ascenders) and 'l'. Distinguished by the open body stroke on the right.
  Variants: Consistent.

i:
  Letterform: Short minim stroke, usually with a dot above. In this hand, the dot is usually present.
  Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout), "is" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u', 'n', 'm'. Careful minim-counting needed.
  Variants: Consistent; sometimes lacks dot.

j:
  Letterform: Extended 'i' with a descender. Rarely used as a distinct letter.
  Example words: Not clearly observed as distinct from 'i'.
  Confusion risks: With 'i'.
  Variants: N/A

k:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with angular body strokes. Distinctive in this hand.
  Example words: "kindes" (lower page)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

l:
  Letterform: Simple tall ascender stroke, sometimes with a slight loop at the top.
  Example words: "Lavander" (line 1), "light" (mid-page), "let" (if present), "like" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'h'. Distinguished by simplicity — no bowl, no open body.
  Variants: Sometimes looped, sometimes plain.

m:
  Letterform: Three minim strokes connected. Broader than 'n'.
  Example words: "migram" (mid-page), "made" (lower page), "much" (various), "most" (lower page)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'in', 'ni', 'iu', etc.
  Variants: Consistent.

n:
  Letterform: Two connected minim strokes. Narrower than 'm'.
  Example words: "not" (various), "nor" (various), "never" (mid-page)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'm', 'u', 'iu'.
  Variants: Consistent.

o:
  Letterform: Closed circular stroke. In this hand, generally well-formed and round.
  Example words: "of" (throughout), "or" (throughout), "one" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'e' and 'a'. Generally distinguishable by its symmetrical closed form.
  Variants: Consistent.

p:
  Letterform: Descender below the baseline with a bowl at the line level.
  Example words: "put" (if present), "part" (if present), "palsie" (lower page), "composition" (title)
  Confusion risks: With 'q' (rare in English). The "special p" indicates per-, pre-, pro-, par-.
  Variants: Standard and abbreviated forms.

q:
  Letterform: Not frequently observed. Would have a rightward-curving descender.
  Example words: "qualitie" (line 8)
  Confusion risks: With 'p'.
  Variants: N/A

r:
  Letterform: Two forms: (1) short upstroke with rightward hook — the most common form, and (2) 2-shaped form after certain letters.
  Example words: "red" (if present), "rather" (if present), "are" (various)
  Confusion risks: Short 'r' can look like 'v' or 'n'. The 2-shaped form can look like 'z'.
  Variants: Two forms as described.

s:
  Letterform: Two forms: (1) Long-s (ſ) — tall stroke, used initially and medially. (2) Round-s — used word-finally.
  Example words: "some" (long-s), "is" (round-s), "sicknes" (long-s + round-s), "spirits" (long-s)
  Confusion risks: Long-s with 'f' — the primary confusion risk.
  Variants: Positional variants (long-s and round-s).

t:
  Letterform: Short vertical stroke at minim height with a crossbar. The crossbar often extends rightward.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "that" (throughout), "to" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: With 'c' and 'r' when the crossbar is faint.
  Variants: Consistent.

u:
  Letterform: Minim stroke with a curved bottom. Used medially.
  Example words: "but" (various), "upon" (if present), "humors" (mid-page)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'n', 'i'. Also 'u'/'v' interchange (v initial, u medial).
  Variants: Consistent.

v:
  Letterform: Pointed stroke like modern 'v'. Used word-initially where modern English uses 'u'.
  Example words: "vertues" (mid-page section title), "very" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'u' (positional interchange). Can look like 'r' when hastily written.
  Variants: Consistent.

w:
  Letterform: Double-v form, wide. Well-formed in this hand.
  Example words: "water" (line 10), "with" (if present), "which" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct due to width.
  Variants: Consistent.

x:
  Letterform: Two crossing strokes. Rare.
  Example words: Not clearly observed.
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

y:
  Letterform: Short stroke above the line with a leftward-curving descender below the baseline.
  Example words: "ye" (= "the"), "you" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With thorn (þ), which also looks like 'y'. "ye" = "the."
  Variants: Consistent.

z:
  Letterform: Not observed on this page.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

============================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
============================================================

th: Very common. Often written with thorn (looking like 'y'), especially in "the" = "ye" and "that" = "yt".

sh: Long-s + h in combinations.

-tion/-sion: May be abbreviated with a tilde or mark over the vowel.

-er: The '-er' graph (hook-shaped upstroke) appears in words like "water," "after."

-es: Terminal '-es' sometimes as a graph.

ff: Double-f = capital F.

DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
============================================================

1. The hand is neat and relatively formal — this appears to be a carefully written herbal or medical text.
2. The writing is well-spaced with clear word boundaries.
3. The text is organized with centered headings ("The vertues").
4. The scribe uses "her" (referring to lavender as feminine) consistently.
5. Long lines fill most of the page width.
6. Some Latin or quasi-Latin terms appear (pharmaceutical vocabulary).
7. The ink is dark brown, fairly consistent.
8. Capitalization is somewhat irregular — some words are capitalized mid-sentence.
9. The page is numbered "7" at the top right corner.
10. The title section reads "Of Lavander" with information about its composition and temperament.

============================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (Highest to Lowest)
============================================================

1. LONG-S (ſ) vs. F
   Why similar: Both are tall strokes. The crossbar on 'f' is the distinction.
   Distinguishing feature: Look for the crossbar. In this hand, 'f' crossbars are moderately visible.

2. E vs. O
   Why similar: Both small round letters. In rapid writing, nearly indistinguishable.
   Distinguishing feature: 'e' has a slight rightward opening; 'o' is more closed and symmetrical.

3. MINIM CONFUSION: I / U / N / M
   Why similar: All formed from short vertical strokes. In sequences, difficult to parse.
   Distinguishing feature: Count minims carefully; look for dots over 'i'.

4. B vs. H vs. L
   Why similar: All have tall ascenders.
   Distinguishing feature: Bowl (b), open body (h), simple stroke (l).

5. C vs. T vs. R
   Why similar: All short strokes at minim height.
   Distinguishing feature: Crossbar (t), curve (c), hook (r).

6. SECRETARY D vs. CL
   Why similar: The leftward loop of 'd' can look like two letters.
   Distinguishing feature: Single connected stroke for 'd'.

7. Y vs. THORN
   Why similar: Identical appearance.
   Distinguishing feature: Context — "ye" before a noun = "the" (thorn).
