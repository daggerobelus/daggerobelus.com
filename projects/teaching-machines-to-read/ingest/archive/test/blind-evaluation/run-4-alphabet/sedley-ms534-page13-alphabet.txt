HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Sedley MS 534, Page 13 (opening showing pages 20 and facing page)
Scribe: A neat, well-practiced italic hand with some secretary influences. This is one of the more legible hands in the set. The writing is well-spaced with clear word separation.

============================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
============================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with a well-formed round bowl. The top closes neatly with a small stroke. Very consistent and legible.
  Example words: "Salve" (title), "and" (throughout), "a" (throughout), "all" (left page), "applied" (right page)
  Confusion risks: Minimal — well-formed in this hand. Slight risk with 'o' in compressed passages.
  Variants: One consistent form.

b:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with a clear round bowl at the baseline. The ascender is moderately tall.
  Example words: "best" (left page), "but" (left page), "bay" (right page), "boyle" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'h' and 'l' — but the bowl is clear in this hand.
  Variants: Consistent.

c:
  Letterform: Open curved stroke, similar to modern 'c'. Clear and distinct in this hand.
  Example words: "colour" (left page), "Comfry" (right page), "Cordial" (right page title), "Cinnamon" (right page)
  Confusion risks: Minimal in this hand. The curve is clear.
  Variants: Capital 'C' is larger and more ornate.

d:
  Letterform: In this hand, 'd' shows some italic influence — the ascender often curves to the right rather than the full leftward loop of secretary 'd'. Some instances show the secretary form.
  Example words: "dust" (left page), "dry" (left page), "drop" (left page), "drink" (right page)
  Confusion risks: Less risk of the cl/d confusion because the italic-influenced form is closer to modern 'd'. When the secretary form appears, the usual cl confusion applies.
  Variants: Both italic and secretary forms appear.

e:
  Letterform: In this hand, the 'e' is closer to the italic form than the full secretary reversed loop. It appears as a small curved stroke, sometimes almost like a modern 'e'.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "every" (if present), "else" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'o' — moderate risk. Also with 'i' in some compressed passages.
  Variants: Fairly consistent.

f:
  Letterform: A descending stroke with a crossbar. The crossbar is usually clear.
  Example words: "for" (throughout), "of" (throughout), "face" (left page), "finger" (right page)
  Confusion risks: With long-s (ſ). The crossbar distinguishes them.
  Variants: 'ff' at the start of words represents capital F.

g:
  Letterform: Rounded bowl with a descender that loops left below the baseline. The loop is often well-formed.
  Example words: "good" (various), "great" (if present), "Glister" (left page)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct due to the descender loop.
  Variants: Consistent.

h:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with an open body. The right stroke descends smoothly.
  Example words: "heals" (right page), "half" (right page), "head" (right page), "herbs" (right page)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'l'. Distinguished by the open body.
  Variants: Consistent.

i:
  Letterform: Short minim stroke, usually with a dot above.
  Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout), "is" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u', 'n', 'm'.
  Variants: Consistent.

j:
  Letterform: Not commonly observed as distinct from 'i'.
  Example words: "juice" written as "iuice" or similar.
  Confusion risks: With 'i'.
  Variants: N/A

k:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with angular body strokes.
  Example words: "Kidney" (right page), "keep" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

l:
  Letterform: Tall simple ascender, sometimes with a slight loop.
  Example words: "lead" (title), "laid" (left page), "Lemon" (right page), "last" (left page)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'h'.
  Variants: Consistent.

m:
  Letterform: Three connected minim strokes. Clear in this hand.
  Example words: "make" (left page), "Mortar" (right page), "mingle" (right page)
  Confusion risks: Standard minim confusion, but less problematic in this neat hand.
  Variants: Consistent.

n:
  Letterform: Two connected minim strokes.
  Example words: "not" (left page), "nor" (left page), "night" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Standard minim confusion.
  Variants: Consistent.

o:
  Letterform: Closed round stroke. Well-formed in this hand.
  Example words: "of" (throughout), "or" (throughout), "one" (right page), "on" (left page)
  Confusion risks: With 'e' and 'a' — moderate.
  Variants: Consistent.

p:
  Letterform: Descender below baseline with a bowl at the line. Clear.
  Example words: "put" (left page), "pot" (left page), "pint" (right page), "Plaster" (left page)
  Confusion risks: Standard. The 'special p' may appear for per-, pre-, etc.
  Variants: Standard and abbreviated.

q:
  Letterform: Rare. Bowl with rightward descender.
  Example words: "quarter" (right page)
  Confusion risks: With 'p'.
  Variants: N/A

r:
  Letterform: In this hand, 'r' is predominantly the short upstroke with rightward hook. Clear and consistent.
  Example words: "red" (left page), "remedy" (left page), "reddish" (left page)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'v' or 'n'.
  Variants: Mostly one form.

s:
  Letterform: Two forms: (1) Long-s (ſ) for initial and medial — tall stroke without crossbar. (2) Round-s for final — modern 's' form.
  Example words: "Spanish" (long-s, left page), "sores" (both forms), "salt" (long-s, right page)
  Confusion risks: Long-s with 'f'. In this hand, the distinction is usually clear.
  Variants: Positional variants.

t:
  Letterform: Short stroke with a crossbar. Clear in this hand.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (throughout), "this" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Minimal — the crossbar is usually clear.
  Variants: Consistent.

u:
  Letterform: Minim with curved bottom.
  Example words: "upon" (left page), "use" (if present), "until" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion; u/v interchange.
  Variants: Consistent.

v:
  Letterform: Pointed stroke. Used initially where modern English has 'u'.
  Example words: "very" (if present), "vpon" (= upon)
  Confusion risks: With 'u'.
  Variants: Consistent.

w:
  Letterform: Double-v, wide and clear.
  Example words: "water" (right page), "with" (throughout), "will" (left page)
  Confusion risks: Distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

x:
  Letterform: Crossing strokes. Rare.
  Example words: "XXVII" (Roman numeral, left page), "XXVIII" (right page)
  Confusion risks: N/A in Roman numerals.
  Variants: N/A

y:
  Letterform: Short stroke with leftward descender below baseline.
  Example words: "you" (if present), "your" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With thorn (but thorn does not appear to be heavily used in this manuscript).
  Variants: Consistent.

z:
  Letterform: Not observed.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

============================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
============================================================

th: Written as connected letters. "the" is very common. Thorn does not appear to be used here.
st: Common — "stir," "stop," "Stomach."
-er: Written out or with a slight hook.
-es: Written out in full.
ff: Double-f = capital F.

============================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
============================================================

1. A neat italic hand with some secretary influences — more italic than the other manuscripts in this set.
2. Recipes are numbered with Roman numerals (XXVII, XXVIII, XXIX).
3. Recipe titles are written in a slightly larger, more decorative script and sometimes underlined.
4. The scribe uses clear paragraph indentation.
5. Word separation is generally clear.
6. The ink is brown, consistent throughout.
7. The left page is numbered "20" at the top left.
8. The writing fills most of the page with moderate line spacing.
9. The hand is very regular — letter sizes and spacing are consistent.
10. Some text in the titles appears to be in a different style or emphasis (slightly larger or more ornate).

============================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (Highest to Lowest)
============================================================

1. LONG-S (ſ) vs. F
   Why similar: Both tall strokes. The crossbar on 'f' is the key.
   Distinguishing feature: In this hand, the crossbar is usually visible, making this less problematic.

2. E vs. O
   Why similar: Both small round letters.
   Distinguishing feature: 'e' is slightly more open; 'o' is closed.

3. MINIM CONFUSION: I / U / N / M
   Why similar: All minim-based.
   Distinguishing feature: This hand is neat enough that minims are generally distinguishable, but care is still needed.

4. B vs. H vs. L
   Why similar: All tall ascenders.
   Distinguishing feature: Bowl (b), open body (h), plain stroke (l).

5. R vs. V vs. N
   Why similar: The short 'r' hook can resemble 'v' or 'n' in some contexts.
   Distinguishing feature: 'r' curves right at the top; 'v' is pointed; 'n' has two minims.

6. ITALIC D vs. SECRETARY D
   Why similar: This scribe uses both forms — sometimes the italic upright 'd', sometimes the secretary looped 'd'.
   Distinguishing feature: Context and careful observation of whether the ascender loops left (secretary) or curves right (italic).
