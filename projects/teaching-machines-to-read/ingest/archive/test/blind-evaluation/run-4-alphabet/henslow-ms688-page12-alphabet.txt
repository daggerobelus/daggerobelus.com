HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Henslow MS 688, Page 12
Scribe: A very neat, formal secretary hand. This is an accomplished scribe — the letterforms are carefully formed, well-spaced, and highly consistent. The hand shows clear secretary features with decorative elements.

============================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
============================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with a well-formed rounded bowl. The top closes neatly. Very consistent and clear throughout.
  Example words: "Take" (multiple recipe openings), "and" (throughout), "a" (throughout), "Caunterberries" (line 1)
  Confusion risks: Minimal confusion in this hand — the 'a' is well-formed. Slight risk with 'o' in very rapid passages.
  Variants: One consistent form.

b:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with a clear bowl at the baseline. The ascender has a slight leftward approach. Well-formed.
  Example words: "Brothe" (line 2), "before" (line 3), "Borrage" (line 4), "blacke" (last recipe)
  Confusion risks: With 'h' and 'l' — but in this hand, the bowl is very clear, making 'b' fairly distinct.
  Variants: Consistent. The capital 'B' in "Brothe" and "Borrage" is larger and more decorative.

c:
  Letterform: A clear curved stroke open to the right. In this hand, very similar to modern 'c'.
  Example words: "Caunterberries" (line 1), "Consumptione" (line 2), "Colewort" (middle section)
  Confusion risks: Minimal — the curve is clear. Slight risk with 't' in very small writing.
  Variants: Capital 'C' is quite ornate with a large sweeping stroke.

d:
  Letterform: Secretary-style 'd' with the characteristic leftward-looping ascender. The loop is pronounced in this hand.
  Example words: "and" (throughout), "handefull" (line 3), "Defnes" (last section heading)
  Confusion risks: Could look like 'cl' to modern readers, but in this hand the stroke is clearly connected.
  Variants: Consistent secretary form.

e:
  Letterform: Secretary 'e' — a small reversed loop or backward-facing curve. In this hand, it is distinctly formed and generally legible.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (throughout), "leaues" (throughout), "one" (middle section)
  Confusion risks: With 'o' — moderate risk. The 'e' in this hand usually has a slight rightward opening.
  Variants: Consistent; slightly more prominent at the end of words.

f:
  Letterform: Long descending stroke with a clear crossbar. The crossbar is horizontal and cuts through the descender.
  Example words: "for" (section headings), "of" (throughout), "frie" (lower section), "flixe" (section heading)
  Confusion risks: With long-s (ſ). The crossbar is the key distinction. In this hand, the crossbar is generally clear.
  Variants: Capital 'F' in "For" is larger and more ornate. 'ff' = capital F.

g:
  Letterform: Rounded bowl with a descender that loops to the left below the baseline. The descender loop is often quite large and decorative.
  Example words: "grounde" (middle section), "growes" (last recipe), "grease" (last recipe)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct due to the large descender loop.
  Variants: Consistent.

h:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with an open body — the right stroke curves down and often extends slightly below the baseline.
  Example words: "handefull" (line 3), "horselounge" (lower section), "hott" (lower section)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'l'. The open body (no closed bowl) distinguishes 'h'.
  Variants: Consistent.

i:
  Letterform: Short minim stroke, often with a visible dot above. Well-formed in this hand.
  Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout), "is" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u', 'n', 'm' — standard risk.
  Variants: Consistent.

j:
  Letterform: Not observed as a distinct letter.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

k:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with two angular strokes at the body. The form is somewhat elaborate.
  Example words: "Take" (multiple), "blacke" (last recipe)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

l:
  Letterform: Tall simple ascender stroke, sometimes with a slight loop at the top. Clean and clear in this hand.
  Example words: "leaues" (throughout), "lorde" (line 1), "laye" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'h'. Distinguished by simplicity.
  Variants: Sometimes looped, sometimes plain.

m:
  Letterform: Three connected minim strokes. In this hand, the minims are well-separated and clear.
  Example words: "morninge" (middle section), "made" (lower section), "moole" (last recipe)
  Confusion risks: Standard minim confusion, though less of an issue in this neat hand.
  Variants: Consistent.

n:
  Letterform: Two connected minim strokes. Clear and well-formed.
  Example words: "and" (throughout), "new" (lower section), "not" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Standard minim confusion with 'm', 'u', 'i'.
  Variants: Consistent.

o:
  Letterform: Closed or nearly closed round stroke. In this hand, well-formed and symmetrical.
  Example words: "of" (throughout), "one" (middle section), "or" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: With 'e' — but in this hand, the distinction is usually clear.
  Variants: Consistent.

p:
  Letterform: Descender below the baseline with a bowl at the baseline level. Clear and well-formed.
  Example words: "pyles" (section heading), "putt" (throughout), "put" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: With 'q' (rare). The 'special p' for per-, pre-, pro- may appear.
  Variants: Standard 'p' and possibly abbreviated forms.

q:
  Letterform: Bowl with a rightward-curving descender. Rare.
  Example words: "quantitye" (line 5)
  Confusion risks: With 'p'.
  Variants: N/A

r:
  Letterform: Two forms: (1) short upstroke with a rightward hook, (2) 2-shaped form. The hook form is predominant.
  Example words: "rose" (if present), "rather" (if present), "wrappe" (middle section)
  Confusion risks: Short 'r' can resemble 'v' or 'n' in some contexts.
  Variants: Two forms; hook form dominant.

s:
  Letterform: Two forms: (1) Long-s (ſ) for initial and medial positions — a tall stroke similar to 'f' but without crossbar. (2) Round-s at word end — looks like modern 's'.
  Example words: "Strawberrie" (long-s, line 4), "leaues" (round-s, throughout), "sauinge" (long-s, line 3)
  Confusion risks: Long-s with 'f' — the primary risk.
  Variants: Positional variants as described.

t:
  Letterform: Short stroke at minim height with a clear crossbar. In this hand, the crossbar is usually visible.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (throughout), "them" (throughout), "together" (line 4)
  Confusion risks: Can resemble 'c' or 'r' when the crossbar is faint. In this hand, generally clear.
  Variants: Consistent.

u:
  Letterform: Minim stroke with a curved bottom. Used medially.
  Example words: "untill" (middle section), "upon" (lower section), "use" (middle section)
  Confusion risks: Standard minim confusion. Also 'u'/'v' interchange.
  Variants: Consistent.

v:
  Letterform: Pointed stroke, used word-initially where modern English has 'u'.
  Example words: "upon" written as "vpon" (if present), "violett" (line 3)
  Confusion risks: With 'u' (positional interchange).
  Variants: Consistent.

w:
  Letterform: Double-v form, wide and well-formed. Distinctive.
  Example words: "wrappe" (middle section), "with" (various), "woole" (last recipe)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

x:
  Letterform: Two crossing strokes. Appears in "flixe" (section heading).
  Example words: "flixe" (section heading)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: N/A

y:
  Letterform: Short stroke above the line with a leftward-curving descender below.
  Example words: "you" (lower section), "yow" (lower section), "pyles" (section heading)
  Confusion risks: With thorn (þ), though thorn does not appear to be used on this page.
  Variants: Consistent.

z:
  Letterform: Not observed on this page.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: N/A
  Variants: N/A

============================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
============================================================

th: Written as two connected letters — the 't' crossbar flows into the 'h' ascender. Very common. No thorn usage observed on this page.

sh: Long-s + h, written fluidly. Seen in "sheepe" (last recipe).

ck: Appears in "blacke" — the 'c' connects to the 'k' with a smooth stroke.

-es: Terminal '-es' written out in full (not as a graph). Seen in "leaues," "pyles," etc.

-inge: Common word ending in this manuscript. Seen in "morninge," "euenynge," "sauinge."

-tion/-sion: Written as "-tione" in "Consumptione" — note the terminal 'e'.

ff: Not observed as a capital F indicator on this page.

============================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
============================================================

1. This is an exceptionally neat, formal secretary hand — likely a professional scribe or someone of high literacy.
2. Recipe headings are centered and flanked by decorative dots/points (. . . . . For the pyles . . . . .).
3. The first word of each recipe ("Take") is written larger and with a decorative capital 'T'.
4. The page opens with "My lorde of Caunterberries" — indicating these recipes are attributed to or from the Lord of Canterbury.
5. Terminal 'e' is very common: "Consumptione," "Colewort*e*," "morninge," "euenynge."
6. Doubled consonants appear: "putt," "hott."
7. The spelling preserves many archaic features: "leaues" (leaves), "pyles" (piles/hemorrhoids), "flixe" (flux/dysentery).
8. The ink is dark and consistent.
9. The writing occupies the full width of the page with generous line spacing.
10. Decorative line fillers (dots) appear between recipes and in the headings.

============================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (Highest to Lowest)
============================================================

1. LONG-S (ſ) vs. F
   Why similar: Both tall strokes with descenders. The crossbar on 'f' is the only distinction.
   Distinguishing feature: In this hand, the crossbar on 'f' is usually clear, making this less problematic than in rougher hands, but still the highest-risk pair.

2. E vs. O
   Why similar: Both small round strokes at minim height.
   Distinguishing feature: The 'e' has a slight rightward tail; 'o' is symmetrically closed. In this neat hand, the distinction is usually clear.

3. MINIM CONFUSION: I / U / N / M
   Why similar: All formed from minim strokes.
   Distinguishing feature: This hand is neat enough that minims are generally distinct, but careful counting is still necessary in sequences like "min," "nim," "inn."

4. B vs. H vs. L
   Why similar: All have tall ascenders.
   Distinguishing feature: Bowl (b), open body (h), plain stroke (l). In this hand, these are usually distinguishable.

5. SECRETARY D vs. CL
   Why similar: The leftward loop of 'd' can appear as 'cl'.
   Distinguishing feature: Single connected stroke for 'd'. Clear in this hand.

6. C vs. T
   Why similar: Both short strokes; 't' has a crossbar, 'c' is curved.
   Distinguishing feature: Look for the crossbar on 't'.
