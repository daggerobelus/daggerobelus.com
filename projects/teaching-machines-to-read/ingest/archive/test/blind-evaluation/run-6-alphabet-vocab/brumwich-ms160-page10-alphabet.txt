HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Brumwich MS 160, Page 10
================================================================

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS:
This is a mixed hand from the mid-17th century, combining secretary and
italic features. The writing is relatively small and closely spaced.
Two pages are visible (verso and recto of an opening). The hand is
reasonably consistent but shows some variation in letter size and spacing,
particularly when the scribe is writing quickly. Ink is brown, with some
fading in places. The hand leans slightly to the right.

================================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
================================================================

a:
- Letterform: Single-compartment 'a', formed with a rounded bowl and a
  short upstroke on the right. Sometimes resembles a 'u' when hastily written.
- Example words: "water" (line 1), "and" (throughout), "Take" (multiple recipes)
- Confusion risks: Can resemble 'u' or 'o' in rapid writing
- Variants: Fairly consistent single form

b:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with a rounded bowl at the baseline. The
  ascender sometimes loops.
- Example words: "buglosse" (left page), "brothe" (left page), "burning" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'l' when bowl is poorly formed
- Variants: Single form

c:
- Letterform: Simple curved stroke, open on the right. Generally clear.
- Example words: "cowslip" (left page), "consumption" (left page), "cough" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Can resemble 'e' or 't' in secretary contexts
- Variants: Single form

d:
- Letterform: Secretary 'd' with a looped ascender that curves back to the
  left. The bowl is round.
- Example words: "and" (throughout), "distilled" (left page)
- Confusion risks: The looped ascender can make it look like 'e' + 'l'
- Variants: Single secretary form predominantly

e:
- Letterform: Small backward-facing loop, typical of secretary hand. Sometimes
  quite open.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "leaues" (left page), "Take" (multiple)
- Confusion risks: Easily confused with 'o' when loop is open; also with 'c'
- Variants: Generally consistent

f:
- Letterform: Long descender with a crossbar. Sits on the line with the
  stroke descending below.
- Example words: "for" (throughout), "french" (left page), "of" (throughout)
- Confusion risks: Confused with long-s (which lacks the full crossbar)
- Variants: Single form; 'ff' used for capital F (e.g., "ffrench")

g:
- Letterform: Round bowl with a descending loop that curves to the left.
- Example words: "gallon" (left page), "good" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Descender loop can be confused with 'y' in some cases
- Variants: Single form

h:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with a curved stroke at the baseline that
  sometimes descends below the line.
- Example words: "handfull" (left page), "harts" (left page)
- Confusion risks: When the descender loops, can look like 'b' or 'k'
- Variants: Single form

i:
- Letterform: Simple minim stroke, sometimes dotted, sometimes not.
- Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout)
- Confusion risks: Minims (i, n, m, u) are the primary confusion risk
- Variants: Used interchangeably with 'j' in some positions

j:
- Letterform: Extended descender form of 'i', used mainly in Roman numerals.
- Example words: "iij" (if present)
- Confusion risks: Same minim issues as 'i'
- Variants: See 'i'

k:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with angular strokes forming the diagonal arms.
- Example words: "Take" (multiple recipes)
- Confusion risks: Can look like 'h' when poorly formed
- Variants: Single form

l:
- Letterform: Simple tall ascender, straight or with a slight curve.
- Example words: "leaues" (left page), "liuer" (left page), "lettice" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'b' or tall 'h'
- Variants: Single form

m:
- Letterform: Three minim strokes. Can be broad.
- Example words: "morning" (right page), "make" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'in', 'ni', 'iu', etc.
- Variants: Single form

n:
- Letterform: Two minim strokes.
- Example words: "and" (throughout), "night" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u', part of 'm'
- Variants: Single form

o:
- Letterform: Round, fully closed loop.
- Example words: "of" (throughout), "one" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'e' or 'a' when hastily written
- Variants: Single form

p:
- Letterform: Descender with a round bowl at the baseline.
- Example words: "parsley" (left page), "pint" (left page), "plague" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Descender can be confused with 'q'; special 'p' forms
  for abbreviations (per-, pre-, pro-, par-)
- Variants: Standard and abbreviation forms

q:
- Letterform: Round bowl with descender curving to the right.
- Example words: "quart" (if present)
- Confusion risks: Similar to 'p' but tail goes opposite direction
- Variants: Single form

r:
- Letterform: Multiple forms visible. A short stroke, sometimes resembling
  a 'v' shape or a '2'-shaped mark.
- Example words: "rosemary" (left page), "water" (line 1)
- Confusion risks: Secretary 'r' can look like 'v' or 'c'
- Variants: Secretary form and italic form both appear

s:
- Letterform: Two forms: long-s (tall, like f without full crossbar) used
  at the start and middle of words; round-s used at word ends.
- Example words: "sage" (long-s at start), "leaues" (round-s at end)
- Confusion risks: Long-s easily confused with 'f'
- Variants: Long-s and round-s

t:
- Letterform: Short stroke with a crossbar. Generally clear.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (multiple), "them" (throughout)
- Confusion risks: Can look like 'c' or poorly-formed 'r'
- Variants: Single form

u/v:
- Letterform: 'v' used at the start of words, 'u' in the middle, as was
  conventional. Both formed as curved minim strokes.
- Example words: "vpon" (=upon), "leaues" (u in middle), "neuer" (u in middle)
- Confusion risks: 'u' confused with 'n' in minim sequences
- Variants: Positional variants (v initial, u medial)

w:
- Letterform: Double-v form, fairly broad.
- Example words: "water" (line 1), "with" (throughout)
- Confusion risks: Generally clear due to width
- Variants: Single form

x:
- Letterform: Two crossing diagonal strokes.
- Example words: Rare on this page
- Confusion risks: Minimal
- Variants: Single form

y:
- Letterform: Descending stroke with a dot or short stroke above. The
  descender curves to the left.
- Example words: "you" (if present), "they" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Can look like thorn (which also resembles 'y')
- Variants: Single form

z:
- Letterform: Not clearly visible on this page.
- Example words: None identified
- Confusion risks: N/A
- Variants: N/A

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Very common. The 't' and 'h' are often connected in a single pen
    movement. Sometimes thorn (resembling 'y') is used for 'th'.

sh: The long-s connects to the 'h', forming a tall combination.

ff: Double-f at the start of a word = capital F ("ffrench").

-es: Terminal '-es' sometimes appears as a graph (looped downstroke).

-er: The '-er' graph appears as a hook-shaped upstroke in some words.

================================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Mixed hand: combines secretary letter forms (especially 'e', 'd', 'r', 's')
   with italic forms, typical of mid-17th century English hands.
2. Relatively small, closely-spaced writing.
3. Recipe headings are sometimes slightly larger or set off from the body text.
4. Ink is brown, with some variation in darkness.
5. The scribe uses both long-s and round-s conventionally.
6. Abbreviation marks appear as tildes (~) over vowels for missing 'n' or 'm'.
7. Superscript letters appear in common abbreviations (w*th, y*r, etc.).

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. MINIMS (i/n/m/u) — HIGHEST RISK
   Why: In this hand, minim strokes are closely spaced and nearly identical.
   A sequence like 'nim' could be read as 'min', 'uin', 'niu', etc.
   Distinguishing features: Context and word-shape help; dots over 'i' are
   inconsistent.

2. e/o — HIGH RISK
   Why: The secretary 'e' (backward loop) is sometimes open enough to
   resemble 'o', and vice versa.
   Distinguishing features: 'e' tends to have a more angular left side;
   'o' is more fully round.

3. long-s/f — HIGH RISK
   Why: Both have tall ascenders. The crossbar on 'f' is sometimes very
   faint, making it hard to distinguish from long-s.
   Distinguishing features: Look for the crossbar; 'f' should have strokes
   on both sides of the descender.

4. c/t — MODERATE RISK
   Why: In this hand, the 'c' and 't' can look similar when the crossbar
   of 't' is faint or displaced.
   Distinguishing features: 't' should have a visible crossbar.

5. Secretary r/v — MODERATE RISK
   Why: The secretary 'r' (2-shaped) can resemble 'v'.
   Distinguishing features: 'r' tends to be shorter and more contained;
   'v' is wider at the top.

6. a/u — MODERATE RISK
   Why: When 'a' is written hastily, its bowl can open and resemble 'u'.
   Distinguishing features: 'a' has a closing stroke at the top right.

7. h/b — LOW-MODERATE RISK
   Why: Both have tall ascenders; 'h' can have a descending curve that
   fills in to look like a bowl.
   Distinguishing features: 'b' has a clear right-side bowl; 'h' opens
   to the right at the baseline.

8. d/cl — LOW RISK
   Why: Secretary 'd' with its looped ascender can look like 'c' + 'l'.
   Distinguishing features: 'd' is a single pen stroke; 'cl' requires a lift.

9. y/thorn (th) — LOW RISK
   Why: Thorn looks like 'y' and represents 'th'.
   Distinguishing features: Context determines reading; thorn typically
   appears before common words like 'the', 'that', 'them'.
