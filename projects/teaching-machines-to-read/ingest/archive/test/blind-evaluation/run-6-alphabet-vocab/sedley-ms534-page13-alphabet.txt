HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Sedley MS 534, Page 13
================================================================

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS:
This is a very clear, well-formed late 17th-century hand that is primarily
italic with some secretary features retained. The hand is confident and
regular, with good spacing. The scribe uses a relatively modern-looking
script that is highly legible. Ink is dark brown/black on paper. The page
is numbered "20" at the top left.

The opening shows two pages. The left page contains recipe XXVII ("A Salve
for sundry sores called the Leaden plaister") and the beginning of its
virtues list. The right page contains recipes XXVIII ("A [Plud?] Cordiall
for A wooman in Childbed") and XXIX ("Pills for the Gall" / remedy
"After a Tetter").

The numbering of recipes in Roman numerals suggests this is a systematic
compilation.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
================================================================

a:
- Letterform: Single-compartment italic 'a' with a clear rounded bowl
  and closing stroke. Very consistently formed.
- Example words: "Salve" (title), "and" (throughout), "all" (multiple),
  "applied" (right column)
- Confusion risks: Very low; clearly differentiated from 'u' and 'o'
- Variants: Consistent single form

b:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with rounded bowl at baseline. Clear and
  consistent.
- Example words: "bee" (if present), "backe" (left page), "bruises" (left),
  "bay" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Minimal
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital 'B' in some positions

c:
- Letterform: Simple open curve, italic style. Clear.
- Example words: "called" (title), "coales" (left page), "colour" (left),
  "Cordiall" (right page heading)
- Confusion risks: Can occasionally resemble 'e' but distinction is
  generally clear in this hand
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital 'C'

d:
- Letterform: Italic 'd' with a tall, mostly straight ascender (not the
  looped secretary form). This is a key distinguishing feature of this
  later hand.
- Example words: "sundry" (title), "dust" (left page), "darker" (left),
  "Drams" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Very low; the straight ascender makes this easy to
  identify
- Variants: Single italic form; decorative capital 'D'

e:
- Letterform: Simple rounded 'e', more italic than secretary. The
  backward loop of secretary 'e' is less pronounced.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "leaden" (title), "these" (multiple)
- Confusion risks: Low; more clearly differentiated from 'o' than in
  full secretary hands
- Variants: Consistent single form

f:
- Letterform: Long descender with clear crossbar. Well-formed.
- Example words: "for" (multiple), "fire" (left page), "fingers" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Can be confused with long-s, but this scribe's 'f'
  crossbar is prominent
- Variants: Single form

g:
- Letterform: Round bowl with descending loop curving left.
- Example words: "glory" (left page), "good" (right page), "Gall" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Low; bowl is distinctive
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital 'G'

h:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with curved stroke at baseline. Does not
  descend below the line much.
- Example words: "heals" (left page), "halfe" (right page), "hearbe" (right)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

i:
- Letterform: Simple minim stroke, consistently dotted in this hand.
- Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout), "is" (throughout)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion present but mitigated by dots
- Variants: Standard

j:
- Letterform: Extended 'i' form with descender; appears in "iuice"
  (right page).
- Example words: "iuice" (right page)
- Confusion risks: See 'i'
- Variants: See 'i'

k:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with angular arms. Clear.
- Example words: "know" (left page), "Kidnyes" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

l:
- Letterform: Tall ascender, straight. Clear.
- Example words: "Leaden" (title), "laied" (multiple), "lemon" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Low; distinct from 'b'
- Variants: Single form

m:
- Letterform: Three minim strokes, wider than 'n'.
- Example words: "marble" (left page), "Morter" (right page), "mingle" (right)
- Confusion risks: Moderate minim confusion
- Variants: Decorative capital 'M'

n:
- Letterform: Two minim strokes.
- Example words: "not" (multiple), "nor" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u'
- Variants: Single form

o:
- Letterform: Round, fully closed. Clear and consistent.
- Example words: "of" (throughout), "oyle" (left page), "one" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Low; clearly differentiated from 'e' in this hand
- Variants: Single form

p:
- Letterform: Descender with round bowl. Clear.
- Example words: "plaister" (title), "pott" (left page), "pint" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Decorative capital 'P'

q:
- Letterform: Round bowl with rightward descender.
- Example words: "quarter" (right page)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

r:
- Letterform: Primarily italic 'r' — a short downstroke with a rightward
  curl. More modern-looking than the secretary '2'-shaped 'r'.
- Example words: "remedy" (left page), "reines" (left page), "reddish" (left)
- Confusion risks: Low; more clearly differentiated from 'v' than
  secretary 'r'
- Variants: Primarily italic form

s:
- Letterform: Two forms: long-s (at word-start and middle) and round-s
  (at word-end). Both are clearly differentiated from 'f'.
- Example words: "sores" (long-s + round-s, title), "sundry" (long-s),
  "sticke" (long-s), "diseases" (round-s at end)
- Confusion risks: Low-moderate; long-s/f distinction is usually clear
- Variants: Long-s and round-s

t:
- Letterform: Short stroke with clear crossbar. Consistently formed.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (multiple), "together" (right)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Decorative capital 'T'

u/v:
- Letterform: 'v' used initially, 'u' medially. Both clear.
- Example words: "veines" (left page), "vpon" (left page), "iuice" (u, right)
- Confusion risks: Low; 'u' can be confused with 'n' in minims
- Variants: Positional variants

w:
- Letterform: Double-v, broad and clear. Sometimes decorative.
- Example words: "well" (left page), "wormes" (left page), "water" (right)
- Confusion risks: Very low
- Variants: Single form

x:
- Letterform: Two crossing diagonal strokes. Appears in Roman numerals.
- Example words: "XXVII", "XXVIII", "XXIX", "fluxe" (left page)
- Confusion risks: Minimal
- Variants: Single form

y:
- Letterform: Descending stroke with a dot. The descender curves slightly.
- Example words: "yeares" (left page), "yt" (=it/that, throughout)
- Confusion risks: Low; distinct from thorn in this hand
- Variants: Single form; also used for "y*t*" (=it/that)

z:
- Letterform: Not clearly present on main text of this page.
- Example words: "glazed" (left page) — the 'z' is visible
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Very common. 't' and 'h' are clearly formed. The scribe does not
    use thorn; 'th' is spelled out.

yt: The scribe uses "yt" as a pronoun/conjunction, likely representing
    "it" or "that." This is written as y + t, not as thorn + t.

-ing/-inge: Common word ending, clearly formed.

& : Ampersand used frequently ("Aches & bruises").

&c.: "et cetera" appears at the end of the tetter remedy.

================================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Primarily italic hand — later date than the other manuscripts. The
   secretary features are minimal (mainly long-s).
2. Very legible and consistent letterforms.
3. Recipe numbers in prominent Roman numerals (XXVII, XXVIII, XXIX).
4. Decorative capitals at the start of recipe headings and "Take."
5. Page numbered "20" at top left.
6. The scribe uses "yt" frequently (for "it" or "that").
7. Good line spacing; minimal interference from adjacent lines.
8. Both pages are well-preserved with no significant damage.
9. The right column of the left page continues the text of recipe XXVII
   (virtues of the leaden plaster).

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. MINIMS (i/n/m/u) — MODERATE RISK (highest for this hand)
   Why: Even in this clear hand, minim strokes remain the most similar
   letters. The scribe dots 'i' relatively consistently, which helps.
   Distinguishing features: Dots over 'i'; word shape; spacing.

2. long-s/f — LOW-MODERATE RISK
   Why: Both have tall strokes, but the scribe's crossbar on 'f' is
   generally clear.
   Distinguishing features: Crossbar on 'f'.

3. e/o — LOW RISK
   Why: This later hand has a clearer distinction between 'e' and 'o'
   than full secretary hands. 'e' is more modern-looking.
   Distinguishing features: 'e' has a horizontal bar; 'o' is round.

4. c/t — LOW RISK
   Why: The crossbar on 't' is consistent.
   Distinguishing features: Crossbar on 't'.

5. y / y*t* abbreviation — LOW RISK
   Why: "yt" (meaning "it" or "that") is written as two letters but
   could be confused with 'y' + 't' forming part of another word.
   Distinguishing features: Context; "yt" typically appears as a
   standalone word.

6. d (italic) — VERY LOW RISK
   Why: The italic 'd' with a straight ascender is very easy to
   identify, unlike the looped secretary 'd' in other manuscripts.
   Distinguishing features: Straight ascender.
