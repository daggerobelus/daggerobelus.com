A Salue for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pd of oyle oliue of the best Take of oaten
lead very well [battered?] with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pott well glased & when the oyntment is
[pared?] that the sope seemes [aporcosed?] & putt it vpon
a small fier of coales continnuinge yor said fier an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [bowe?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, yr make yr fier som
what bigger till the reddnes be turned into a graye
colour but yr must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle som what
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher yr it
neither cleue to yr fingers nor the trencher [fearfully?]
ennous then toppe [bloodes?] in that, the barbes of yor
[sheere?] dipped will last 20 yeares, p yr glory yr [layes?]
this plaister laide to the stomacke [prouokes?] appetite and
takes awaye the paines of the stomacke laide to the
[sides?] tis a remedye for the [stitche?] laid to the
reines of the backe it stopps the bloudy fluxe

A Tried Cordiall for A woman in Childbedd
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pinte of Cowslip water & ounces of
Syrupe of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of [Gascon?] wine & Nutmegs
[drawe?] mingle all these together and it is a soueran
good cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbedd

Pills for the [Gall?]
XXIX

Two [weeks?] [grains?] [...] and [...]
After A Tetter

Take one handfull of Sallanders & as much bay
salt as can take vp betweene yr finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp yr together in a
Mortar & straine the iuice out & smale the place & so
& put some of the hearbs vpon it, vse this 2 or 3
[dayes?] together dressinge it 3 or 4 times in a Daye
togeather &c
