A Salve for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle olive of the best Tu [...]
lead very small contriuted wth dust 12 ounces of
Spanish soper incorporate all these well togeather in
an earthen pott well glased & when the confection is
parted that the soper seemes aparentest & put it upon
a small fier of coales contineuynge yor said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [browne?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fier some
what eager, till the reddnss be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leave stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle somwhat
darker, then drop it on a woden trencher yt it
nether cleave to yt fingers nor the trencher fassettly
enoughe then dripp thearein in that, the barke of ye
thereon dippid will last 20 yeares, yt of cloth yt hee
this plaister laid to the stomacke [provokes?] appetite and
takes away the paine of the stomacke laid to the
belle, tis a remedye for the [chollike?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy fluxe

Openinge of the veines [that?] of the kidneys [workethe?]
of the back, it heales all swellinges, Aches & [bruises?], it
[clankes?] ffistulars, Impostumes, pushes & [syles?] them in
[baringe?] applyed to the [forheade?] it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [menicle?]

A Tried Cordiall for A woman in Childbedd
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowsly water & [a quarter?] of
Syropp of Cloue gilliflowers & a pennyworthe of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of [claret?] wine & [Mounns?]
[Driuid?] mingle all these togeather and it is a soueran
cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbedd
