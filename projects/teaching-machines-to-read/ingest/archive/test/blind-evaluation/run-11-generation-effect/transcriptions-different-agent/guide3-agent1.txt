A Salue for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle oliue of the best Take of oliu
lead very well [entered?] with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot well glased & when the sope is incor=
porated that the softe comes abouemost & putt it upon
a small face of coales continueinge the said fire an
houres & a halfe still stirringe it in a bolt of [bysse?]
fastned to a stick of wood, yt make yt fire some
what eager till the reddnes be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle somwhat
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & yf it
neither cleue to yt fingers nor the trenchar it selfe be
enuogh, then bypp it harder in that, the bardes of
thereon dippin will last 20 yeares, pt of oliue yt [...]
this plaister laid to the stomack [prouoketh?] appetite and
takes away the paines of the stomack laid to the
[nailes?], tis a remedy for the [childes?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy fluxe

Openinge of the veines [that?] of the Kidneys worketh
of the back, it heales all swellinges, Aches & [bruises?], it
[cvankes?] fistloes, Impostuimes, pushes & [steles?] them in
[taringe?] applyed to the [iunctures?] it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [menindes?]

A Third Cordiall for A [woman?] in Chilbedd
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & [ounces?] of
Syrrop of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of Claret wine & Mommes
[Draule?] mingle all these together and it is a soueran
good cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Chilbedd

After A Tetter

Take one Handefull of Sallanders & as much bay
salt as can take up betweene yt finger & Thumbe &
