A Salue for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle oliue of the best Take of red
lead very well [leuiated?] with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish soper incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pott well glased & when the oyntment is
purged that the softs seems [agreeable?] & putt it upon
a small face of coales contenuowsly the said fire an
houres & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [brown?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fire some
what eager till the reddnes be turned into a grey
colour but yt must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle somwhat
darker, then drop it on a woden trencher & if it
neither cleue to yt fingers nor the trencher [faultely?]
enoughe, then dippt [clowtes?] in that, the [charoles?] or
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, pt of [Glory?] & [taxes?]
this plaister laid to the stomache [provokes?] appetite and
takes away the paines of the stomacke laid to the
[sides?], tis a remedy for the [stitche?] laid to the
reines of the back it stopps the bloudy fluxe
