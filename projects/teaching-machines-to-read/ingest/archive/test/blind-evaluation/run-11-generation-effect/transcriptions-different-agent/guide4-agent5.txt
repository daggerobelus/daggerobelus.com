A Salve for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister

XXVII

Take one pt of oyle oliue of the best Tu [ounces?] of
lead very well centured with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish soper incorporate all those well togeather in
an earthen pot well glased & when the lead is incor-
porated that the softe comes appermost & put it upon
a small fier of coales continewynge the said fier an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [bow?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fier some
what [neger?] till the reddnes be turned into a grey
colour but yt must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyles somwhat
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher [&?] if it
neither cleue to yt fingers nor the trencher frasablye
enoughe then dipps clothes in that, the [charcklye?] or
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, pt of oloie yt [hase?]
this plaister laide to the stomacke [provoke?] appetite and
takes awaye the paine of the stomacke laide to the
[sides?] tis a remedye for the stitche laide to the
[reines?] of the backe it stops the bloudy fluxe
