A Salue for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle oliue of the best Take a pounde of
lead very well centred withe dust 12 ounces of
Spanish soper incorporate all these well togeather in
an earthen pott well glased & when the oyntment is
poured that the soper [comes?] abouesayd & putt it upon
a small fyer of coales continewinge the said fyer an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [lynnen?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fyre some
what niger, till the reddnes be turned into a graye
colour but yt must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle somewhat
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & yf it
neither cleue to yt fingers nor the trencher [fastlye?]
enoughe then dippp [bloudes?] in that, the [charcloth?] or
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, pt of clarr yt [hues?]
this plaister laid to the stomacke [cureth?] appetitte and
takes awaye the paines of the stomacke laid to the
[sides?], tis a remedye for the [Collicke?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloodye fluxe

A Treid Cordiall for A woman in Childbedd
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pinte of [Condye?] water 2 ounces of
Syropp of [Clovegillyflowers?] 2 a pennyworthe of Cinamon
water & a Spoonefull of [Cassia nine?] & [Momuns?]
[Dranis?] mingle all these togeather and it is a soveran
good cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbedd
