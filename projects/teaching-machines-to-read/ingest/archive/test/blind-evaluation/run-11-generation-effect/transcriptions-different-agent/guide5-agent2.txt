A Salve for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle olive of the best & of
lead very well [cerused?] with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well togeather in
an earthen pott well glased & when the oyntment is
poured that the sope seemes [apersment?] & putt it upon
a small fire of coales contenuynge the said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it w a bolt of boxe
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make it fire some
what [niger?] till the reddnes be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leave stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some what
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & if it
neither cleave to yt fingers nor the trencher feasiblye
enouge then dippe [clothes?] in that, the charclor of
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, p yt glory yt [hues?]
this plaister laid to the stomack [provokes?] appetite and
takes away the pains of the stomack laid to the
[loines?], tis a remedy for the [chollicke?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy fluxe

openings of the veines [that?] of the kidneys [workethe?]
of the backe, it heales all swellinges, Aches & [bruises?], it
[cureth?] [fistloes?], Impostuines, [pushes?] & [syles?] [therin?] in
[turnings?] applyed to the [fundamt?] it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [memitie?]

A Third [Cordiall?] for Mr [Nowill?] in Chilbedd
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of [Cowslip?] water & 2 ounces of
Syrop of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of [Gascon?] wine & [Manus?]
[Christi?] mingle all these togeather and it is a soverain
cordiall for any especialy for one that lyes
in Chilbedd
