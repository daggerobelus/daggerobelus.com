A Salve for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle olive of the best Take of oaten
lead very well clensed with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish soper incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot well glased & when the colour is
paryed that the sope seems aportinate & put it upon
a small fyre of coales continuoinge y[ou]r said fyre an
houre & a halfe still stirring it in a bolt of [boxe?]
fastned to a stick of wood, y[ou] make y[ou]r fyre som-
what [nyer?] till the reddnys be turned into a gray
colour but y[ou] must not leave stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some-
[what?] darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher if it
neither cleave to y[ou]r fingers nor the trencher forsaking
ennough then [happy?] liberales in that, the [basis?] of ye
theron dippid will last 20 yeares, p[u]t of alone y[ou] [lase?]
this plaister laid to the stomach [provokes?] appetites and
takes away the paine of the stomach laid to the
[sides?], tis a remedy for the [stitch?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy fluxe

A Tried Cordiall for A woman in Childbid
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water 2 ounces of
Syrup of Clovegillyflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of [Castor mine?] & [Metomes?]
[Drams?] mingle all these together and it is a souerain
good cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Chilbid

- "pt" likely abbreviates "pint"
