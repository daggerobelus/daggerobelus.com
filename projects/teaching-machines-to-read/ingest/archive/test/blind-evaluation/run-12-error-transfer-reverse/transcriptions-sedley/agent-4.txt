A Salve for sundry vses called
the Leaden plaister

XXVII

Take one pd of oyle oliue of the best & a
lead very well [sifted?] wth dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot well glazed & when the colour is in=
corporated that the sope seemes appointed & put it vpon
a small fire of coales continnewing yor said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirring it on a bole of boxe
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yr fire som
what bigger, till the reddesy be turned into a gray
colour but yor must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some=
darker, then drop it on a woodden trencher or a
poreles [cleane?] to yr fingers nor the trencher [fastlye?]
enough then typp thinker in that, the [charcoale?] or
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, p yr glory yr [have?]
this plaister laid to the stomach [provokes?] appetite and
takes away the paines of the stomacke laid to the
sides, t'is a remedy for the [Collicke?] laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy flux

[page 21]

[Openings?] of the reines that of the Kidney or [weaknes?]
of the back, it heales all swellings, Aches & [bruises?] as
[Crankes?] ffellons, Impostuines, pushes & [byles?] [cleane?] &
[brings?] applyed to the [forehead?] it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [memorie?]

A Tried Cordiall for Mr [Nowell?] in Childbed

XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & 2 ounces of
Syrrup of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefulls of Clarrett wine & [Nutmegs?]
[Dramm?] mingle all these together and it is a souer=
aigne cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbed

Pills for the [Gall?]

XXIX

ffor A Tetter

Take one handfull of Sallandine & as much bay
salt as can take vp between yr finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp yr together in a
Mortar & straine the iuice out & smalt the places and
yr put some of the hearbs vpon it, vse this 2 or 3
dayes together [dressing?] it 3 or 4 times in a Day
together &c
