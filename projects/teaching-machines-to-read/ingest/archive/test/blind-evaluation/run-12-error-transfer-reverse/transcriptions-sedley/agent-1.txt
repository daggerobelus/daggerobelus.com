A Salve for sundry vses called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pd of oyle oliue of the best & of
lead very well centred wth dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot well glazed & when the [colour?] is
[...] that the sope comes aboue melt & put it vpon
a small fire of coales continuinge yor said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bole of [...]=
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fire som=
what bigger till the reddesse be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leaue stirringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some=
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & if it
nether cleaue to yr fingers nor the trencher [...]=
enoughe, then drye it harder in that, the harder you
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, p & glory of [...]
this plaister laid to the stomach [provokes?] appetite and
takes away the paines of the stomach laid to the
reines of the back it is a remedy for the [collicke?] laid to the
reines of the back it stopps the bloudy flux

XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & as much
Syrup of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of Claret wine & Nutmeggs
[...] & mingle all these together and it is a souerau=
gne cordiall for any espetially for one that lyes
