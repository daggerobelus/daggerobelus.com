A Salve for sundry vses called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pd of oyle oliue of the best & a quarter of
lead very well centured wth dust & 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot well glased & when the oyle is incor=
porated that the sope comes aboue set it vpon
a small fire of coales continuinge the said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of boxe
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fire some
what eager, till the reddish be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leaue stirringes till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some what
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & if it
neither cleaue to yt fingers nor the trencher [fisifully?]
enoughe then [typpe?] [flourider?] in that, the [searclothes?] or
thereon dipped will last 10 yeares, pt of glory & [hast?]
this plaister laid to the stomack prouokes appetite and
takes away the paines of the stomack laid to the
belly, tis a remedy for the collicke laid to the
reines of the back it stops the bloudy flux

A Third Cordiall for Mr Nowill in Childbed
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & as much
Syrupp of Clouegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water & a Spoonefull of Claret wine & Nutmegs
[Draue?] mingle all these together and it is a souerai=
gne cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbed

Pills for the Gall
XXIX

Take one handfull of Sallandine & as much bay
salt as can take vp betweene yr finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp yt together in a
Mortar & straine the iuice out & make this pilles and
& put some of the hearbs vpon it, vse this 3 or 4
dayes together forbeare eatinge it 3 or 4 times in a Day
togeather &c
