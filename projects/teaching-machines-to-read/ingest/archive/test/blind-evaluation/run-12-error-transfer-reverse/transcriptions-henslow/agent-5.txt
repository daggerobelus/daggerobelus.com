My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .
Take like as before sauinge putt thereto a handfull of violett leaues
langdebeefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knitt
together of euerie one alike quantitye. . . . . . .

. . . . For the pyles . . . .
Take one handfull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the imbers untill they bee well rested
then take them, and putt awaye the Colewotte leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them together
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a tosser, and vse this
morninge and euenynge. . . . . . .

. . . . . For the flixe . . . . . .
Take horsedeunge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and putt it in a cloathe and laye itt to the fundaments as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and lye vppon vntill yow haue ease.

. . . . . . . For Deafnes . . . . .
Take blacke wolle whiche growes vppon the flanke
of a sheepe and [tarre?] and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare
