My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .
Take like as before sauinge put thereto a handfull of violett leaues
Langdebeefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit-
together of euerie one alike quantitye. . . . . . .

. . . . for the pyles . . . .
Take one handefull of mollet leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the [imbers?] untill they bee well rosted
then take them, and pull awaye the Colewort leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and put to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them together
then take it and laye it to the pyles withe a trusser, and vse this
morninge and evenynge. . . . . . .

. . . . for the flixe . . . . . .
Take horsedounge new made and frie it withe sweete butter
and put it in a Clothe and laye it to the fundament, as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and [so?] vppen untill you haue ease.

. . . . . . For Dimnes . . . . .
Take blacke wolle whiche growes vpon the flanke
of a sheepe and [tarre?], and mingell them together
and putt them into the eare
