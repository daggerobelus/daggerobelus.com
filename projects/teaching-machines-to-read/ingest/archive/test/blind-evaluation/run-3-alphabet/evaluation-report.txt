======================================
ASSESSMENT
======================================

THE ALPHABET-FIRST APPROACH IMPROVED RESULTS SIGNIFICANTLY

The alphabet-first method reduced CER by roughly half compared to the
previous two runs on this manuscript (from ~11-12% to ~6.1%). This is a
substantial improvement, though the result still falls short of the <5%
"usable" threshold and remains well above the 0.45% CER achieved on the
Sedley manuscript.

However, this manuscript (Henslow MS 688) is written in a notably more
difficult hand than the Sedley, so direct comparison of the CER numbers
across manuscripts is misleading. The relevant comparison is across the
three runs on THIS same manuscript page, where the alphabet method cut
the error rate nearly in half.

KEY ERROR PATTERNS

1. COMPLETE WORD MISREADINGS (16 of 58 edits, 28%)
   The three largest errors are complete word misreadings:
   - "langdebeeffe" -> "and buglosse" (9 edits)
   - "sitte" -> "laye it" (7 edits)
   - "mollett" -> "violett" (3 edits, likely influenced by
     "violett" appearing in the previous recipe)
   These three errors alone account for 19 edits -- a third of all
   errors. Eliminating them would drop CER to ~4.1%.

2. SYSTEMATIC DOUBLE-LETTER OMISSION (8 of 58 edits, 14%)
   The blind transcription consistently missed doubled consonants:
   itt->it (5x), putt->put (2x), ytt->yt (1x). This is a systematic
   bias, not random error. The AI appears to normalize doubled
   consonants toward single ones. If corrected, CER would improve
   further.

3. CAPITALIZATION INCONSISTENCY (5 of 58 edits, 9%)
   Mixed direction: some capitals were lowered (Lorde->lorde,
   Flixe->flixe), others were raised (strawberrie->Strawberrie,
   tarre->Tarre). No systematic pattern.

4. TERMINAL 'E' OMISSION (4 of 58 edits, 7%)
   The blind transcription dropped terminal 'e' from several words
   (handefull->handfull, Coleworte->Colewort, moreninge->morninge,
   fundamente->fundament). This may reflect modernization bias,
   since modern English has dropped many of these terminal 'e' forms.

5. U/V CONVENTION DISAGREEMENT (3 of 58 edits, 5%)
   The blind transcription used 'v' word-initially (vse, vppon,
   vntill) where the reference used 'u'. The blind reading actually
   follows the expected early modern convention more closely,
   suggesting the reference transcriber may have modernized here.
   These may not be true errors.

6. PUNCTUATION (4 of 58 edits, 7%)
   Three missing periods and one missing comma. Minor but systematic
   under-reporting of punctuation marks.

WHAT THE ALPHABET METHOD IMPROVED

Compared to ~11-12% CER in previous runs, the ~6% CER suggests the
alphabet helped with routine letterform identification. The basic
letter-by-letter reading is substantially better. The remaining errors
are concentrated in:
- A few catastrophic word misreadings (ambiguous or compressed letter
  sequences where context led the AI astray)
- Systematic patterns (double-letter omission, terminal 'e' omission)
  that could potentially be addressed through prompt refinement

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FURTHER IMPROVEMENT

1. Add explicit instruction to preserve doubled consonants (itt, putt,
   ytt) rather than normalizing to single consonants.
2. Add instruction to preserve terminal 'e' on words even when the
   modern form omits it.
3. For difficult passages, consider a two-pass approach: first a
   letter-by-letter decoding, then a contextual reading, comparing
   the two to flag discrepancies.
4. The complete word misreadings ("langdebeeffe", "sitte", "mollett")
   suggest the AI is sometimes substituting contextually plausible
   words rather than faithfully decoding letterforms. A stricter
   instruction to report what the letters show, even when the result
   is an unfamiliar word, might help.
