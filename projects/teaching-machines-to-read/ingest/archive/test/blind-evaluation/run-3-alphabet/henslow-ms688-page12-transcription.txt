My lorde of Caunteberries
. . . . Brothe for a Consumpshone . . . . .
Take like as before sauinge put thereto a handfull of violett leaues
and buglosse leaues Boorrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knitt
togeather of euerie one alike quantitye. . . . . .

. . . . For the pyles . . . .
Take one handfull of violett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and bake them in the [embers?] vntill they bee well rosted
then take them, and putt awaye the Colewort leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them togeather
then take it and laye it to the pyles withe a trosser, and vse this
morninge and euenynge. . . . . .

. . . . For the flixe . . . . .
Take horsedounge new made and frie it withe sweete butter
and put it in a clothe and laye it to the fundament as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and laye it vppon vntill you haue ease.

. . . . For Deafnes . . . . .
Take blacke wolle whiche growes vppon the flanke
of a sheepe and Tarre, and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare.

CONFIDENCE NOTES

1. "Caunteberries" (line 1, heading) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
   Letterforms: C-a-u-n-t-e-r-b-e-r-r-i-e-s. Clear capital C with the
   round curve, followed by 'a' with its angular top, 'u' (two minims
   connected at bottom), 'n' (two minims connected at top), 't' with its
   crossbar, secretary 'e', then 'r-b-e-r-r-i-e-s'. The double 'r' is
   visible. However, it is possible that one 'r' is absent, giving
   "Caunteberries" rather than "Caunterberries." The minims in the middle
   of the word are slightly compressed but I can trace each stroke.

2. "buglosse" (line 4) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
   Letterforms: The initial letter has a tall ascender with a closed bowl,
   consistent with 'b' in this hand. Followed by 'u-g-l-o-s-s-e'. The
   double long-s in the middle is distinguishable from 'ff' by the absence
   of a full crossbar. "Buglosse" (borage family plant, Anchusa) is a
   standard early modern ingredient name.

3. "[embers?]" (line 9, recipe 2) -- MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
   Flagged reading. The initial letterform is ambiguous: it could be a
   secretary 'e' (reversed loop) or an 'i' (simple minim). The remaining
   letters m-b-e-r-s are clear. If the initial letter is 'e', the word is
   "embers" (hot coals); if 'i', it could be "imbers" (a variant spelling
   of embers). The context of baking in hot coals supports either reading.
   My reading favors 'e' because the initial stroke appears to have the
   slight backward curve characteristic of secretary 'e' rather than the
   straight vertical of 'i', but the distinction is subtle.

4. "rosted" (line 9, recipe 2) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
   The second letter is a round, fully closed loop consistent with 'o' in
   this hand, not the smaller reversed loop of secretary 'e'. This gives
   "rosted" (roasted) rather than "rested." The remaining letterforms
   r-o-s-t-e-d are all clear.

5. "bake" (line 9, recipe 2) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
   The initial letter has a tall ascender with a rounded bowl at the
   baseline, consistent with 'b' rather than the minim-height secretary
   'r'. The remaining letters a-k-e are clear. "Bake" (to cook in embers)
   fits the letterforms.

6. "yt" (line 11, recipe 2) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
   Letterforms clearly show y-t. In early modern manuscripts "yt" commonly
   represents "it" (where 'y' may function as a thorn-derived spelling).
   Transcribed as written per semi-diplomatic convention. Not expanded
   because the scribe spells it out as two distinct letters rather than
   using a superscript abbreviation mark.

7. "trosser" (line 12, recipe 2) -- MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
   Letterforms: t-r-o-s-s-e-r. The third letter is round and closed,
   consistent with 'o'. The double long-s is visible. The final letter
   could be a round-s (giving "trosse") or a secretary 'r' (giving
   "trosser"). I read it as ending in 'r' based on the angular quality
   of the final stroke, but it could alternatively be "trosse." A trosser
   or truss is a medical compress or pad, consistent with the recipe
   instruction to apply a poultice.

8. "laye it vppon" (line 16, recipe 3) -- MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
   The text after "suffer ytt, and" is somewhat compressed. I can trace
   what appears to be l-a-y-e followed by what looks like 'i-t' then
   'v-p-p-o-n'. However, the letterforms in "laye it" are slightly less
   clear than surrounding text, possibly due to the pen moving quickly.
   Alternative reading: "lay it vppon" without the terminal 'e'. The 'v'
   at the start of "vppon" is clearly word-initial (= "upon" per u/v
   convention).

9. "Tarre" (line 18, recipe 4) -- MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
   The initial letterform is tall with what appears to be a horizontal
   crossbar, consistent with capital 'T' in this hand. Followed by
   a-r-r-e. "Tarre" (tar) is a plausible ingredient in an ear remedy
   combined with black wool. However, the initial letter could
   alternatively be read as 'b' (with an ascender and bowl), giving
   "barre" -- though this is a less likely reading both from the
   letterforms (I see a crossbar, not a bowl) and from context. Another
   possible reading is "burne" (burn), but the letterforms after the
   initial tall letter do not support 'u-r-n-e'.

10. "Deafnes" (line 16, recipe 4 heading) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Large decorated letters in the heading: D-e-a-f-n-e-s. The capital D
    is ornate. The 'f' is clearly distinguished from long-s by its visible
    crossbar. The word means "deafness" in early modern spelling.

11. "Consumpshone" (line 2) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Letterforms: C-o-n-s-u-m-p-s-h-o-n-e. The 'sh' combination in the
    middle is clearly the long-s (tall stroke without full crossbar)
    followed by 'h' (tall stroke with rightward arch). This is the
    scribe's phonetic spelling of "consumption."

12. "flixe" (line 13, recipe 3 heading) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Letterforms: f-l-i-x-e. The 'f' has its crossbar, 'l' is a simple
    tall ascender, 'i' is a minim, 'x' shows two crossing diagonal strokes,
    and terminal secretary 'e'. "Flixe" = flux (dysentery), a common
    ailment in early modern medical recipe books.

13. "horsedounge" (line 14, recipe 3) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Letterforms: h-o-r-s-e-d-o-u-n-g-e. The 'h' has its characteristic
    ascender and arch. The secretary 'd' has its leftward loop. The 'g'
    has its descender curving left. "Horsedounge" = horse dung, a common
    ingredient in early modern remedies.

14. "fundament" (line 15, recipe 3) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Letterforms: f-u-n-d-a-m-e-n-t. The 'f' with crossbar, followed by
    minim letters 'u-n', secretary 'd', 'a-m-e-n-t'. "Fundament" = anus,
    standard medical term in early modern English.

15. "morninge and euenynge" (line 13, recipe 2) -- HIGH CONFIDENCE
    Both words clearly formed. The terminal '-inge' / '-ynge' endings
    show the scribe's spelling conventions. The 'y' in "euenynge" is
    clearly a letter y (with descender) rather than thorn, given the
    context.
