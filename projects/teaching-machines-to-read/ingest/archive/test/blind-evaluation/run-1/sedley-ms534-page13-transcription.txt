A Salue for sundry sores called
  the Leaden plaister
            XXVII

Take one p*oun*d of oyle oliue of the best The [quantitie?] of
lead very well [cerused?] w*i*th dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all those well together in
an earthen pot well glased & when the [colour?] be tur=
porned that the sope seemes [apon*n*ment?] & put it vpon
a small face of coales continuinge the said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirring it on a bolt of [boxe?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, y*ou* make y*ou*r fire som*e*
what eager, till the redness be turned into a grey
colour but y*ou* must not leaue sturringe till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyster couler
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & if it
neither cleue to y*ou*r fingers nor the trencher [flatt*er*ly?]
enoughe, then dipp*e* cloutes in that, the plaist*er* or
theron dipped will last 20 yeares, p*ro*u*ided* y*ou* [keepe?]
this plaister laid to the stomach [prouok*es*?] appetite and
takes away the paine of the stomach laid to the
[side?], tis a remedy for the [Collicke?] laid to the
[raines?] of the back it stops the bloody fluxe

openings of the vaines bloat of the Kidneys [voydeth?]
of the back, it heales all swellings, Aches & [bruises?], it
[Coankes?] ffellons, Impostuemes, pushes & [steles?] the*m* in
[bring*es*?] applyed to the [Ioyntes?] it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [minice?]

A Plead Cordiall for A [woman?] in Childbed
            XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & [a?] [ounces?] of
Syrrop of Clouglillflowers & a pennyworth of Cinamon
water, & a Spoonefull of [Clarrett?] wine & [Musse?] [Amorus?]
Drams mingle all these together and it is a souerai*n*
and cordiall for any, especially for one that lyes
in Childbed

            Pills for the Gall
            XXIX

Take [two?] [mugge?] [of?] [...]  and [diuers?]
     After A Tetter

Take one [Handfull?] of Sallandine & as much bay
salt as can take vp betweene y*ou*r finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp y*ou*r together in a
Morter & straines the iuice out & wash the place w*i*th it

========================================
CONFIDENCE NOTES
========================================

General observations:
- This is the clearest of the five manuscripts. The hand is a neat,
  regular italic hand with good spacing and legible letterforms
- Recipe numbers (XXVII, XXVIII, XXIX) are clearly marked in
  Roman numerals
- The scribe uses consistent spelling and clear abbreviation marks

Specific uncertain readings:

Left page:
- "cerused" — the word describing what is done with the lead and dust;
  letterforms suggest this reading but it could be "cursed" or another
  word; "ceruse" (white lead) is a known early modern ingredient
- "apon*n*ment" — uncertain; this describes what the soap seems to do;
  could be a compound or technical term
- "boxe" — reading of the wood type for the bolt; could be "bone"
- "flatt*er*ly" — uncertain reading; describes how the plaister behaves
  on the trencher
- "Collicke" — almost certainly "colic" but the C and k are slightly
  ambiguous
- "voydeth" — probable reading; text continues from left to right page
- "Coankes" — uncertain; may be "Cankers" with unusual spelling
- "minice" — uncertain final word; could be "mince" or another term
- "steles" — uncertain; may be "steeles" or "stales"

Right page:
- "Clarrett" — likely "Claret" wine; the double-t is uncertain
- "Musse Amorus" — uncertain reading; may be "Musk" or "Muscadine"
  with additional descriptor
- Recipe XXIX heading and first line are partially obscured/unclear
- "Sallandine" — likely "Celandine" (a common medicinal herb) but
  transcribed as written with apparent S
- "goe" — uncertain; might be "for" or another preposition

The Sedley manuscript is notably more legible than the others in this
set, with clear recipe divisions and a consistent, practiced hand.
