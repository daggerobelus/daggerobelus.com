1. JANE JACKSON MS373 PAGE 20 — COMPLETE FAILURE
================================================================================

Reference text (3,345 characters) contains recipes for:
  - "For the pilling of the face" (turpentine and rose water)
  - "For freckles on the face" (white poppy distilled)
  - "For a Timpany" (flower delice in white wine)
  - "A Fume for the same" (white wine, running water, horse dung)
  - "For the same" (urine and sheep's tallow)
  - "Another of the same" (radish roots in white wine)
  - "A restoratiue for a Consumption" (egg yolks, cow milk, rosewater)
  - "For an euill sore in the head" (neats foot oil, dove dung)
  - "For the Migrim in the head" (houseleek and garden worms)
  - "For a stich or ach in the side" (camomile, wormwood, etc.)

Blind transcription contains NONE of these recipes. Instead it produced:
  - "ffor thornes and hay to be taken awaie"
  - "ffor healinge and drawinge of a wounde"
  - "ffor the stopping or for to clearsse"
  - "ffor Coughe"
  - "ffor the dropsie"
  - Various heavily flagged [...] passages

The blind transcription also claims this is a "two-page spread, left and
right pages" with severe water/mold damage. The reference text shows a
densely packed page of clearly legible recipes with no mention of damage.

Assessment: The agent either looked at the wrong page entirely, or the
manuscript image was so damaged/unclear that it hallucinated plausible
recipe text rather than transcribing what was actually there. Given that the
confidence notes describe "significant water/mold damage" while the reference
text is dense and complete, this appears to be a case where the agent
either received the wrong image or fabricated content when faced with a
difficult-to-read page. The vast majority of words are flagged as uncertain
[word?] or illegible [...], suggesting the agent could not actually read
the page.

CER: ~95.58% (effectively a complete miss)

2. BRUMWICH MS160 PAGE 10 — COMPLETE FAILURE
================================================================================

Reference text (2,406 characters) contains recipes for:
  - "A water of Exelent uirtue to helpe the weake or dimme sight of the eyes"
  - "A water to Stringthen the sight"
  - "A powder for the eyes"
  - "To Cuer a blind horse or Cristane"
  - "A water for sore eyes whose lides are all swelled"
  - (page 7) "A Medicine for sore eyes"
  - "For a pinne or pin & web in the Eye"
  - "A water for sore eyes commeded by Sir James Langham"
  - "A uery good receipt for sore eyes commended by Mrs Worsley"

The page is thematically unified: ALL recipes concern eye ailments.

Blind transcription produced entirely different content:
  - "A water of Endiue ciccoris" (endive/chicory — not eye-related)
  - "Dynary of Treach in powder" (treacle preparation)
  - Recipes about brimstone, antimony, and alchemical preparations
  - A veterinary recipe ("Tell any a blind horse")
  - Recipes for aches and joints ("Gonnes in the Ioyntes")
  - Attribution to "Mr Bostley"

The only partial overlap is "blind horse" appearing in both, but in
completely different recipe contexts. The blind transcription's content
bears no meaningful relationship to the reference.

Assessment: Same pattern as Jane Jackson — the agent produced plausible
early modern recipe text that does not correspond to the actual manuscript
page. Nearly every word in the blind transcription is flagged as uncertain
[word?], indicating the agent could not confidently read the manuscript
but generated text anyway rather than marking it illegible.

CER: ~96.09% (effectively a complete miss)

--- SYSTEMATIC PATTERNS ---

- "itt" → "it": The reference consistently doubles the 't' in "itt", "ytt",
  "putt". The blind transcription systematically drops the second 't'. This
  accounts for roughly 10-12 character errors.

- "ffor" vs "For": The blind transcription uses "ffor" (double-f for capital F)
  for recipe titles, while the reference uses "For". This is a transcription
  convention difference — the blind transcriber was following the instruction
  to use double-f, while the FromThePage reference normalizes to "For". This
  is arguably not an error but a convention difference.

  that are not in the reference. If these are present in the manuscript, the
  reference omitted them; if not, the blind transcription added them.


================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. COMPLETE FAILURES ON TWO MANUSCRIPTS (Jane Jackson, Brumwich)

   The blind transcription produced entirely fabricated content for 2 of the
   5 manuscripts. The transcribed recipes bear no relationship to the actual
   manuscript text. This is not a matter of poor accuracy — the agent
   generated plausible-sounding early modern recipe text that is completely
   invented. This is the most serious finding: when the agent could not
   confidently read a manuscript, it appears to have hallucinated content
   rather than marking text as illegible.

   Both failed transcriptions share characteristics:
   - The vast majority of words are flagged as uncertain [word?]
   - The confidence notes describe severe damage or difficulty
   - Despite acknowledging inability to read the text, the agent still
     produced full transcriptions rather than marking passages as illegible

   This is a critical reliability problem. A transcription system that
   generates plausible but entirely wrong text is worse than one that
   simply reports "I cannot read this" — because the errors are not
   obviously wrong to a reader unfamiliar with the manuscript.

2. POOR ACCURACY ON ALIGNED MANUSCRIPTS

   Even for the 3 manuscripts where the blind transcription identified the
   correct content, accuracy is poor:
   - Sedley: ~15.77% CER
   - Bulkeley: ~22.79% CER
   - Henslow: ~11.27% CER

   All three are far above the 5% usable threshold, and far worse than the
   Transkribus Egerton model's ~3% CER. The Sedley manuscript was described
   in the confidence notes as "the clearest of the five manuscripts" yet
   still had ~16% CER.

3. DOMINANT ERROR TYPE: LETTERFORM MISREADING

   The most common error category across all three aligned manuscripts is
   letterform misreading — the agent read entirely wrong words. These are
   not subtle single-character errors but wholesale misreadings:
   - "fire" → "face"
   - "Iron" → "boxe"
   - "bigger" → "eager"
   - "belly" → "side"
   - "tarre" → "Fayre"
   - "consistinge" → "purgessinges"
   - "spike" → "her"
   - "trusser" → "troffer"

   Many of these change the meaning of the recipe instructions significantly.

4. MODERNIZATION BIAS (MINOR)

   Modernization bias is present but is a relatively minor issue compared to
   the letterform misreadings:
   - "bloudy" → "bloody"
   - "espetially" → "especially" (twice in Bulkeley)
   - "togeather" → "together"
   - "yow" → "you"

   These are the types of silent corrections the project documentation
   identified as a core methodological risk, but they are dwarfed by the
   more severe misreading errors.

5. SYSTEMATIC OMISSION OF DOUBLE CONSONANTS

   The Henslow transcription shows a systematic pattern of dropping doubled
   consonants: "putt" → "put", "itt" → "it", "knitt" → "knit", "mollett"
   → "mollet". This is a consistent bias, not random error.

6. CONVENTION DIFFERENCES

   Some differences may reflect transcription convention choices rather than
   errors:
   - "ffor" vs "For" for recipe titles (double-f convention)
   - u/v interchange ("vsed"/"used", "vpon"/"upon")
   - Decorative dots included/excluded
   These are judgment calls that depend on the transcription standard being
   followed.

7. COMPARISON TO BENCHMARKS

   | System                        | CER      |
   |-------------------------------|----------|
   | Transkribus Egerton model     | ~3%      |
   | "Very good" threshold         | <1%      |
   | "Usable" threshold            | <5%      |
   | This blind test (best case)   | 11.27%   |
   | This blind test (3 aligned)   | 17.03%   |
   | This blind test (all 5)       | 67.70%   |

   The blind transcription pipeline does not meet the usable threshold on
   any of the 5 manuscripts tested. Even the best result (Henslow, 11.27%)
   is more than double the Transkribus Egerton baseline and more than double
   the usable threshold.

8. COMPARISON TO PREVIOUS GUIDED TEST RESULTS

   The project documentation reports a previous full manuscript test on
   Sedley MS534 achieving 0.45% CER overall. This blind test on a single
   Sedley page achieved ~15.77% CER — roughly 35x worse. The project
   documentation also reports a 5-manuscript baseline averaging ~98.7%
   word accuracy (~1.3% word error rate). This blind test is dramatically
   worse on every metric.

   The most likely explanation for the discrepancy is that the previous
   tests were "guided" (with the paleography guide loaded and possibly
   with iterative correction), while this is a blind test. The difference
   in performance suggests that the paleography guide and/or the guided
   workflow are essential to achieving usable accuracy — the vision model
   alone, without expert guidance, produces unreliable results.


================================================================================
METHODOLOGY NOTE
================================================================================

CER was computed by manually identifying all differences between the cleaned
blind transcription text and the reference text, counting character-level
edit operations (substitutions, insertions, deletions), and dividing by
the total number of characters in the reference text. For Jane Jackson and
Brumwich, where the texts are entirely different, CER was estimated based
on the near-total character divergence (Levenshtein distance approaches
the length of the longer string when texts are unrelated).

The blind transcription text was cleaned before comparison by:
- Removing all header lines (manuscript name, page number, conventions)
- Removing the "CONFIDENCE NOTES" section
- Removing page section headers ("LEFT PAGE", "RIGHT PAGE", etc.)
- Stripping confidence markers: [word?] → word, [...] → omitted
- Stripping italic expansion markers: *letters* → letters
- Removing editorial notes in brackets about page damage

The reference text was cleaned by:
- Removing the source/metadata header lines
- Removing page numbers ({6}, {7}, 20, 21)

u/v and i/j interchanges were generally NOT counted as errors, as these
are valid period spelling variants. However, they were noted where they
appear.
