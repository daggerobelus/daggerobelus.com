My lorde of Caunterberries
  . . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge put therto a handfull of violett leaues
langdebeefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit
togeather of euerie one alike quantitye . . . . . . . .

   . . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handefull of mollet leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the [Imbers?] vntill they bee well rosted
then take them, and put awaye the Colewort leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and put therto oyle of roses, and grinde them together
then take it and laye it to the pyles withe a troffer, and vse this
    moreinge and euenynge . . . . . . . .

   . . . . ffor the flixe . . . . . . .

Take horsedonge new made and frie it withe swete butter
and put it in a Clothe and laye it to the [fundament?] as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and lye vppen vntill you haue ease.

   . . . . . ffor Deafnes . . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche growes vpon the flanke
  of a sheepe and [Fayre?] and mingell them togeather
    and putt them into the eare

========================================
CONFIDENCE NOTES
========================================

General observations:
- This is the most legible manuscript in the set. The hand is large,
  clear, and carefully formed — likely a presentation or fair copy
  rather than a working manuscript
- The decorative dots (. . . .) are used as section dividers between
  recipes and are reproduced as written
- Recipe titles are centered and written in slightly larger script
- The double-f in "ffor" represents capital F, as expected
- The text begins mid-recipe ("Take like as before sauinge") indicating
  this page continues from a previous one

Specific readings:

- "Caunterberries" — My Lord of Canterbury's; this attributes the broth
  recipe to the Archbishop of Canterbury, a common practice of attributing
  recipes to notable persons
- "langdebeefe" — lang de boeuf / langue de boeuf (ox tongue plant,
  Anchusa/Bugloss); transcribed as written as one word
- "mollet" — likely "mallow" leaves but transcribed as written;
  could be "mullet" though mallow is more likely in this medicinal context
- "Imbers" — likely "embers" (hot coals); the initial letter could be
  read as E but appears to be I or an unusual E form
- "troffer" — uncertain; likely "trough" or a small dish/applicator;
  transcribed as it appears
- "flixe" — "flux" (diarrhea/dysentery); standard early modern spelling
- "fundament" — the anus; standard medical term of the period
- "Fayre" — uncertain; could be "fayre" (fair/clean) describing the
  quality of wool, or possibly another material to be mixed with
  the black wool
- "horsedonge" — horse dung; a known ingredient in early modern folk
  medicine

The recipe attributions and contents are consistent with early modern
domestic medicine: the Canterbury broth for consumption (tuberculosis/
wasting disease), pile/hemorrhoid treatment with mallow poultices,
flux treatment with horse dung (a common folk remedy), and a deafness
cure using sheep's wool placed in the ear.
