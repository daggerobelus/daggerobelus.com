SELF-TAUGHT GUIDE TO READING HENSLOW MS688 SECRETARY HAND
==========================================================
Based on study of 5 training pages (pp. 8, 10, 14, 16, 20)

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS
--------------------
This is a relatively neat, practiced secretary hand from a 1601 recipe
book. The scribe writes in a consistent style with clear recipe
structure: titles in slightly larger or more decorated script, followed
by "Take" as the opening word of each recipe. The hand is legible once
you learn the key letterforms, but contains many traps for a modern
reader.

LETTERFORM INVENTORY
--------------------

LOWERCASE LETTERS:

a - Open-topped, sometimes resembles a modern 'a' but can be more
    angular. The bowl is usually clear.

b - Has a tall ascender with a loop curving to the right, then a
    bowl below. Can resemble 't' if the crossbar of t is faint.

c - Small, rounded, open to the right. Can be confused with 't'
    in mid-word positions.

d - Two forms observed: (1) a looped ascender curving backward
    (secretary d), common at word starts; (2) a simpler form
    mid-word. The descender is relatively straight compared to 'g'.

e - Small, sometimes resembling a reversed '3' or a simple loop.
    Often connects fluidly to the next letter.

f - Tall letter with a crossbar. Distinguished from long-s by
    having a horizontal stroke crossing through the vertical shaft.
    At word beginnings, 'ff' = capital F (e.g., "ffasten", "Fistula"
    written as "ffistula"). This double-f convention also appears
    mid-word (e.g., "Deffnes", "stiffe").

g - Has a descender that curves back to the left, forming a loop.
    Compare to 'd' which has a straighter descender.

h - Tall letter with an ascender and a curved stroke descending
    to the right from the top, forming the body.

i - A single minim stroke, sometimes with a dot above but not
    always. Part of the minim family (u, n, m, i).

k - Has a tall ascender with angular strokes forming the body.
    Seen clearly in words like "Take" and "keepe."

l - Tall letter without a crossbar. Distinguished from long-s
    (which also lacks a crossbar) by context and slight
    differences in the top of the stroke.

long-s - Tall, looks like 'f' WITHOUT the crossbar. Used at the
    beginning and middle of words (e.g., "seethe", "sodde",
    "spoonefulls"). Round-s appears at word endings.

m - Three minims in sequence. When adjacent to other minim
    letters (n, u, i), counting minims carefully is essential.

n - Two minims. Adjacent to other minim letters, easily confused
    with 'u' (also two minims but shaped differently at top —
    'n' has pointed tops, 'u' has a rounded bottom).

o - Round, small. Can be confused with 'a' in some positions.

p - Has a descender going below the line, with a bowl to the
    right.

r - In this hand, 'r' can take a form that resembles a '2' or
    a small 'v'. Context and comparison with confirmed r's in
    words like "rosemarie" and "runninge" helps.

round-s - Used at the end of words. Looks like a modern 's'.

t - Short ascender with a crossbar. Can be confused with 'b'
    when the crossbar is faint. The crossbar distinguishes it
    from other letters.

u - Two minims with rounded bottom connection. Distinguished
    from 'n' by the curve at bottom vs. pointed tops.

v - Used in this scribe's hand inconsistently at word starts.
    The scribe often writes 'u' at word beginnings where modern
    convention might expect 'v' (e.g., "untill", "use", "uppon").
    Do NOT impose v-initial convention — transcribe what you see.

w - Wide letter formed from connected v-shapes or u-shapes.
    Clear in words like "watter", "withe", "white."

x - Rare, appears as crossed strokes.

y - Has a descender. Clear in words like "ytt", "yow", "youre."
    The tail curves below the line.

CAPITAL LETTERS:

A - Decorative, with sweeping strokes. Can look very different
    from modern 'A'. Seen in "A watter", "A plaister", etc.

B - Large, looped form. Seen in "Buorrage", "Boyle", "Butter."

C - Sweeping curved stroke. Seen in "Cinkfoud."

D - Looped, decorative. Seen in "Decayed", "Deffnes."

F - Written as 'ff' (double-f convention). Seen in "ffasten",
    "Firste", "Fistula" (as "ffistula"). Both at word start and
    sometimes appears as "ff" mid-word for emphasis.

G - Large, curved with descender loop. Seen in "Gargisine."

H - Large looped form. Not common as initial.

I - Can look radically different from lowercase 'i'. Check
    carefully — may resemble other capital letters.

L - Seen in "Laye", "Liver", "Longes."

M - Decorative, wide. Seen in "Make", "Marye."

Q - Decorative with a long tail. Seen in "Quinsie", "Queene."

R - Can look very different from modern R. Seen in "Redd."

T - Large, decorative. Seen at the start of "Take" (the most
    common word in recipes) — use this as a reliable anchor.

Y - Seen in "Yow muste."

NUMBERS AND SYMBOLS:

This scribe writes out most numbers as words ("three", "two",
"nine"). When numerals appear, be aware that early modern numerals
may not look like modern ones. The numeral '2' can resemble 'a'.

SCRIBE-SPECIFIC HABITS
-----------------------

1. DOUBLED FINAL CONSONANTS: This scribe consistently doubles
   final 't' in common words:
   - "putt" (not "put")
   - "ytt" / "itt" (not "yt" / "it")
   - "pott" (not "pot")
   - "butt" (not "but")
   - "att" (not "at")
   - "hott" (not "hot")
   Always preserve these doublings.

2. SPELLING PATTERNS:
   - "withe" for "with" (final -e common)
   - "seethe" / "boyle" / "straine" — final -e forms
   - "togeather" (not "together")
   - "moreninge" (not "morning")
   - "euerie" (not "every")
   - "yow" (not "you")
   - "youre" (not "your")
   - "muche" (not "much")
   - "thereto" / "thereof" — compound prepositions
   - "witheall" (not "withal")
   - "withowt" (not "without")
   - "verie" (not "very")
   - "owte" (not "out")

3. u/v CONVENTION: This scribe does NOT consistently follow the
   v-initial convention. Words like "untill", "use", "uppon" appear
   with initial 'u'. Transcribe as the scribe writes.

4. RECIPE STRUCTURE:
   - Titles often centered or set apart
   - Recipes begin with "Take" (sometimes with decorative capital T)
   - "probatum" appears at the end of some recipes (meaning "proven")
   - Ingredients listed with "of eache" for equal quantities
   - Measurements: "handefull", "spoonefull", "quarte", "pinte", "ounce"

5. WORD SPACING: Generally clear word spacing, though some compound
   terms may run together.

6. PUNCTUATION: Uses colons (:) and commas (,) occasionally.
   Periods appear at ends of some recipes. Preserve all punctuation.

COMMON TRAPS
-------------
1. Do not modernize "putt" to "put" or "ytt" to "yt"
2. Do not change "withe" to "with"
3. Do not impose v-initial convention (use -> vse)
4. Long-s is NOT f — check for the crossbar
5. Long-s is NOT l — compare to confirmed examples
6. "ff" at word start = capital F, but "ff" can also appear mid-word
7. Minim sequences require careful counting
8. Unfamiliar words (langdebeeffe, Cinkfoud, etc.) are real — do not
   replace them with familiar modern words
9. "probatum" is a Latin word meaning "proven" — it is not an error
10. Recipe ingredients may include substances that seem unusual to modern
    readers (horsedounge, tarre, coprose) — transcribe what you see
