SELF-TAUGHT GUIDE TO EARLY MODERN SECRETARY HAND
=================================================
Based on study of Henslow MS688 training pages (08, 10, 14, 16, 20)

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS
=====================
The Henslow scribe writes in a fairly clear secretary hand with moderate
cursiveness. Recipes follow a consistent structure: a centered title
(sometimes with decorative dots or lines), followed by "Take" as the
opening instruction word. The hand is legible but contains all the
standard secretary-hand challenges.

KEY LETTERFORM OBSERVATIONS FROM TRAINING
==========================================

1. DOUBLED CONSONANTS ARE HABITUAL
   The scribe consistently doubles final consonants:
   - "putt" (not "put") — appears repeatedly
   - "ytt" / "itt" (not "yt" / "it") — the scribe writes both forms
   - "pott" (not "pot")
   - "togeather" — the scribe's consistent spelling of "together"
   I must preserve these doublings exactly as written.

2. LONG-S VERSUS F
   The scribe uses long-s extensively in initial and medial positions.
   In the training pages:
   - "seethe," "sitte," "straine," "sweete," "spoonefulls" — all begin
     with long-s that could be misread as 'f'
   - 'f' appears in "for," "faire," "fyer," "fine," "freshe" — these have
     a crossbar on both sides of the vertical stroke
   - Long-s has a crossbar only on the left side, or no clear crossbar
   KEY: When in doubt between long-s and f, check the crossbar carefully.

3. SECRETARY D
   The scribe forms 'd' with the characteristic backward-looping ascender
   curving to the left over the bowl. Visible in:
   - "and," "good," "heade," "powder," "sodde"
   This form could be misread as 'e' or 'cl' by an untrained reader.

4. THE SCRIBE'S 'e'
   Small reversed-3 shape or simple loop. Appears clearly in "the,"
   "Take," "thee," "teethe." Sometimes very similar to 'o' in quick writing.

5. MINIM LETTERS (i, n, m, u)
   The scribe forms these with standard minims. Key observations:
   - 'n' in "runninge" — two minims with arch at top
   - 'u' in "putt" — two minims curved at bottom
   - 'm' in "muche" — three minims
   - In sequences like "runninge" the minims cluster together; counting
     is essential
   Dots on 'i' are sometimes displaced or absent.

6. SPELLING CONVENTIONS
   The scribe has consistent archaic spellings:
   - "moreninge" (not "morning")
   - "euerie" (every)
   - "togeather" (together)
   - "withe" (with — sometimes "withe," sometimes "with")
   - "verie" (very)
   - "youre" (your)
   - "yow" (you)
   - "muche" (much)
   - "bloude" (blood)
   - "seethe" (to boil/cook)
   - "annoynte" (anoint)
   - "whilest" / "untille" — archaic forms with final -e

7. u/v CONVENTION
   The scribe does NOT consistently use v at word beginnings. Evidence:
   - "untille" (not "vntille") — uses 'u' initially
   - "uppon" — the scribe may vary
   I should NOT impose a v-initial convention. Transcribe what I see.

8. ff = CAPITAL F
   At the beginning of words, "ff" represents a capital F:
   - "ffasten" = "Fasten" (but transcribe as "ffasten")
   This convention is standard for secretary hand. Also watch for
   medial ff as in "Deffnes" (deafness) — this is NOT a capital F
   but an actual double-f.

9. PUNCTUATION
   The scribe uses:
   - Commas (small marks, sometimes like modern commas)
   - Colons (in "colde: washe")
   - Periods (occasional)
   - No consistent end-of-recipe punctuation
   These must be preserved.

10. CAPITALIZATION
    The scribe capitalizes:
    - Recipe titles ("For the kinges euelle in the throte")
    - "Take" at recipe openings
    - Some proper nouns ("Queene Marye")
    - Some nouns within text ("Sunne," "Docke," "Archangell")
    Capitalization is not fully consistent but should be preserved as seen.

11. WORD SPACING
    Generally clear word boundaries, but some running together occurs.
    The scribe writes phrases like "too ffasten" (two words) and
    "langdebeeffe" (one compound word). Preserve manuscript spacing.

12. RECIPE VOCABULARY
    Common ingredients and terms from training:
    - rosemarie, rewe, sage, fennelle, parsely, Broome blossomes
    - Bowlarmene (bole armeniac — a medicite clay)
    - gommasticke (mastic resin)
    - copperis (copperas — iron sulfate)
    - corralle (coral)
    - Cinkfoud (cinquefoil)
    - Langdebeefe (bugloss)
    - methridatum (mithridate — an antidote compound)
    - polippes (polypody — a fern)
    - smallage (a celery-like plant)
    - perwinkell (periwinkle)
    - Archangell (archangel — a plant, dead-nettle family)
    - clary, neepp (neep — turnip)

TRANSCRIPTION STRATEGY
=======================
1. Start by identifying recipe titles — they are centered and sometimes
   decorated with dots or lines
2. Look for "Take" at recipe openings as anchor points
3. Read letter by letter, especially for unfamiliar words
4. Count minims carefully in every word containing i, n, m, u
5. Check long-s vs. f by examining the crossbar
6. Preserve all doubled consonants
7. Preserve archaic spellings — do not modernize
8. Mark genuinely uncertain readings with [?]
9. After completing a section, re-read for normalization errors
