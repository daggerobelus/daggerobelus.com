BRUMWICH MS160 PAGE 10 — LETTERFORM ALPHABET CHART
====================================================
Built from anchor words visible in the manuscript before transcription.

This scribe writes in a smaller, more cursive hand than the Henslow scribe.
The writing is denser and somewhat less formal — closer to a mixed
secretary/italic hand. Two pages are visible (a left and right page spread).

ANCHOR WORDS USED:
- "Take" — appears multiple times at recipe starts (both pages)
- "the" — very frequent
- "and" — very frequent
- "of" — very frequent
- "water" — appears multiple times
- "a" — frequent article
- "put" / "putt" — appears in instructions
- "good" — appears several times
- "for" — appears in recipe titles

=====================================================================
LOWERCASE LETTERS
=====================================================================

a — This scribe writes 'a' as a two-stroke letter with an open top,
    similar to Henslow but slightly more rounded. Confirmed in: "and,"
    "a," "Take," "water." NOTE: Per the error protocol, be careful
    not to confuse with the numeral 2.

b — Ascender with a loop and rounded bowl. Confirmed in: "bee," "boyle,"
    "blood." The ascender is relatively short in this hand.

c — Small curved stroke, open to the right. Confirmed in words containing
    'c'. Can resemble 't' — check carefully per protocol warnings.

d — Secretary-style d with backward-looping ascender (loop goes to the
    RIGHT). This scribe's d-loop is tighter than Henslow's. Confirmed
    in: "and," "good."

e — Small, often resembles a reversed '3' or a small curved mark.
    This scribe's e is quite small. Confirmed in: "the," "Take," "water."

f — Has a crossbar distinguishing it from long-s. This scribe's f is
    relatively clear. Confirmed in: "for," "of." The ff = capital F
    convention may apply.

g — Descender curves back under the line. This scribe's g is compact.
    Confirmed in: "good," words ending in "-inge."

h — Ascender with a curved stroke going down to the right. Confirmed
    in: "the," "halfe."

i — Single minim. This scribe sometimes places a dot/tick above.
    Part of the minim group (i, u, n, m) — count carefully.

k — Ascender with diagonal strokes. Confirmed in: "Take," "make."

l — Tall stroke. In this hand, l is slightly shorter relative to
    ascenders than in Henslow. DANGER: confusion with long-s.
    Check for crossbar (= f) or curvature (= long-s).

m — Three minims. Confirmed in: "make," common words. Count minims.

n — Two minims. Confirmed in: "and," "in." DANGER: confusion with u.

o — Small round letter. This scribe's o is fairly round and consistent.
    Confirmed in: "of," "good," "or."

p — Descender with bowl at line level. Confirmed in: "put," "putt."

q — Rare. Descender with a tail. Look for it in "quart," "quantity."

r — This scribe uses a form that can look like a '2' shape or small 'v'
    after certain letters (especially after 'o'). DANGER: confusion
    with 'v'. Confirmed in: "for," "water." Compare carefully.

s — TWO FORMS in this hand:
    (1) Long-s: tall, no crossbar (vs. f which has crossbar). Used at
        word starts and in the middle of words.
    (2) Round-s: at word endings, resembles modern 's'.
    DANGER: long-s / l / f confusion. Always check for crossbar.

t — Short letter with crossbar. This scribe's crossbar is sometimes
    light. DANGER: confusion with 'b' when crossbar is faint.
    Confirmed in: "Take," "the," "to."

u — Two minims with connector at bottom. DANGER: confusion with 'n.'
    Need to check this scribe's u/v convention — does this scribe use
    'v' initially and 'u' medially, or 'u' everywhere like Henslow?

v — More angular than u, strokes meet at a point. Confirmed in: "very"
    (if present). Check this scribe's specific v form.

w — Wide letter, like two v's. Confirmed in: "water," "with."

x — Rare. Cross shape.

y — Descender curving left/down. Confirmed in: "you," possibly "ytt."
    Check whether this scribe uses "yow" or "you."

=====================================================================
CAPITAL LETTERS
=====================================================================

A — Large ornate form. Look for it at recipe beginnings.

F — Likely written as 'ff' (double-f convention) as in Henslow.
    Confirmed in: "ffor" = "For" in recipe titles.

T — Large form with prominent crossbar. Confirmed in: "Take," "To."
    This is the most frequent capital in recipe books.

  Per protocol: watch for '2' that looks like 'a' and '1' that looks

=====================================================================
KEY CONFUSIONS TO WATCH IN THIS HAND
=====================================================================
1. Long-s vs. l vs. f — check for crossbar
2. u vs. n — count minims, compare to confirmed examples
3. r vs. v — especially after 'o'
4. d vs. g — check descender direction
5. t vs. b — check for crossbar
6. Numerals vs. letters (2/a, 1/&)
7. Capital letters — may look very different from modern forms
