SELF-TAUGHT GUIDE TO READING SECRETARY HAND
Based on study of Henslow MS688 training pages (08, 10, 14, 16, 20)
=====================================================================

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS
This is a fairly clear, practiced secretary hand from around 1601. The scribe
writes recipe titles in a larger, more decorative script and body text in a
smaller, more compact hand. The writing is generally legible with consistent
letterforms throughout.

ALPHABET — LOWERCASE LETTERS

a — Typically has a rounded bowl with a short curved stroke. Can look like
    a modern lowercase a. Sometimes has an angular top.

b — Has a tall ascender looping to the left, then a rounded bowl at the
    baseline. The ascender is distinctive and helps distinguish from t.

c — Small, rounded, open on the right. Similar to modern c. Can be confused
    with t when written quickly.

d — Has an ascender that loops back to the left (secretary d). The loop goes
    LEFT, unlike modern d where the ascender goes right/straight up. This is
    one of the most distinctive secretary hand letters. Example: "dothe,"
    "drinke," "doges."

e — Small rounded letter, often with a visible horizontal stroke. Secretary e
    can sometimes look like a small o with a tick. Confirmed in "eache," "euelle."

f — Has a long descender below the baseline AND a crossbar. Distinguished from
    long-s by its crossbar. Confirmed in "for," "fower," "fine," "fier."

g — Has a descender that curves back to the right in a loop. Compare to d which
    has a straighter or leftward descender. Confirmed in "gargolle," "greace."

h — Has a tall ascender and a curved stroke descending to the right at the
    baseline. The right stroke can extend below baseline. Confirmed in "halfe,"
    "his," "houre."

i — A single minim (short vertical stroke), sometimes with a dot. Part of the
    minim confusion cluster (i, u, n, m). Confirmed in "in," "itt."

k — Has an ascender with two angular strokes meeting at the right. Confirmed
    in "kinges," "keepe," "killed."

l — Tall vertical stroke, similar height to long-s and f but WITHOUT a crossbar
    and usually straighter than long-s. Confirmed in "longes," "liuer," "lett."

m — Three minims joined at top. Wider than n. Confirmed in "mouthe," "make."

n — Two minims joined at top. Confirmed in "narrowe," "nine."

o — Rounded, can be very small. Sometimes barely distinguishable from the
    connections between other letters. Confirmed in "one," "ounce," "ouer."

p — Has a descender going straight down and a rounded bowl. Confirmed in
    "putt," "potte," "powder."

q — Has a descender curving to the right. Confirmed in "quarte," "Quinsie."

r — Can take two forms: (1) a short stroke with a shoulder (like modern r),
    (2) a v-shaped form (secretary r) that resembles v. When reading, be
    aware that what looks like v might be r. Confirmed in "runninge," "rootes."

s — TWO FORMS:
    (1) Long-s: Tall letter used at word beginnings and middles. Looks like
        f but WITHOUT the crossbar. Can be confused with l. Examples: "sage,"
        "seethe," "straine," "spoonefulls."
    (2) Round-s: Used at word endings. Looks like modern s. Examples: "eyes,"
        "rootes," "ounces."

t — Short ascender with a crossbar. Can be confused with b when the crossbar
    is faint or when written quickly. Confirmed in "the," "them," "then."

u — A minim letter: two minims joined at bottom, creating a curved base.
    Part of the minim confusion cluster. The scribe sometimes uses u where
    modern English uses v (e.g., "euerie" = every, "euelle" = evil).
    Confirmed in "untill," "use," "uppon."

v — Used where modern English might use u at word beginnings in some hands,
    BUT this Henslow scribe does NOT consistently follow the v-initial
    convention. The scribe writes "untill" not "vntill," "use" not "vse."
    Confirmed in "verie," "vnnilla."

w — Wide letter formed of two v-shapes joined. Distinctive and usually clear.
    Confirmed in "watter," "water," "withe," "wine," "when."

x — Angular crossing strokes. Confirmed in "waxeth."

y — Has a descender that curves left below baseline. Sometimes has a dot
    above. Confirmed in "yow," "ytt," "youre."

z — Rare. Angular form.

ALPHABET — UPPERCASE / CAPITAL LETTERS

A — Can look like a larger, more ornate version of lowercase a, or have
    an angular top with decorative strokes. Confirmed in "A watter," "A greene."

B — Large rounded letter with ornate strokes. Confirmed in "Buorrage,"
    "Boyle," "Butt."

C — Large rounded form, sometimes with a decorative flourish. Confirmed
    in "Cinkfoud," "Cowslip."

D — Large with a distinctive leftward loop. Confirmed in "Decayed," "Deffnes."

F — Written as ff (double-f) at word beginnings = capital F. This is a KEY
    convention of secretary hand. "ffasten" = "Fasten." The ff can also
    appear mid-word (e.g., "Deffnes"). Confirmed in "ffasten," "fower."

H — Decorative, larger version of h. Confirmed in "Halle."

L — Tall with a decorative loop or flourish. Confirmed in "Liver," "Longes."

M — Large with multiple strokes. Confirmed in "Make," "Marye."

Q — Ornate with a large descending tail. Confirmed in "Quinsie," "Queene."

R — Can look very different from modern R. Sometimes resembles a larger
    version of secretary r. Confirmed in "Redd."

S — Large ornate form of round-s. Confirmed in "Sunne," "Sunn."

T — Large with a prominent crossbar. Confirmed in "Take," "To," "The," "This."

Y — Large with a prominent descender. Confirmed in "Yow."

DOUBLED CONSONANTS
This scribe CONSISTENTLY doubles final consonants:
- tt: putt, ytt, itt, att, butt, lett, cutt
- ll: wille, untill, vntill, halfe (note: halfe not hallfe)
- ff: stiffe, softe, selfe (mid-word ff in Deffnes)
- ss: blossomes (medial)
- dd: sodde, Redd, bloude (not doubled here — "bloude")

KEY SPELLING PATTERNS
- -inge endings: runninge, moreninge, stoppinge, swellinge
- -ethe endings: achethe, waxeth
- th for "th" sound: withe (with), togeather (together), throte (throat)
- ee for long vowels: teethe, seethe, sweete, keepe, breade
- ow for "ou" sound: owte (out), powre (pour), fowre (four)
- ytt/itt for "it": the scribe uses both forms

ABBREVIATION MARKS
This scribe rarely uses abbreviations. Words are generally written out in full.
Do NOT transcribe abbreviated forms unless they are clearly present.

PUNCTUATION
- Uses colons (:) and periods (.)
- Sometimes uses commas
- Decorative dots or dashes separate recipe titles

WORD SPACING
Generally clear word spacing, though some words can run together in cramped
passages. The scribe maintains fairly consistent spacing.

COMMON WORDS AS REFERENCE POINTS
These words appear frequently and can be used to confirm letterforms:
- "Take" — appears at the start of most recipes; confirms T, a, k, e
- "and" — very frequent; confirms a, n, d
- "the" — very frequent; confirms t, h, e
- "withe" — frequent; confirms w, i, t, h, e
- "ytt" / "itt" — frequent; confirms y/i, t (doubled)
- "putt" — frequent; confirms p, u, t (doubled)
- "them" — frequent; confirms t, h, e, m
- "water" — frequent; confirms w, a, t, e, r
- "togeather" — frequent; confirms the full word (note spelling)
