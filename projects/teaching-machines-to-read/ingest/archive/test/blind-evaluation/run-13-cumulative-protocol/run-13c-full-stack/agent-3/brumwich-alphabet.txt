SECTION 1: LOWERCASE LETTERS
====================================================

a — Open-topped, fairly round. Visible in "and," "a," "take," "water."
     This scribe's a is similar to the Henslow scribe's but slightly more
     open and rounded.

b — Ascender with a round bowl. Visible in "be," "butter," "bay."
     The ascender is moderate height with a slight rightward lean.

c — Small open curve. Visible in "come," "cloth." Similar to modern c.
     Can be confused with t in some positions.

d — This scribe's d has a looped ascender curving LEFT, consistent with
     secretary convention but the loop is less pronounced than Henslow.
     Visible in "and," "good," "dried."

e — Small, often like a tight reversed-3 or simple loop. Visible in
     "the," "take," "one." Sometimes open, sometimes closed.

f — Descends below the baseline with a crossbar. Visible in "for,"
     "fresh," "from." Distinguished from long-s by the crossbar.

g — Descender curves back. Visible in "good," "green," "great."
     Descender loops back to the right and up.

h — Ascender with a curved bottom stroke going right. Visible in
     "the," "this," "that," "halfe."

i — Single minim with dot (when present). Visible in "in," "it," "is."
     The dot is not always clear.

k — Tall with angular bottom strokes. Visible in "take," "make."

l — Tall vertical stroke with slight serif. Visible in "let," "lay."
     Can be confused with long-s.

long-s — Tall, NO crossbar (unlike f). Visible in "sugar," "spoonful."
     This scribe uses both long-s and round-s.

m — Three minims. Visible in "make," "much," "morning."

n — Two minims. Visible in "and," "one," "in."
     Difficult to distinguish from u in many cases.

o — Round. Visible in "of," "one," "or."

p — Descender below baseline, bowl at top. Visible in "put," "pound."

q — Rare, with tail. Visible in "quantity."

r — This scribe's r can appear in two forms: (1) a short angular stroke
     resembling v, (2) a more modern-looking r. Visible in "rosemary,"
     "receipt," "rinds." Must be carefully distinguished from v.

round-s — Used at word endings. Visible in "rinds," "days," "hours."

t — Short stroke with crossbar, does not extend far above the line.
     Visible in "take," "the," "to," "put."
     NOTE: Need to check if this scribe doubles final t's.

u — Two minims. Visible in "put," "sugar," "until."
     Nearly identical to n — must use context.

v — Angular downstroke. Visible in "very," "vinegar."
     Must distinguish from r.

w — Multi-stroke letter. Visible in "water," "with," "was."
     Distinctive, hard to confuse.

x — Rare. Cross shape.

y — Descender with possible dot. Visible in "you," "your," "yolks."

z — Not observed on this page.

consistently than the Henslow scribe. I will watch for "putt," "itt,"
and similar forms but will transcribe only what I see.

u/v CONVENTION:

"vinegar") and u medially. This may be more of a positional convention
than the Henslow scribe used.
