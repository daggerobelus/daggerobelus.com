BRUMWICH MS160 PAGE 10 — LETTERFORM ALPHABET CHART
====================================================
Built from study of the test manuscript before transcription.
This scribe writes in a mixed secretary/italic hand, more cursive
and compact than the Henslow training hand.

NOTE: This is a two-page spread. I am working through both visible
pages to identify letterforms from confident readings.

ANCHOR WORDS USED TO BUILD ALPHABET:
- "Take" — appears at the start of several recipes (large T)
- "the" — frequent throughout
- "and" — frequent throughout
- "of" — frequent throughout
- "water" — appears in recipes
- "a" — frequent article
- "to" — frequent preposition
- "for" — appears in recipe titles
- "good" — appears in recipe text
- "put" / "putt" — common recipe instruction

LOWERCASE LETTERS:
------------------
a — Open form, similar to modern lowercase a but rounder at top.
    Found in: "Take", "and", "water", "a"

b — Has a tall ascender with a loop curving right into the bowl.
    The bowl is round. Found in: words with "b" throughout.
    Can be confused with initial "t" if crossbar is faint.

c — Small open curve to the right. Similar to Henslow c.
    Found in: various words.

d — Secretary d with backward-looping ascender. The loop curves
    left/backward. Found in: "and", words ending in "-ed".

e — Small loop form. Very frequent in terminal position.
    Found in: "the", "Take", many words.

f — Tall letter WITH crossbar. Distinguished from long-s by this
    crossbar. Found in: "for", "of".
    NOTE: In this hand, "f" in "of" has a clear crossbar.

g — Descender curves right and loops back. Distinguished from "d"
    by the direction of the descender. Found in: "good", words
    with "-ing" endings.

h — Ascender with open bowl. The descender sometimes extends
    below the baseline in this hand. Found in: "the", "that".

i — Single minim, dot sometimes present. Part of minim cluster.

k — Angular strokes to the right from an ascender. Found in: "Take".

l — Tall ascender, no crossbar, no loop. In this scribe's hand,
    "l" is relatively straight. Distinguished from long-s by
    a slight rightward serif at top.

m — Three minims. This scribe writes them relatively evenly.

n — Two minims. In this hand, the peaks are somewhat angular.

o — Round, closed form sitting on baseline.

p — Descender below baseline with round bowl above.

r — In this hand, secretary "r" is a short stroke that can look
    like a shoulder or comma shape. Can be confused with "v".
    Found in: "water", "for".

s — TWO FORMS in this hand:
    Long-s: Tall, used initially and medially. NO crossbar
    (distinguishes from f). In this hand, long-s is relatively
    straight.
    Round-s: Used at word endings. Resembles modern "s".

t — Ascender with crossbar. In this hand, the crossbar is
    often connected to the next letter. Found in: "Take", "the",
    "to", "that".

u — Two minims with rounded bottom. In this hand, sometimes
    hard to distinguish from "n".

v — Angular bottom, used at word starts in some instances.

w — Wide letter, like two v's joined. Clear and distinctive.
    Found in: "water", "with".

x — Cross-shaped, rare.

y — Descender curving left below baseline. Found in: various words.

CAPITAL LETTERS:
----------------
Capital T — Large decorative form at recipe openings. Has wide
    horizontal strokes extending left and right. Very clear.

Capital F — May use ff convention or a large single F. Need to
    watch for both forms.

Capital A — Angular, larger version of lowercase with a more
    pronounced top stroke. Found in recipe titles.

Capital I — Watch carefully — can look very different from modern I
    in secretary hand.

SCRIBE HABITS OBSERVED:
-----------------------
1. DOUBLED CONSONANTS: Need to verify — checking for "putt", "ytt"
   patterns. This scribe may or may not double as consistently as Henslow.

2. SPELLING: Likely archaic spellings present. Must preserve exactly.

3. WORD SPACING: This scribe appears to have tighter word spacing than
   Henslow, making word boundaries harder to identify.

4. ABBREVIATIONS: Need to check if this scribe uses abbreviations or
   writes words out fully.

5. PUNCTUATION: Some punctuation visible — periods and possibly commas.

6. u/v CONVENTION: Need to observe whether this scribe uses v at word
   starts or u throughout.

7. The hand is more cursive than Henslow, with more connected letters
   and ligatures between letters within words.

8. The ink appears somewhat faded in places, making some letterforms
   harder to distinguish.

CONFUSABLE LETTER PAIRS IN THIS HAND:
--------------------------------------
- long-s / f / l — all tall letters; check for crossbar (f only)
- u / n — minim confusion; count carefully
- d / g — check descender direction
- r / v — secretary r can look like v
- c / t — in medial position
- e / o — when small and close together
- a / o — open vs. closed top
