Built from studying the test manuscript image before transcription.
This scribe's hand differs from the Henslow training hand in several
ways noted below.

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS:
- This is a two-page spread; I am transcribing the visible page(s)
- The hand is smaller, more informal, and more cramped than Henslow
- Less decorative — recipe titles are not as prominently set off
- The writing is in a mixed secretary/italic hand (some italic letter
  forms appearing alongside secretary forms)
- Ink varies in darkness — some passages are faded

LOWERCASE LETTERS:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
a — Open-topped, similar to Henslow. Identifiable in "Take," "and,"
     "water." Fairly consistent form.

b — Ascender with a bow. Visible in "bee," "boyle." Similar to Henslow
     but sometimes slightly more angular.

c — Small curve, can be hard to distinguish from t in some positions.
     Visible in "colde," common recipe words.

d — Secretary d with leftward-curving ascender, as in Henslow.
     Visible in "and" (very common). Sometimes the curve is less
     pronounced than in Henslow.

e — Small loop form. Very common. Visible in "the," "Take," etc.
     Sometimes appears as a simple curved stroke.

f — Tall with crossbar, descends below baseline. Distinguished from
     long-s by the crossbar. Visible in "for," "of" (very common).

g — Descender curves left. Visible in "good," final -inge endings.
     In this hand, the descender loop is often tight.

h — Ascender with rightward curve at bottom. Visible in "the,"
     "them," "halfe."

i — Single minim. Often lacks a dot in this hand. Must be counted
     among other minims carefully. Visible in "in," "itt."

k — Tall with angular lower strokes. Visible in "Take."

l — Tall WITHOUT crossbar. In this hand, l appears to have a slight
     loop or hook at top. Must distinguish from long-s and f.
     Visible in "lett," "litle."

m — Three minims. Visible in "make," "muche."

n — Two minims. Visible in "and," -inge endings.

o — Round, small. Visible in "of," "or," "one."

p — Descender below baseline with bowl. Visible in "putt," "pinte."

q — Rare. Descender going right. May appear in "quarte," "quantitie."

r — In this hand, r appears in both secretary form (like a 2) and a
     more italic form. Visible in "rosemarie," "water." Be cautious
     of r/v confusion.

s — TWO FORMS as expected:
     Long-s: tall, no crossbar (beginning/middle of words)
     Round-s: modern s-shape (end of words)
     In this hand, long-s is sometimes shorter than in Henslow,
     making it harder to distinguish.

t — Short ascender with crossbar. Shorter than l, f, long-s.
     Visible in "the," "to," "Take."

u — Two minims, similar to n. This scribe appears to use u at word
     starts (like Henslow), not v. Visible in "untill," "use."

v — Appears mid-word in words like "euerie," "ouer." May also appear
     at word starts — need to check carefully and not impose a rule.

w — Double-v shape, wide. Visible in "water," "withe," "with."

x — Rare. Cross shape.

y — Descender curves or goes straight down. Visible in "yow," "ytt."

CAPITAL LETTERS:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
A — Larger version, sometimes with a sweeping top stroke.
     Visible in recipe titles.

F — Written as ff (double-f convention) at word starts, same as Henslow.

R — May look different from modern R. Need to cross-reference carefully.

T — Large with horizontal bar. Visible in "Take" at recipe starts.
     Very consistent — good anchor letter.

SCRIBE HABIT NOTES FOR BRUMWICH:
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DOUBLED CONSONANTS: This scribe appears to double final consonants
   similarly to Henslow (putt, itt/ytt). Need to preserve these.

2. u/v CONVENTION: This scribe appears to use u at word beginnings
   (not v), similar to Henslow. Do not impose v-initial convention.

3. ABBREVIATIONS: This scribe may use more abbreviations than Henslow.
   Watch for tilde/macron marks over letters indicating missing n/m.

4. WORD SPACING: Some words appear to run together in this more
   cramped hand. Preserve manuscript spacing.

5. SPELLING: Expect -inge endings, -oure/-our forms, doubled consonants,
   and other early modern spellings. Do NOT normalize.

6. INK AND LEGIBILITY: Some portions are faded or cramped. Mark
   genuinely illegible sections with [...] and uncertain readings
   with [?].

7. PUNCTUATION: This scribe uses some punctuation marks (periods,
   colons). Preserve what is visible.
