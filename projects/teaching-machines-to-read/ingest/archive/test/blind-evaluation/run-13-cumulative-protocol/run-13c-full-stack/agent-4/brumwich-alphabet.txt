BRUMWICH MS160 LETTERFORM ALPHABET
Built from page 10 (two-page spread, pages 6-7)
=====================================================================

NOTE: This scribe writes in a more cursive, informal hand compared to the
Henslow MS688 training pages. The writing is smaller and more compressed,
with more ligatures between letters.

IDENTIFIED LETTERFORMS FROM CONFIDENT READINGS
(Using recipe titles, "Take," common words like "the," "and," "of," "in")

LOWERCASE LETTERS:

a — Rounded bowl, sometimes angular at top. Appears consistently in "Take,"
    "and," "water," "a." This scribe's a is fairly standard but can be
    confused with o when written quickly.

b — Ascender loops left, then rounded bowl. Visible in "be," "boyle," "but."
    Ascender is shorter than in Henslow.

c — Small rounded open form. Visible in "cold." Can look like t in this hand.

d — Secretary d with leftward loop on ascender. Confirmed in "and," "good."
    The loop is tighter/smaller than in Henslow.

e — Small rounded, often with a horizontal connector. Confirmed in "the,"
    "take," "three." This scribe's e can look like a small circle.

f — Long stroke with crossbar, descends below baseline. Confirmed in "for,"
    "of," "fresh." Distinguished from long-s by the crossbar.

g — Descender curves right in a loop. Confirmed in "good," "grease." Can
    be confused with d at word endings — check descender direction.

h — Ascender with curved right stroke. Confirmed in "the," "them," "thereto."
    The right stroke sometimes barely extends below baseline in this hand.

i — Single minim, sometimes dotted. Part of minim cluster. Confirmed in
    "in," "it," "is."

k — Ascender with angular strokes. Confirmed in "Take," "make."

l — Tall straight stroke. Confirmed in "let," "like." Can be confused with
    long-s in this hand — look for any slight curve that might indicate s.

m — Three minims. Confirmed in "make," "much," "mortar."

n — Two minims. Confirmed in "and," "in," "an." Hard to distinguish from u
    in this compressed hand — count minims carefully.

o — Small rounded form. Confirmed in "of," "or," "one." In this hand, o
    can be quite small and similar to a.

p — Descender straight down, rounded bowl. Confirmed in "put," "parts."

r — Two forms visible: (1) short stroke with shoulder, (2) v-shaped secretary
    r. This scribe uses both. Confirmed in "water," "or."

s — Two forms: (1) Long-s at word beginnings/middles — tall, no crossbar.
    (2) Round-s at word endings. Confirmed in "so," "some," "roses" (round-s).
    Long-s can look very like l in this hand — be cautious.

t — Short ascender with crossbar. Confirmed in "the," "to," "that," "take."
    This scribe's t is relatively clear.

u — Two minims joined at bottom. Part of minim cluster. Confirmed in "put,"
    "unto," "until."

v — Appears at some word beginnings. Confirmed in "very." This scribe seems
    to use v and u somewhat interchangeably at word starts.

w — Two v-shapes joined, wide. Confirmed in "water," "with," "when."
    Distinctive and usually clear even in this compressed hand.

x — Angular crossing. Rare.

y — Descender curves left. Sometimes dotted. Confirmed in "you," "your."

UPPERCASE / CAPITAL LETTERS:

A — Ornate, larger form. Confirmed in recipe titles beginning with "A."

F — Written as ff (double-f convention). Should appear at word starts as
    capital F equivalent.

T — Large form with prominent crossbar. Confirmed in "Take," "To," "The."
    This is the most frequently seen capital.

SCRIBE HABITS FOR THIS MANUSCRIPT:

1. DOUBLED CONSONANTS: This scribe appears to double some final consonants
   but perhaps less consistently than the Henslow scribe. Watch for: tt in
   "putt," "itt," "lett." Need to check each instance carefully.

2. SPELLING PATTERNS: Uses -inge endings (like Henslow). Uses "withe" for
   "with." Uses early modern spellings throughout.

3. WORD SPACING: This scribe sometimes compresses words together, making
   word boundaries harder to detect than in Henslow. Be careful about where
   words begin and end.

4. ABBREVIATIONS: Need to check — this scribe may use more abbreviations
   than the Henslow scribe given the more compressed writing style.

5. PUNCTUATION: Uses periods and possibly colons. Some decorative marks
   between recipes.

6. u/v CONVENTION: Need to verify — does this scribe follow the v-initial
   convention or use u everywhere?

7. LINE DENSITY: This scribe fits more text per page than Henslow, making
   careful line-by-line reading essential to avoid eye-skip.

MOST DANGEROUS CONFUSIONS IN THIS HAND:
- Long-s vs. l (very similar height and form)
- n vs. u (minims compressed together)
- a vs. o (both small and rounded)
- d vs. g at word endings (descender direction is key)
- r vs. v (secretary r resembles v)
- c vs. t in word middles
