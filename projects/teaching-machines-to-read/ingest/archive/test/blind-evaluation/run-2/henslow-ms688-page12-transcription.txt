Henslow MS 688, Page 12
Semi-diplomatic transcription following Folger editorial conventions.

---

My lorde of Caunterberries
......Brothe for a Consumptione......

Take like as before sauinge put therto a handfull of violett leaues
Langdebeefe leaues Boorrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit
togeather of euerie one athe quantitye. ......

......For the pyles......

Take one handefull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Coleworte
leafe, and rake them in the [Imbers?] untill they bee well rosted
then take them, and put awaye the Coleworte leafe, and grinde
them in a mortier, and put to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them togeather
then take it and laye it to the pyles withe a troffer, and vse this
          morninge and euenynge. ......

......For the flixe......

Take horsedonge new made and frie it withe sweete butter
and put it in a Clothe and laye it to the [fundament?] as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and lye vppen vntil you haue ease

......For Deafnes......

Take blacke wole whiche growes vpon the flanke
of a sheepe and [Farre?] and mingell them togeather
          and put them into the eare

---

Confidence Notes:

1. [Imbers?] — Line 2 of "For the pyles": The word appears to read "Imbers" (meaning embers/hot coals), which makes sense in context (roasting in hot embers), but the initial letter could be an 'I' or 'E'; the letterforms are slightly ambiguous.

2. [fundament?] — Line 2 of "For the flixe": The word is partially obscured at the right margin edge. The visible letterforms support "fundament" (a medical term for the anus, consistent with a remedy for flux), but the final letters are compressed at the margin.

3. [Farre?] — Line 2 of "For Deafnes": This word is not entirely clear. The letterforms could be read as "Farre" or possibly "Larde." The initial letter has a vertical stroke that could be either F or L, and the middle letters are somewhat compressed. "Farre" is my best reading of the visible strokes.

4. "athe" — Line 3 of the Consumptione recipe: This appears to read "athe" though "alike" is a possible alternative. I have transcribed what the letterforms appear to show.

5. "troffer" — Line 6 of "For the pyles": The letterforms appear to read "troffer" (a trough-like applicator). This is a clear reading.

6. "mollett" — Line 1 of "For the pyles": The letterforms clearly read "mollett" — this likely refers to mallow (malva) leaves.

7. "rake" — Line 2 of "For the pyles": The letterforms appear to read "rake" rather than "bake." This may be an archaic term or variant spelling.

8. The decorative dots (......) before and after recipe titles are line fillers in the original and have been represented here with dots.
