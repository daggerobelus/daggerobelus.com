ALPHABET SNAPSHOT — after set 02 (pages 007–011)
Lady Sedley receipt book, single scribe, 1686.
Extends alphabet-after-set-01. The hand is confirmed: a regular round/italic late-17thC
hand with light secretary features. Set 2 (recipes VI–XXIII) added many materia-medica
proper nouns and confirmed several habits. Changes from set-01 are marked [NEW]/[CONF].

=== CARRIED FORWARD (unchanged core, see alphabet-after-set-01) ===
Minuscules a–z, majuscules, and confusion risks as documented in set-01 snapshot.
The hand is stable across all 11 pages so far — same scribe, no hand-change.

=== CONFIRMATIONS & REFINEMENTS FROM SET 2 ===

[CONF] long-s (ſ): consistently initial/medial; round s terminal. "soarce", "stamp",
       "Substance", "Sallett". Doubled-s "ſſ" in "Masse", "diseass", "possett".

[CONF] terminal -e habit: "Coole", "boyle", "rowle", "searcloath", "houre". Strong.

[CONF] doubled consonants: "pott", "lett", "putt", "stopp", "fitt", "skellitt",
       "spoonefull", "handfull", "Allablaster", "Allcompaine". Very consistent.

[CONF] "yu"=you, "yr"=your/their (superscript graphs, lowered). Ubiquitous. ALSO
       "yt"=that/which — note the scribe uses "yt" loosely for both "that" and "which";
       transcribe as written "yt".

[NEW] Roman-numeral recipe numbers (VI, VII, VIII ... XXIII) centered under each
      recipe title. These are part of the page text and are transcribed as written.

[NEW] "&" remains the dominant conjunction graph -> "&". The scribe also writes "and"
      in full frequently (esp. line-final). Both occur; transcribe what is on the page.

[NEW] capital C vs Co: "Coole", "Ceros", "Cancer", "Camomill" — large open C, clear.

[NEW] "ff" = capital F confirmed: "ffirst" (page010). Rendered "ff".

[NEW] money/weight symbols: "pd" (pound) written as a flourished abbreviation; kept as
      "pd" per Folger (money/measure symbols kept as-is). "galls"=gallons, "dram(m)"=
      drams, "scruple", "ounce".

[NEW] numerals: arabic 2,3,4,5,6,10,12,14,20 used freely for quantities; clear.

=== ORTHOGRAPHIC HABITS (extended) ===
- v initial / u medial CONFIRMED: "vntill", "vlter", "vpon", "vinegar", "valerian".
- "ee" for long-e: "weeckes", "beere", "seeth", "reedes", "seede", "Beeswax".
- "oo"/"ou": "bloud", "houre", "poure", "rowle", "Coole".
- -inge / -ings present participle: "Stirringe", "droppinge", "comeing", "fastinge",
  "eatings", "drinkings". Both -inge and -ings occur.
- "soe", "noe", "doe", "goe", "twoe?": -oe terminal habit.
- scribe frequently writes "see" where modern reads "soe"/"so" — CAUTION: the words
  "see"(observe/then-so) and "soe"(thus) both occur; distinguish by context+strokes.
- Latin apothecary tags appear verbatim: "Pulvis sanctus", "Pulvis ad Calicum",
  "Probatum est", "aqua vitæ". Transcribe Latin as written.

=== CONFUSION RISKS (updated) ===
- "stone" vs "stanne"/"stand": the s-t-+minims cluster recurs for vessels and for
  "stand"/"stone"/"stew". Read carefully each time; flag when ambiguous.
- b / h initial loops in herb names (e.g. "barke"/"braine").
- "see"/"soe" homograph-ish (see above).
- proper-noun ingredients (Goats Rhine, chissels, Sentron, bellange, Scribers) are the
  main residual difficulty — many are rare materia medica; flag and leave near as-seen
  rather than normalize.
- w vs v initial in a few words ("wines"/"veines" type) — letterform genuinely
  ambiguous; transcribe the stroke, flag the sense.
