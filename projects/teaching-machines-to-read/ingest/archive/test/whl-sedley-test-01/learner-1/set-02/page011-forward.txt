A Sear Cloth for an Ache
XXI
Take halfe a pint of the best Sallett oyle, one ounce
and a halfe of white Ceros, a quarter of a pd of adust
Lead, beat them severally, put yr oyle into a lead
pan & set it on the coales till it begin to boyle
then then put in yr Ceros Stirringe it with a long
stick till it loose like faire water, then put in your
leade & Stir it till it looke blackish and dropp of it
till yu can rowle of it in yr hand, yu shall see it
enough boyled by droppinge it in fair water &
when it will rowle yu may make yr searcloath
presently.

A Diett drink for the dropsey Short
windedness or the Tisicke
XXII
Take one pound of Liquerish, Carraway seeds sweet
Coriander seeds, Annis seeds of each 2 ounces, parsly
one ounce unlesse the patient can make noe whole
then halfe an ounce will serve; Allcompaine roots
Pollypodie of each 2 ounces, Bay berries 3 ounces,
Rosemary, sage, Thyme, Burrage, Hysope, Winter
savory, Pennyroyall, Woodbinde, Neath wind, some
call it weathorwind, others call it Soats wind of each
one handfull, Take 3 galls of Spring water & boyle
all those together, till they come to halfe the quantity
when that is cold, Straine it through a cloan
cloath, put it into earthon bottles & drinke it halfe
an houre before yr eat or drinke any thing ells
in a morninge, one quarter of a pint at a draught

A receipt for a redd face
XXIII
Take the Iuice of Southern wood one large
spoone full put it into a pint of Strong Ale
or halfe a pint of Sacke drinke this by Six
of the Clock in the morninge fastinge, abstaine
from eatings or drinkings untill Tenn a Clocke
then take Southernwood & put a litle vinegar
to it & eat it with bread & butter as sage & all
Twelve you may goe to diner, this is to be observ-
ed for Tenn dayes together //
