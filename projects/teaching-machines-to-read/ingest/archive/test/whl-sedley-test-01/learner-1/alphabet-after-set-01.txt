ALPHABET SNAPSHOT — after set 01 (pages 002–006)
Lady Sedley receipt book, single scribe, 1686.
A round, mostly-italic late-17thC hand with residual secretary features. Generally
very legible, regular spacing, consistent letterforms. Built from the clearest words
across pages 003–006.

=== GENERAL CHARACTER OF THE HAND ===
- Predominantly an italic/round hand, NOT heavy secretary. Most minuscules are close
  to modern cursive, which makes this hand comparatively easy.
- Slight rightward slant. Ascenders (b,d,h,k,l) loop. Descenders (g,p,y) loop left.
- Words well separated; recipes set as running prose with titles centered/flourished.

=== MINUSCULES a–z ===

a  — single-storey round 'a', open or closed bowl, like modern italic a. Clear.
     examples: "Take", "Ale", "water", "hand"

b  — tall looped ascender, bowl to the right. examples: "boere/beere", "bottles", "Barly"

c  — small round c, like modern c. Can resemble 'e' or 'r' when cramped.
     examples: "closed", "ounce", "Cloves"

d  — looped ascender curving back left (secretary-ish), bowl round. examples: "drink",
     "dropsey", "dryed", "double"

e  — round modern-ish e with a small loop; sometimes the terminal -e has a long
     curling tail. Confusable with o and c. examples: "the", "Ale", "beere"

f  — long descender, single crossbar; like long-s but with the bar. examples: "fire",
     "first", "full", "of"

g  — looped descender curling left, open bowl above. examples: "great", "goings",
     "strong", "Ginger"

h  — tall loop ascender, long limb dropping below the line and curling. Confusable with
     b and with ll. examples: "haveing", "hand", "Hartshorne", "helps"

i  — short, dotted (dot often present). examples: "in", "it", "first"

j  — not clearly distinct; final-j in numerals not seen yet. i/j interchangeable.

k  — looped ascender + open loop to the right. examples: "make", "drink", "weeke"

l  — single tall loop ascender. examples: "little", "Ale", "salt", "Lady"

m  — three minims, rounded. examples: "make", "Mortar", "morning"

n  — two minims, rounded. examples: "new", "in", "ounce"

o  — round closed o. Confusable with e and a. examples: "of", "or", "Moone"

p  — descender straight or looped; bowl to right. "special p" for per-/pre- possible
     but not yet clearly seen. examples: "put", "pye", "pott", "pint"

q  — round bowl + straight descender. examples: "quart", "Quarter", "quantity"

r  — two forms: (1) a rounded modern r; (2) a short 'v'-like / 2-shaped r, esp. after o.
     examples: "roses", "drink", "Mortar", "water"

s  — TWO forms. long-s (ſ) initial/medial, looks like f without crossbar:
     "strong", "first", "ſhred". round/terminal s at word end: "roses", "as", "is".
     Doubled long-s "ſſ" common: "Crosse", "presse".

t  — short ascender with crossbar; often the crossbar links to next letter.
     examples: "Take", "the", "pott", "water"

u  — two minims like n inverted; u medial, v initial (see v). examples: "ounce", "must"

v  — v initial in words ("very", "vpon"); = u sound. examples: "very", "vertue"

w  — double-u, rounded, looped. examples: "water", "wash", "Wormwood", "new"

x  — seen rarely. example: "Excellent" (Exc-), "Mixe"

y  — looped descender curling left, like modern y; also used in "yt"(=that),
     "ye"(=the), "yr"(=your/their) superscript-style abbreviations.
     examples: "you", "pye", "may", "yt"

z  — not clearly attested yet in set 1.

=== MAJUSCULES (capitals) ===
A — large looped A ("A Receipt", "Ale", "Aqua", "Anoint")
T — large T, often with sweeping crossbar ("Take", "The", "Then")
M — ("Mortar", "Moone", "Mistress")
B — ("Barly", "Balme", "Bugloss")
S — ("Sorrell", "Strong", "Southernewood")
W — ("Wormwood", "With")
C — ("Cloves", "Crosse")
P — ("Probatum", "Put", "Pansey")
R — ("Receipt", "Rosemary")
Capitals used somewhat inconsistently mid-sentence (Folger tip #8).

=== LIGATURES & GRAPHS ===
- "&" / "and": the scribe very frequently uses an ampersand-like graph for "and" —
  a looped mark resembling a reversed-e with a tail, mid-sentence. Transcribe as "&"
  where it is the ampersand graph (Folger: preserve ampersands).
- th: written plainly (th); also thorn-y form "yt" = that, "ye" = the, "yu/you".
- final -es: round-s terminal, no special looped -es graph dominant; plain "-es".
- -er: plain.
- ff = capital F at word start ("ffixe"? check), and "ff" doubled.
- ct/st ligatures present in italic words ("Excellent", "first").

=== ORTHOGRAPHIC HABITS ===
- v word-initially ("very", "vpon", "vertue"), u medially ("ounce", "must", "haue").
- terminal -e frequent ("Moone", "weeke", "pye", "Ale", "beere"/"boere").
- doubled consonants: "pott", "bottles", "Sorrell", "Crosse", "putt".
- ie/ey spellings: "dropsey", "pye", "Barly", "Jelly".
- "yt"=that, "ye"=the, "yr"=your, "wch"=which, "wth"=with abbreviations expected.
- "boere"/"beere" both seen for beer; "Ale" capitalized.
- &c. for etcetera at line ends.

=== CONFUSION RISKS IN THIS HAND ===
- e / o / c — all round, distinguish by loop direction and openness.
- long-s (ſ) / f — f has the crossbar.
- h / b / ll — tall loops; h drops a long limb below line.
- r rounded vs r 2-shaped (after o, esp. "or", "Mortar").
- u / n — context and word position.
- minim strings (m, n, u, i) in "minimum"-like clusters.

=== CALIBRATION (5 clear words, letter-by-letter, vs vocab reference) ===
1. Take      -> T-a-k-e        (standard recipe opener)
2. water     -> w-a-t-e-r
3. ounce     -> o-u-n-c-e
4. Wormwood  -> W-o-r-m-w-o-o-d
5. Rosemary  -> R-o-s-e-m-a-r-y
All five are common, well-formed English/recipe words; checked below against vocab.
