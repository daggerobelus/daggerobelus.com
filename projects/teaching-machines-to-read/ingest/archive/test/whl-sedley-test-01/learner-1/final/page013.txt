A Salve for sundry uses called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII
Take one pd of oyle olive of the best & of white
Lead very well calcinated into dust, 12 ounces of
Spanish soap, incorporate all these well together in
an earthon pott well glazed & when they be well incor-
porated that the soap comes uppermost & put it upon
a small fire of coales, continuemgs the said fire an
houre & a halfe still stirring it wth a bolt of yron
fastned to a stick of wood, yt make yr fire some
what bigger, till the redness be turned into a grey
colour but yu must not leave stirring till the
matter be turned into the colour of oyle somar
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher & if it
neither cleave to yr fingers nor the trencher its boyled
enough then dippy cloathes in that, the searcloth
therein dipped will last 20 yeares, & yt older, yt better
this plaister laid to the stomach provokes appetite and
takes away the pains of the stomach laid to the
belly, t is a remedy for the Collick laid to the
reins of the back it stops the bloudy flux
Runings of the reins heat of the kidneys & weakness
of the back it heals all swellings, Aches & Bruises, it
breakes ffellons, Impostumes, pustles & holes them
beings applyed to the fundamt it heals all diseasses
therein laid to the head, it is good for the vmilla

A Good Cordiall for a woman in Childbed
XXVIII
Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & 2 ounces of
Syrrup of Clove gillyflowers & 2 pennyworth of Cinamon
water & 2 Spoonefulls of Clarett wine & Magnus one
Drame mingle all those together and it is a soveraigne
good cordiall for any especialy for one that lyes
in Childbed
