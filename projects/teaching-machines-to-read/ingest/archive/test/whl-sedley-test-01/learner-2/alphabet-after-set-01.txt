ALPHABET SNAPSHOT — after set 01 (pages 002–006)
Hand: single scribe, Lady Sedley receipt book, 1686. Mixed secretary/italic
("round") hand — leans more toward a late-17thC italic cursive than a heavy
early secretary, but retains secretary features (long-s, looped d, terminal
graphs). Built bottom-up from the clearest words on pp.003–006.

=== LOWERCASE LETTERFORMS ===

a — single-compartment round 'a', open or nearly closed bowl with a short
    upstroke. Clear in "Take", "large", "water". Confusion: with 'o' when bowl
    closes; with 'u' when bowl opens at top.

b — tall ascender, bowl to the right at baseline. "Barley", "boyle", "Bugles".
    Confusion: with 'h' (h loops below line, b does not).

c — small open hook; can look like 'r' or 't'. "close", "ounce", "Cloves".

d — looped ascender curving back left (secretary d), but also a straighter
    italic d appears. "drink", "dropsey", "made". Confusion: with 'cl'.

e — round italic 'e' with reversed loop; very common. "the", "Ale", "water".
    Sometimes Greek-e form. Confusion: with 'o', with 'c'.

f — long descender with crossbar. "for", "froth", "first". ff = capital F
    ("ffirst"?). Confusion: with long-s.

g — looped descender, open figure-8-ish tail. "garden", "great", "Bugles".

h — tall ascender + descending loop below baseline. "hearth", "Harts", "the".
    Confusion: with 'b'.

i — short minim, usually dotted. "it", "in", "pint".

j — not clearly distinguished; i/j interchangeable.

k — looped ascender with kick. "make", "drink", "weeke".

l — tall single ascender, sometimes looped. "Ale", "little", "salt".

m — three minims. "make", "morning", "small".

n — two minims. "new", "in", "wine".

o — round closed bowl. "of", "or", "roses". Confusion: with 'a','e'.

p — bowl + descender; descender plain (no obvious special-p brevigraph seen
    yet). "pye", "pot", "put".

q — descender with tail. "Quart", "Quarter".

r — two forms: a short 2-shaped/italic r mid-word, and a longer r. "roses",
    "very", "Barley". Confusion: with 'c', 'v'.

s — TWO forms: long-s (ſ) tall like f-without-crossbar, used initial/medial
    ("strong", "first", "close"); round terminal s at word end ("roses",
    "ounces", "pyes"). Doubled long-s "ſſ" in "passages"/"vessell".

t — short ascender just above x-height, crossed. "Take", "the", "put".
    Confusion: with 'c'.

u/v — v word-initial ("very","vpon"), u medial ("Quart","ounce"). Used per
    secretary convention; preserve as written.

w — double-u, looped. "water", "new", "wormwood".

x — seen in "Excellent". rare.

y — descender with tail; also used in "ye"=the (thorn-derived y) and "yt"=that,
    "yu"/"yor"=you/your superscript abbreviations. "very", "yellow", "may".

z — not yet clearly attested.

=== MAJUSCULES ===
T — large looped "T" in "Take", "The".
A — "Ale", "Aqua", "And".
B — "Barley", "Bugles", "Beere".
Q — "Quart", "Quarter".
P — "Palsy", "Probatum", "Put".
S — "Sorrell", "Strong".
R — "Receipt", "Roses".
M — "Mortar", "Moone", "Mirabilis".
W — "Wormwood", "Wound", "Water".

=== ABBREVIATIONS / GRAPHS SEEN ===
ye  = the (y = thorn)            yt = that            yu = you
yor / yr = your                  wch = which          wth = with
&   = and (also "&c.")           Probatum est (recipe-end formula)
~ over vowel = omitted m/n
terminal -es graph at word end.
Superscript letters silently lowered per Folger convention.

=== ORTHOGRAPHIC HABITS ===
- v initial, u medial (preserve).
- doubled final consonants: "Sorrell", "vessell", "well", "froth".
- terminal -e common: "Moone", "Beere", "close", "pye".
- "beere"/"Beere" for beer; "Ale"; "pye" for pie.
- i/j and u/v interchangeable.
- spelling preserved exactly (e.g. "Snayle", "Mirabilis", "froth").
