# Transcription Method — Build-Your-Own Hand Chart (capitalization & punctuation focus)

You are transcribing one page of an English secretary-hand recipe book (Lady Sedley
MS534). Produce a **semi-diplomatic** transcription: preserve original spelling,
word division, abbreviations, and line breaks; do not modernize or correct.

Do NOT transcribe top-to-bottom on a first read. Instead, work in three passes. The
first two passes build a private model of THIS scribe's hand; the third applies it.

---

## PASS 1 — Build the letterform chart for THIS hand

Before writing any transcription, survey the whole page and build a small chart of how
THIS scribe forms letters. For each of the following, find a clear instance on the page
and note its distinguishing feature:

- **Easily confused minuscules:** c/e/t, r/v, n/u, m/w, i/j, long-s (ſ) vs f (does the
  cross-stroke fully cross? f crosses, long-s does not), final-s shapes, terminal
  flourishes that are NOT letters.
- **Ascenders/descenders:** how h, l, b, k, d are looped; how g, y, p, q descend.
- **Common abbreviations:** the macron/tilde for omitted m/n; superscript letters
  (w^th, y^e, y^t); the per/par/pro brevigraphs; terminal -es/-is contractions; the
  thorn-like y^e ("the") and y^t ("that").

Hold this chart in mind. You will read every ambiguous mark by comparing it to your
own chart, not by guessing in isolation.

## PASS 2 — Build the capitalization & punctuation key for THIS hand

Secretary hand capitalizes and punctuates differently from print, and EACH scribe is
internally consistent in idiosyncratic ways. Before transcribing, decide this scribe's
conventions by sampling the page:

1. **Majuscule inventory.** Find 3-4 letters that appear both capital and lowercase
   (commonly C, S, T, A, R, W, H, L). For each, note what makes the capital form
   distinct (extra size, a two-stroke build, an entry loop, a doubled stroke). Some
   secretary capitals look nothing like their minuscule — calibrate before you read.
2. **Capitalization is positional, not grammatical.** This scribe may capitalize the
   first word of a line or recipe, proper names, and emphasized ingredients/quantities,
   while leaving sentence-internal words lowercase. Transcribe the letterform you
   actually see; do NOT impose modern sentence-initial capitals where the hand shows a
   minuscule, and do NOT lowercase a clear majuscule just because it sits mid-sentence.
3. **Distinguish capital from merely large/looped.** A tall ascender (l, h, d, b) or a
   flourished initial is not automatically a capital. Promote to a capital only when the
   form matches a majuscule in your inventory.
4. **Punctuation marks actually present.** Sample what marks this scribe uses: the
   virgule/slash (/) as a comma or pause, the period, the colon (often marking
   abbreviation, e.g. "viz:"), and any otiose terminal strokes. Note that a virgule and
   a descender tail can look alike — count it as punctuation only if it sits on the
   baseline between words, not as part of a letter.
5. **Do not invent punctuation.** Transcribe only marks the scribe wrote. If there is no
   terminal stop, add none. Otiose flourishes and pen-rests are NOT punctuation and are
   NOT letters — omit them.

## PASS 3 — Transcribe against your charts

Now read the page line by line, top to bottom, left to right.

- For every ambiguous letter, resolve it by matching against your PASS 1 chart.
- For every capital decision, resolve it by matching against your PASS 2 majuscule
  inventory — letterform first, never grammar.
- For every mark on the baseline, decide using your PASS 2 punctuation key: real mark,
  or otiose stroke to omit.
- Preserve original line breaks (one transcription line per manuscript line).
- Preserve original spelling and abbreviations exactly; do not silently expand. If you
  expand an abbreviation, mark the supplied letters in [square brackets].
- Use [?] for a fully illegible character and [word?] for an uncertain word.

## Output

Output only the transcription, one line per manuscript line. Do not include your charts,
notes, or commentary.
