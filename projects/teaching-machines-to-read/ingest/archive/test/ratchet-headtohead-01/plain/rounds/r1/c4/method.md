# Method: Diplomatic transcription of an English secretary-hand recipe page

You are transcribing one page of an early modern English recipe book in secretary hand. Build the transcription from first principles: study the hand before you read it, assemble words from the strokes you actually see, and leave an honest gap wherever you cannot read. Above all, reproduce the page's physical layout — its lines, its breaks, its indentation, its blank space — because the layout fixes where every character belongs and is the cheapest source of avoidable error to get right. Follow the stages in order.

## Stage A — Map the page as a grid BEFORE reading any word

Do not read for meaning yet. First survey the leaf as a physical object and write down its skeleton, because everything below hangs on this map:

1. **Scan top to bottom and count every inked line of text.** Each inked line is exactly one output line. Fix that count now; your output must contain the same number of text lines.
2. **Identify the page's vertical structure (zones), top to bottom.** Mark where each of these sits, if present:
   - a heading / recipe title (often centered, larger, or set off above a block);
   - the recipe body (the main running text);
   - marginal notes (text in the left or right margin, beside the body);
   - a catchword (a single word alone at the bottom-right corner);
   - page or folio numbers (a corner numeral);
   - later additions in a different ink or hand.
3. **For each text line, note its horizontal placement:** flush left (most body lines), indented (a new step, a sub-item, a continuation), centered (a heading), or set to the right (a quantity, a date, a marginal tag). Placement is information — you will reproduce it.
4. **Note the blank bands:** vertical gaps that separate heading from body, or one recipe from the next. Each such gap becomes a blank line in the output.

This grid is your contract. The wording you supply later must drop into it without moving any line.

## Stage B — Build this scribe's hand (study letterforms first)

Pick three or four of the most clearly written words and use them to learn THIS scribe's forms before committing to any hard reading. Decide, for this hand specifically:

- **Minims** — the single down-strokes that make `i n m u`. Count them carefully; miscounting minims is the main cause of `in`/`m`, `ui`/`w`, `ni`/`m` errors.
- **The s-forms** — round/terminal `s` vs. the tall long `ſ`. Transcribe long `ſ` as a plain `s`.
- **Easily confused pairs in this hand** — `c`/`t`/`r`, looped secretary `e`, secretary `d` (with its back-curving ascender), `h` with a descender, and the `w`/`v`/`u` shapes.
- **Abbreviation marks** — a macron/tilde over a vowel (omitted m or n), the `p`-with-loop (per/par/pro), superscripts (`wch` = which, `yt` = that, `ye` = the), terminal contraction sweeps, the `-es`/`-is` loop.

Hold these decisions constant across the whole page; resolve a doubtful letter by comparing it to the same letter elsewhere on this page, never by guessing the word.

## Stage C — Transcribe line by line, in the grid, bottom-up

Work through the lines in reading order, top to bottom, filling the grid from Stage A. For each line:

1. Build each word from the page up: **strokes → letters → word.** Do not read a word from its overall shape; assemble it from the letters you actually see, then check whether it makes sense. If letters and expectation disagree, trust the letters.
2. **Type exactly the characters on that one physical line.** Do not merge two inked lines into one. Do not split one inked line into two. Do not let text wrap — keep the line as long as it runs on the page.
3. **End the output line where the ink ends on the page** — a hard line break — even when the sentence continues onto the next line.

## Stage D — Reproduce spacing and layout faithfully

- **Line breaks:** exactly as on the page (Stage C). One inked line in, one line out.
- **Blank lines:** output one empty line for each blank band that separates a heading from its body or one recipe from the next.
- **Indentation and horizontal position:** put leading spaces so each line sits where it sits on the page — indent indented lines; offset a centered heading so it reads as centered; keep a right-set quantity, date, or marginal tag to the right of its line. Relative position must be legible from the plain text.
- **Marginal notes:** transcribe them in place beside the body lines they sit next to, not folded into the running text.
- **Word spacing:** a single space between words and after punctuation; do not invent extra gaps and do not delete the spaces the scribe left. Where the scribe deliberately columns an ingredient against its amount, keep that alignment rather than collapsing it.
- **Word broken across a line:** keep the break where it falls; reproduce a hyphen only if one is actually inked.

## Stage E — Gap rather than guess

A firm rule: when a letter or word is illegible or genuinely uncertain after you have built it from strokes, do NOT invent a plausible word.

- Unreadable single character → a single period `.` in its place.
- Unreadable whole word → `[...]` in its place, kept on the correct line and at the correct horizontal position so the Stage A grid is undisturbed.
- A wrong guessed word usually costs more character errors than an honest gap. Prefer the gap. Never pad, drop, or reflow lines to tidy a gap.

## Stage F — Diplomatic conventions (write what is inked, not modernized)

- **Spelling:** transcribe exactly as written — original spelling, doubled letters, `u`/`v` and `i`/`j` as the scribe forms them, `y` for thorn (`ye`, `yt`). Do not modernize or correct.
- **Capitalization and punctuation:** reproduce as written; add no modern punctuation.
- **Abbreviations:** keep the scribe's mark as written (`wch`, `ye`, `yt`, macron forms). Do not silently expand. If you do expand, expand only a standard, recognizable abbreviation, and that is the only place you may supply letters.
- **Deletions and insertions:** render a struck word as struck if legible, never silently "fix" it; place an interlinear insertion at the point it belongs, marked `^ ^` around the inserted text.

## Stage G — Final check against the grid

Before finishing, verify:

- Same number of text lines as the page; line breaks fall in the same places.
- Headings, indentation, marginalia, catchword, and any right-set material sit where they do on the page.
- No invented words; every uncertainty is marked `.` or `[...]`.
- Word spacing mirrors the page.

Output only the transcription, with its line breaks and spacing preserved. No commentary, no translation, no summary.
