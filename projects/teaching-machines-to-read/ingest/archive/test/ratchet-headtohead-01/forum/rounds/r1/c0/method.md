## Long-s, f, and ſ disambiguation

This hand uses the long form of the letter s (ſ), which closely resembles f. Misreading one for the other is the single most common transcription error in this script. Before committing each tall vertical stroke, decide deliberately whether it is a long-s or an f:

- A long-s (ſ) has NO crossbar, or at most a short spur on the LEFT side of the stem only. Transcribe it as a normal modern "s". Do NOT preserve the long-s glyph; render every long-s as plain "s".
- An f has a full crossbar that crosses THROUGH the stem, extending to both the left and the right of the vertical. Transcribe it as "f".
- When the crossbar is faint or ambiguous, use the word to decide: read the candidate letter both ways ("s" and "f") and keep the reading that forms a real English word. For example "ſelf" is "self" not "felf"; "halfe" has an f, "houſe" has an s.
- Long-s appears initially and medially but NOT as the final letter of a word; a final tall-s is almost always a round terminal "s", so word-final ambiguous strokes default to "s".
- The doubled form "ſſ" and the "ſs" pairing both transcribe simply as "ss" (e.g. "neceſſary" → "necessary").
- Do not confuse a long-s with the ascender of a tall "l" or "b"; those have loops at the top, while long-s rises to a plain or hooked head.

After completing the transcription, re-scan every word containing an "f" or "s" and confirm each forms a plausible English word; correct any long-s/f swaps you find.
