This is an English secretary hand (roughly 1500–1700). Preserve original spelling, capitalization, and word division as written; do not modernize. Expand nothing silently. The instructions below tune how to resolve three letterforms that are the most frequent source of single-character errors in this hand: secretary **e**, **d**, and **c**. When a stroke is genuinely ambiguous, resolve it with these defaults rather than guessing freely.

SECRETARY e
- The common secretary **e** is the reversed/two-stroke ("epsilon"-like) form, not a modern looped e. It is frequently mis-shaped and gets confused with **o**, **c**, and **r**.
- Default rule: a small rounded letter with a horizontal or back-curving cross-stroke that closes the loop is **e**, not **o**. Reserve **o** only for a fully closed round bowl with no cross-stroke.
- A small open letter that does NOT close into a bowl is **c**, not **e**. The presence of a closing/cross stroke distinguishes **e** (closed) from **c** (open).
- Where the stroke could read as **e** or **r**, prefer **e** in mid-word vowel positions; prefer **r** only where a consonant is required by the surrounding letters.

SECRETARY c
- Secretary **c** is broad and open at the right, and is easily confused with **t**, **r**, and **e**.
- Default rule: an open curve with NO vertical shaft or cross-bar is **c**. If there is a clear vertical shaft crossed near the top, read **t**. Do not promote a plain open **c** to **t** without a visible cross-bar.
- Distinguish **c** from **e** by closure (see above): open = **c**, closed by a cross/back stroke = **e**.

SECRETARY d
- Secretary **d** has a looped ascender that curves up and to the LEFT (back over the body), unlike modern straight-ascender d. Its loop can be misread as a separate letter or merged with the preceding letter.
- Default rule: a left-curving looped ascender attached to a round bowl is one letter, **d** — do not split it into two characters.
- A final small round form at word-end is more often **d** or **e** than **o**; resolve **d** vs **e** by the ascender (present = **d**, absent = **e**).

THE GOVERNING HEURISTIC (closure threshold)
- The single decision that drives most e/c/o errors is closure. Apply this order every time a small rounded letter is ambiguous:
  1. Is the right side OPEN (no joining or back stroke)? → **c**.
  2. Is it closed by a light cross/back stroke but the bowl is small and sits on the baseline? → **e**.
  3. Is it a full, fat, fully closed round bowl with no cross stroke? → **o**.
- Calibrate toward **e** in this hand: secretary **e** is the most frequent of the three, so when closure is present but you are unsure between **e** and **o**, default to **e** unless the bowl is unmistakably large and round.

GENERAL TIE-BREAK
- After applying the form rules above, if a letter is still ambiguous, choose the reading that yields a plausible period spelling of an English word over a non-word string, but never silently correct a clearly written letter to "fix" spelling. Preserve what is on the page.
- Do not normalize i/j, u/v, or the long-s; transcribe them as written.
