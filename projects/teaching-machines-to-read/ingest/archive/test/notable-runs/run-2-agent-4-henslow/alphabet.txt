HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET — single scribe, English medical receipt book (secretary/mixed hand)

GENERAL CHARACTER
- Fluent mixed hand: largely secretary forms in the body, more italic/display forms in
  the recipe headings ("For the pyles", "For the flixe", "For Deffnes", "Take").
- Generous spacing; words clearly separated. Headings centered with decorative
  line-fillers (wavy ~ marks) flanking them.

LETTERFORMS (minuscule unless noted)
a  - round single-compartment 'a'; clear. Ex: "and", "take", "a", "maye".
b  - looped ascender; clear. Ex: "before", "blacke", "butter", "bee".
c  - small open c; can resemble 'r' or 't' at speed. Ex: "clothe", "cave/eare", "Colewort".
d  - looped ascender curving left (secretary). Ex: "and", "made", "grinde", "handefull".
e  - secretary reversed-e, also a round italic e in display words; terminal -e common.
     Ex: "leaues", "before", "eare". Confusable with 'o'.
f  - long descender with crossbar; single f. Ex: "for", "of", "fundamente".
ff - doubled f at word start = capital F: "fflixe"->For?? NOTE: here word-initial cap is
     written as ornate "F". Internal -ff- common in this hand: "beeffe", "Deffnes",
     "suffer". Word "Deffnes" uses internal ff.
g  - secretary g with open descender loop. Ex: "growes", "grinde", "mingell", "togeather".
h  - tall loop, descender curl below line; can resemble 'b'. Ex: "the", "them", "withe", "hott".
i  - dotted i; used interchangeably with j (none distinctly j here). Ex: "in", "itt".
k  - looped form, often elaborate. Ex: "take", "knitt", "blacke", "rake".
l  - simple looped ascender; doubled in "well", "mollett", "till".
m  - three minims; clear. Ex: "made", "them", "morter", "mingell".
n  - two minims. Ex: "and", "new", "vntill".
o  - round; confusable with 'e'. Ex: "of", "one", "roses", "woole".
p  - secretary p; "pyles", "putt", "vppon", "wrappe".
q  - (quantitye) clear q + u.
r  - secretary r and a 2-shaped/short r; final -r sometimes hooked. Ex: "for", "roses", "rake".
s  - long-s (f-like, no crossbar) initial/medial: "suffer", "rosted", "roses";
     round/sigma s at word end: "leaues", "growes", "this", "roses".
t  - crossed t; terminal -tt doubled frequently: "itt", "putt", "mollett", "knitt", "hott".
u/v - v at word start, u medial: "vntill", "vppon", "vse", "violett"; "leaues", "haue",
      "suffer", "euenynge". Both written as the same family of strokes.
w  - secretary w with initial loop: "withe", "woole", "wrappe", "whiche", "growes", "new".
x  - in "flixe": x clear.
y  - y with descender; also used in "ytt" (=itt/it) "yow" (=you), "maye".
z  - none observed.

LIGATURES / GRAPHS / ABBREVIATIONS
- "the" written in full (t-h-e); NO thorn "ye" form observed on this page.
- "them" / "togeather" written in full.
- Terminal -es: written out as round-s (e.g. "leaues", "roses", "pyles"); no special -es graph needed.
- Doubled terminal consonants are a strong habit: -tt (itt, putt, mollett, knitt, hott),
  -ff (beeffe, Deffnes), -ll (mollett, well, mingell), -ckе (blacke, Colewort? no).
- Decorative wavy line-fillers (~ ~ ~) flank headings; these are NOT text.

ORTHOGRAPHIC HABITS OF THIS SCRIBE
- v at word-initial, u medial: vpon->"vppon", vntill, vse, violett; haue, leaues, suffer.
- i/y: writes "ytt" and "itt" for 'it'; "yow" for 'you'; "maye".
- Terminal -e very common: clothe, withe, leafe, sheepe, eare, flanke, before.
- Doubled final consonants very common (see above).
- -inge spelling for -ing: euenynge/euenyge, morninge, sauinge.
- "whiche" for which; "growes"; "wrappe".
- ck/cke: "blacke", "rake".
- Spelling examples: "consumptione", "fundamente", "togeather", "mingell".
