Scold yrr Boar as you do a Porter ynn. yee next night take out
all yee ones & leave yee meat Entirely whole, ynn wett itt yee Cross way
into Collars, ynn lay itt in a Tubb where itt may lay full length

out yee blood, ynn. putt that brine from itt & fill yee Tubb with
Spring Water, Shift yee Water morning & Evening for 4 Dayes

you must dry itt very well before you Salt itt; ynn role it as
close as possible, ynn take two pieces of Pack thread & twist
hard about itt with a  Screw att both Ends of e Collar, ynn take
broad filletting & bind round itt very hard & close as to
lap one after Another, ynn have ready a piece of lounte cloath,
with lace Holes made thick on Each side & lace ytt tight
over with pack thread, ynn fill up yee ends of ye Collar with
pieces of Flesh & putt a handfull of Salt att Each end of yee
collar before you sow itt up, ynn tye a Strong String about yee
middle of Each Collar, yee String must be long Enough to lett itt
down near to yee bottom of ye Furnace, ynn lay a Coullstaff
over yee top of yee Furnace & tye yee strings of Each Collar to that





Keep itt Scumming all ye while itt boyls, ye Water must Boyle

A Powder for Fits Mrs Pennistone

Gather the heade of Ladysmock with the small stalks
only, when the flowers are in full bloom; dry them in
pewter Dishes before the fire till they will powder, then
rub them in a Marble Mortar & sift the powder thro' a
lawn sieve, what remains too [grofs?] to pass thro' dry & powder
again, & this must be constantly repeadet, till the whole
is reduced fine enough to go thro' the lawn.

The Dose for a young Child as much as will lie on a
silver groat, for a grown Person as much as will lie on a
Shilling, to be taken in any thing liquid, once every day if
the Fits are frequent & strong.

N.B: These flowers are generally in full Bloom in the beginning
of May & grow in Meadows or Marshy ground, they are call'd
Cuckow-Flower, Meadow Cresses, or Lady-smock.

Receipt to make Pommade Divine

Take of Beef Marrow a full pound and half well picked
from the bones and filaments, put it into an Earthen
Vesel filled with Spring Water, change the Water twice
a day, ten days successively, then drain it off, and put a
pint of Rose Water to it, and let it stand twenty four hours,
then take it out, drain and wipe it thoroughlydry in a
thin Cloth; then beat to a fine Powder, an ounce of B
an ounce of storax, an ounce of cypress-nuts, an ounce of orris
of florence, half an ounce of cinnamon, two drams of cloves,
and two drams of nutmegs; mix all these well with the
marrow, then put it into a Pewter Vessel well stored, so that
nothing may evaporate; suspend the vessel in a copper of
Boiling Water, and let it Boil three hours without ceasing,
having Boiling Water ready to replenish the copper with, so
that the Pewter Vessel may be kept the whole time covered
with the ater; at the expiration of the three Hours, pour
it thro' a fine muslin into small pots, when quite cold,
cover them with paper, this is an Excellant Remedy
for a swell'd Breast, Face, or any swollen member, it is
aslo of Efficacious use in all external Bruises, it must
be melted when used, and well rubbed in, by an Hand
well Heated.
