Pease Soup

Two, or three gallons of water to a
quart of blue pease. Three or four
potatoes & as many onions. Pepper
and Salt to your taste. Then
stew down for four or five hours
the bones & any roasted meat (beef
bones are best) with as much fat
as possible, in an Iron  or cast metal
pot very gently-- Strain it thro' a
hair Sieve & let it stand till
next day. Scum it & take as much
of the jelly as you want. Warm
it & add celery cut in pieces
green tops, a little Mint. When
put in the tureen strew bread cut in
dice & fried in butter on the top--
This must be done last, or it will
sink to the bottom & be heavy. A
marrow bone is a great addition

Sir William Robinson beef
for luncheon

Take a piece of brisket of beef 10 or 12
pounds weight.  Salt it well with
Salt and Saltpetre.  Let it be a
week or ten days.  Then tie it
up very hard in a cloth and let it
boil gently for Nine hours.
Take the bones out, tie it up again
and press it till cold.  When
perfectly So take off the cloth & it
is ready for Eating.   It should
be Eaten with Mustard and
Vinegar.

Receipt for a Curry (Mr. Westings)

A fowl is to be cut in the same manner as
for a fricassee, fry'd till light brown with fresh
butter; one onion shreded & some garlick fry'd
till they are light brown; with salt to the taste.
Then take three teaspoonfuls of curry powder,
mix it with a little gravy made of the neck of
the fowl & put it with the fowl into a stew pan;
with good gravy to cover the fowl. Then take
one ounce of almonds, blanch & pound them in
a mortar with a little milk to present
their oiling & put it to the fowl.  All these
ingredients must stew together slowly for
two hours & before it is dish'd up, add the juice
of one lemon.

N.B.  One chilli is a great improvement
if it can be procured.

To Boil Rice

Let it be well picked, and washed in several waters: let it
be thrown into boiling water, the larger the quantity of water the
better--  Let it boil for ten minutes-- then pour off the water--
put the rice into a bowl; which it will fill, cover the bowl
with a plate & holding both sideways, drain as much of the
water from the Rice, as will fall from it, and then turning
over the bowl, shake, and lift if off and leave the rice
on the plate.  Lastly let it stand for a little time to
dry near the fire--  If it is well done, the grains will
be equally soft all through, and fall asunder from
each other.-- if too much done they will be pappy
and adhere together; if too little, they will be hard
and gritty.

Receipt to Make Curry Powder

Take one pound coriander seed, Two ounces
of White Cumming seed, One ounce of Fenigreek
Seed, fry them over a slow fire. Then pound
them in a Mortar and sift them fine; put
one and a half ounce of black pepper to it
and the Curry Powder will be complete.
When you want to make Curry take two spoonfuls
of the above mixed powder; add to it one
teaspoonful of tumeric & make the Curry as the
receipt directs.

N.B.  If you have some chillies
they are a pleasant addition.  The Tumeric, the
Lemon, & the onion are not to be added till
within half an hour before the Curry is served
up.  The back bone neck bones of the chicken
are to be boiled & added to the Curry instead
of Water.

Imperial Soup

Put eight yolks of Eggs into a Basin, with a
little grated nutmeg and salt; beat them together,
mixing them with half a pint of strong cleear soup:
Strain the preparation through a hair-sieve with
a plain mould which has been buttered for the
purpose:   Steam this in the same way as
any other custard; & when done, put it
to cool;  Cut the custard, thus prepared;
into fanciful shapes; and having placed
them in the tureen, pour on gently some
bright boiling Soup of the same kind as
that used to make the custard.

C. S.

Calfs' foot jelly (the Avington Receipt)

Take four calfs' feet and four quarts of water.
Boil them five hours, till the water be reduced
to two quarts; then strain off the scum of the
fat. Add one pint of white wine, the
juice of ten lemons, the peel of four, one
tablespoonful of allspice, one of coriander seeds,
six cloves, four bay leaves, a quarter of an
ounce of cinnamon, & one ounce of isinglass.
Sweeten with lump sugar according to your
taste; clarify it with twelve eggs, & then
pass it through the jelly bag.

To Cure Hams

7 lb of Bay salt, 1/2 lb of common salt, 2 oz of
Black Pepper, 2 oz of salt petre.   Grind all the above
quite fine, & mix them well together.  Rub the
Hams well with this mixture & let them lie in
it 4 days, rubbing them well every day.  Then
pour over them 7 & 1/2 lbs of treacle; turn them &
rub them with treacle three times a week for
a month:  Then lay them in a tub of cold spring
water for 24 hours; and on taking them out
they should immediately be hung in a chimney
to dry.  The sum of the above ingredients
is sufficient for two Hams of from 12 to 16 lbs
weight.  When dressed, they should be
put into cold water & boiled with it; and
(N.B.)     not  to be soaked the night before,
like hams in general

To Preserve Figs

Take Figs nearly ripe, and make incisions of
half inch in length near the stem, and put
them into cold water; then put them on the
fire, and let them boil at least half an hour
take them out and put them into cold water;
when cold drain them, and arrange them
in an earthen pan, then cover them with
thin Syrup, the next day drain off the
Syrup, boil it a degree more, skimming
it carefully, and add a little more fresh
Syrup if required, the third day repeat
the same process, on the fourth day drain
them, & boil the Syrup & make it a little
stronger, put in the Figs, & boil the Syrup
over them once, skim them well & put them
in dry pans or glasses for use.

using it your bread or cakes

Receipt to Dry Apricots Whole

Gather some fine Apricots - not too ripe - carefully
take out the stones, then peel the Apricots & blanch the kernels
& put them into the Apricots again-- to every pound of
Apricots put three quarters of a pound of loaf sugar-- boiling
it in a little water for twenty minutes to form a syrup
then just scald the fruit & take them out with great care
into a deep dish-- pour the syrup over them-- do this three
times-- & put them on sieves to dry-- turn them once or twice
a day to keep the shape round

Observe-- After they have been scalded & the
syrup poured over them-- Wait till the next day-- then
again scald them & again pour the syrup over them--
& do the same the third time-- after that put them
on the sieves to dry, dusting them occaionally with pounded
sugar to make them candied-- a cool oven or
what is much better the Sun under a garden glass
will dry them.
