Rules for keepinge fire in Stoues
for drieinge sweetmeats and
Candieinge.

Sett yor fire in a Chafing dish or the like, either
soe wthout the cup=bord, or wthin, at the bottom, yt
the heat be not too hott, but temperate, else
it may melt the sugar againe, and soe
hinder candieinge altogether; or else make
them very tough, or Chang the Couler, and
dim the glassines of the pasts And lett the
stoue haue a Continuall heat in it, by often
putting fire to it in a day; Pasts well dried
will not easily giue againe; If at all kept
drye.
Bricke stoues till they are very well seasoned,
by often vseinge, are very apt to giue against
moisture, wthout a Continuall heat in them,
Nay at the best when they are new for dry
wthout fire they will damp,

Rules to keep Preserues, And all
Manner of Sweet=meats

All wett sweetmeats must be coued close to
the sirup, wth a cleane white paper, when they
are putt vp to keepe and cold, to pvent miteing
and frothing the Longer, yett you must open
yor sweetmeat potts once a month, to pvent
miteing and frothing, that if they doe yow
may put New papers to them; And if they
chance at any time, to froth or Candy, the
best way to recou them is to sett the pott
in a posnett of hott water, till yow see it come
to the pfect sirrup againe; and this is good if
they Sowre alsoe.

But if yow can, pvent wett sweetmeats,
standing in, hott or moist places, but drier
sweetmeats or Candyes, must be kept from
moistnes, though not to drye.
Regard when yow spend of a pott of wett
sweetmeats, take them out if yow can, all of
a Roe, that yor paper may lye as neere to the
sirup as yow can to keepe it from miteing

Note that all sweetmeats, the next yeare
after they are pserued, about the same time
that the same fruite Blossomes, they will
lose theire Couler, att least for that time.





Note that the greener any fruite is it will
keepe the Longer as example the sucket.

Note that if yow will send any sweetmeats
that the sooner yow send them after the pserueing
it is the better; for the Longer they stand
when they are caried, they are the apter
to froth.

If yow will haue yo Quinces to pserue
white, they must not be to ripe, and
new gathered, and the Aple Quinces, and
free from knotts and stonenines.

Some hold that potts or boxes of
Marmalett and Sweetmeats, being sett
one vpon another, will mould the sooner.

Note that if yor Porsnett or pann that
yow pserue in, haue a peece in the bottom
it will make the sweetmeats to be
burnt too.

The Apothicaris best way: for Preserued
Cytrons. Mr Pelling

Slitt & quarter them, take out the pulp, & stringes
lay them to Soake in no more faire water then to
Couer them for 24 Howers, or more, to take out
thear bitter tast. (but then the Vertue is not soe
much) then put them into Hott water, to boyle
tell they bee cleer and tender enough: then make
a syrrop with the vsuall proportion that Wetts
Suger into a Syrrop, as some 2 parts Suger, to one
of Water, or rather to 2 Pound of Suger, a Pint
& a half of Water for Cytron; if the Suger is but
ordinary refined, then Clarefye it, but if Barbery,
then it needs only scuminge; & when the Syrrop
is neer Colde, put in the Cytron, in some Dish
the better to keep it couerd for 3 or 5, or more
dayes as you see cause that the Syrrop growes wat-
erish, then Power the Syrrop out, & boyle it a waume
or 2, then as formerly coole it, so power itt vpon e
Cytron againe, som will Power it on Hott, but he
thinks the Powring it on hott, makes the Cytron
apt to turne into thick, & Speckled white Spotts;
as also the boyling the Cytron makes it hearder;





then as formerly, when ye Syrrop growes Wa-
terish, boyle it againe, do thus 4 or 6 or more
times, vntell the Syrrop will cantinue in a fitt
Substance or thicknes: Yet once in a Month Looke
after them to see if the Syrrop thins; or Works
or such like accidents: And when the Year
grows warme, as in Iune, or Iuly, then set them
Potts in Coole drye Sellors; for Heate at any time
of year makes Cytron grow thick, & spotted
the La Lunly, after the Cytron is boyld in Water
sets them that Night to Draine in a Warme Ouen
after Bread is Drawen, & then as formerly puts
them into the Syrrop
