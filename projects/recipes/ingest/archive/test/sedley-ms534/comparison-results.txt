LIST OF DIFFERENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Word position 14:
   Reference: "pound"
   Guided:    "pd"
   Context:   "Take one [pound/pd] of oyle olive of the best"

   Analysis: This is the first occurrence of the word in the opening
   line of Recipe XXVII. At the image resolution available, the word
   appeared to be abbreviated as "pd" (a common abbreviation for
   "pound" in early modern manuscripts). However, the reference
   transcription reads it as "pound" written out in full. The
   reference transcribers likely had access to a higher-resolution
   image. This is a misreading of an abbreviated vs. spelled-out
   word.

   Error type: Misreading (abbreviation vs. full word)

2. Word position 231:
   Reference: "h^eales"
   Guided:    "heales"
   Context:   "pushes & [h^eales/heales] them"

   Analysis: The reference transcription marks this word with a caret
   (h^eales) to indicate an interlineal insertion -- the letters
   "eales" were added above the line after the initial "h". This is
   an editorial notation following the semi-diplomatic convention for
   insertions (per the paleography guide, Part 4: "Interlineal
   insertions remain superscript and are marked by a caret symbol (^)
   if a caret is present in the original"). The guided transcription
   read the word correctly but failed to note the physical layout
   feature of the interlineal insertion.

   Error type: Missed editorial notation (interlineal insertion)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
ERROR ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The two errors fall into distinct categories:

1. MISREADING (1 instance): Reading "pd" where the manuscript
   actually says "pound" in full. This type of error involves
   misidentifying letterforms, likely due to image resolution
   limitations. At the resolution available, the word was ambiguous.

2. MISSED CODICOLOGICAL FEATURE (1 instance): Failing to note that
   "heales" was written as an interlineal insertion (h^eales). The
   word itself was read correctly, but the physical layout detail --
   that part of the word was inserted above the line -- was not
   captured. This type of error does not affect the textual content
   but does lose information about how the manuscript was physically
   composed (which can be significant for understanding scribal
   corrections and revisions).

Neither error involved:
- Misreading secretary hand letterforms (e/o confusion, long-s/f, etc.)
- Missed or incorrect abbreviation expansion
- Wrong word boundaries or line breaks
- Missed or invented words
- Spelling modernization (which would violate transcription principles)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

At 99.5% word-level accuracy, the guided transcription using the
paleography guide performs very well on this manuscript page. The
Lady Sedley manuscript (1686) is written in a clear, relatively
late hand that is more legible than many earlier secretary hand
manuscripts. Key factors in the high accuracy:

- The hand is neat and consistent
- The ink is generally dark and well-preserved
- The text is a series of recipes with predictable vocabulary
- The paleography guide provided useful context for recipe-specific
  terms, abbreviations, and secretary hand conventions

Limitations observed:
- Image resolution constrains the ability to distinguish between
  abbreviated and fully spelled-out words
- Codicological features (insertions, deletions, changes of hand)
  require very careful attention to physical layout details that
  can be hard to discern at lower resolutions
- A higher-resolution image would likely resolve both errors

This result suggests the paleography guide is effective for guiding
transcription of clearly-written late 17th-century manuscripts.
Further testing on more challenging pages (faded ink, cramped hand,
heavy abbreviation, earlier date) would provide a fuller picture of
the guide's effectiveness.
