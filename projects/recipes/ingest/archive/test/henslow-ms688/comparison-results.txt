ERROR ANALYSIS
--------------
The 6 differences fall into three categories:

1. U/V ORTHOGRAPHIC CHOICES (3 errors: "use"/"vse", "uppon"/"vppon", "untill"/"vntill")
   The paleography guide notes that u and v were used interchangeably in this
   period, with v typically at the start of a word and u in the middle. The
   guided transcription applied this convention more consistently (using v at
   word-initial position), while the reference transcription uses u in some
   word-initial positions ("use", "uppon", "untill"). This is a genuine
   ambiguity in secretary hand: the letterforms for initial u and v can be
   nearly identical, and different transcribers may resolve them differently.
   These are debatable rather than clear-cut errors.

2. LETTER TRANSPOSITION (1 error: "voilett"/"violett")
   The reference reads "voilett" while the guided transcription reads "violett".
   In secretary hand, the letters i and o can look very similar (the secretary
   'e' is often confused with 'o', and minim strokes for 'i' can be ambiguous).
   The reference reading "voilett" preserves a less-standard spelling; "violett"
   may reflect modern bias toward the familiar spelling "violet".

3. LETTER MISREAD (1 error: "euenynde"/"euenynge")
   The reference reads "euenynde" (with a 'd') where the guided transcription
   reads "euenynge" (with a 'g'). In secretary hand, 'd' and 'g' can be
   confused because both have descenders and similar bowl shapes. The reference
   reading "euenynde" is the more unusual spelling, which suggests careful
   paleographic reading rather than modernization. This is a genuine misread.

4. WORD SEGMENTATION (1 error: "Takeone"/"Take one")
   The reference transcribes "Takeone" as a single run-together token, while
   the guided transcription separates them as "Take one". In the manuscript,
   the two words appear to run together without a clear space. The reference
   preserves this feature of the original. This reflects the semi-diplomatic
   convention of reproducing what appears on the page.
