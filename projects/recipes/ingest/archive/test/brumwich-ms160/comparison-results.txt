---------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTES ON THE TRANSCRIPTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Image quality: The test image is a JPEG of a two-page opening
   (manuscript pages 6-7) from the Wellcome Collection's IIIF server.
   The resolution is sufficient to read most text, though some words
   in the more compressed sections of page 7 required careful attention.

2. Hand characteristics: The manuscript is written in a relatively clear
   secretary hand. The scribe uses standard early modern conventions
   including u/v interchange ("uirtue", "uery", "euening"), long-s forms,
   and typical abbreviation marks.

3. Paleography guide utility: The guide was helpful for:
   - Recognizing secretary hand letterforms (especially e, c, r, s, h)
   - Understanding u/v and i/j interchange conventions
   - Identifying recipe-specific vocabulary (drame, scruple, sallendine,
     seethe, posset, pipkin, etc.)
   - Applying semi-diplomatic transcription conventions (preserving
     original spelling, capitalization, and punctuation)

4. Difficult readings in the manuscript:
   - "tueiah" (an eye medicine ingredient) - unusual spelling
   - "epyricum" - possibly a variant of "hepaticum" or similar
   - "ockley Cristy" - plant names with non-standard spelling
   - "sinckfold" - cinquefoil, a common herbal ingredient
   - "perdelicum" - unusual pharmaceutical term
   - "uolus metelorum" - Latin pharmaceutical term
   - "Salprunla" / "salprunlla" - variant spellings within the same entry
   - "[seised]" - flagged as uncertain in the reference transcription

5. Caveats about this test:
   - The JPEG image resolution may limit how well fine details (ink
     damage, faded passages, interlinear insertions) can be assessed.
   - A higher-resolution IIIF image (available via the info.json URL)
     would provide a more rigorous test of the model's paleographic
     capabilities, especially for damaged or faded sections.
   - The 100% accuracy result should be interpreted with caution: the
     model may be influenced by contextual expectations from the
     paleography guide's vocabulary lists and recipe conventions. A
     stricter test would use a manuscript page with more damaged,
     ambiguous, or unusual text.
   - The reference transcription itself may contain errors (it is a
     community transcription, not a triple-keyed scholarly edition).
     Perfect agreement with the reference does not necessarily mean
     perfect accuracy against the manuscript.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

This test demonstrates that Claude's vision capabilities, when guided
by the paleography reference, can produce highly accurate transcriptions
of clearly-written early modern recipe manuscripts. Recommended next
steps:

1. Test with a more challenging manuscript page (faded ink, damaged
   sections, compact hand, more abbreviations).
2. Test with a higher-resolution IIIF image to assess fine detail
   recognition.
3. Test without the paleography guide to measure the guide's specific
   contribution to accuracy.
4. Compare against a triple-keyed scholarly transcription (e.g., from
   EMROC) for a more authoritative reference baseline.
