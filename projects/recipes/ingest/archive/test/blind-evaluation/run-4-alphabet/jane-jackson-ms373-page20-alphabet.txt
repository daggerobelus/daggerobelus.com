HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Jane Jackson MS 373, Page 20 (opening showing two facing pages, right page numbered 9)
Scribe: A moderately neat italic-influenced secretary hand. The writing is more compact than some hands, with a slight rightward slant. Multiple recipes per page.

============================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
============================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with a rounded bowl. Formed with a curved stroke that closes at the top. Generally consistent.
  Example words: "and" (throughout), "Take" (recipe openings), "a" (throughout), "about" (left page)
  Confusion risks: Can resemble 'o' when hastily closed. Also slight risk with 'u' in rapid writing.
  Variants: One main form; slightly larger when initial.

b:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with a clear bowl at the baseline. The ascender sometimes loops leftward.
  Example words: "be" (various), "but" (various), "butter" (left page), "boyle" (right page)
  Confusion risks: With 'h' and 'l'. The closed bowl distinguishes 'b'.
  Variants: Consistent.

c:
  Letterform: A curved open stroke. In this hand, fairly clear and distinct.
  Example words: "cold" (if present), "Colewort" (if present), "come" (various)
  Confusion risks: Slight risk with 't' and 'r'. Distinguished by the curve.
  Variants: Capital 'C' is larger with a more sweeping stroke.

d:
  Letterform: Secretary-style 'd' with a leftward-looping ascender. The loop is moderately sized.
  Example words: "and" (throughout), "day" (various), "doe" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'cl' to modern readers.
  Variants: Consistent secretary form.

e:
  Letterform: Secretary 'e' — a reversed loop or backward curve. Small and round.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (throughout), "eye" (if present)
  Confusion risks: High risk with 'o'. The slight rightward opening helps distinguish.
  Variants: Consistent.

f:
  Letterform: Long descending stroke with a crossbar. In this hand, the crossbar is generally visible.
  Example words: "for" (various), "of" (throughout), "from" (various)
  Confusion risks: With long-s (ſ). The crossbar is the distinction.
  Variants: 'ff' = capital F.

g:
  Letterform: Rounded bowl with a descender looping left below the baseline.
  Example words: "good" (various), "ginger" (if present), "great" (if present)
  Confusion risks: Generally distinct.
  Variants: Consistent.

h:
  Letterform: Tall ascender with an open body — the right stroke descends and may dip below the baseline.
  Example words: "handfull" (various), "hott" (if present), "how" (if present)
  Confusion risks: With 'b' and 'l'. The open body is the distinguishing feature.
  Variants: Consistent.

i:

  Example words: "in" (throughout), "it" (throughout), "is" (if present)

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
============================================================

th: Very common, often written as thorn ('y') + e = "the". Also written as connected 't' + 'h'.
-tion: Written out in various spellings.
-er: The -er graph appears as a hook upstroke.
-es: Written out in full.
ff: Double-f = capital F.

DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
============================================================

1. An italic-influenced secretary hand with a slight rightward slant.
2. Multiple recipes per page, each beginning with an indented or slightly larger heading.
3. Some decorative capital letters, especially 'T' in "Take" and ornamental headings.
4. The left page has heavy water/staining damage on the lower third, rendering much of the text illegible.
5. The right page has some staining but the upper two-thirds are readable.
6. The scribe uses a mix of secretary and italic letter forms.
7. Line spacing is moderate — the text is somewhat compact.
8. The right page appears to be numbered "9" at the top right corner.
9. Recipe titles appear to include decorative elements (flourishes, larger script).
10. The ink varies from dark to faded, especially in damaged areas.

============================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (Highest to Lowest)
============================================================

1. LONG-S (ſ) vs. F
   Why similar: Both tall strokes. The crossbar on 'f' is the distinction.
   Distinguishing feature: Look for the crossbar.

2. E vs. O
   Why similar: Both small round strokes.
   Distinguishing feature: 'e' has rightward opening; 'o' is closed.

3. MINIM CONFUSION: I / U / N / M
   Why similar: All minim-based strokes.
   Distinguishing feature: Count minims; look for dots over 'i'.

4. B vs. H vs. L
   Why similar: All tall ascenders.
   Distinguishing feature: Bowl (b), open body (h), plain stroke (l).

5. WATER DAMAGE / FADED INK
   Special risk: Significant portions of both pages are affected by water/staining damage. The lower third of the left page is heavily stained, making many letterforms partially or fully illegible. This is the dominant challenge for this manuscript page.

6. SECRETARY D vs. CL
   Why similar: The leftward loop looks like two letters.
   Distinguishing feature: Single connected stroke for 'd'.
