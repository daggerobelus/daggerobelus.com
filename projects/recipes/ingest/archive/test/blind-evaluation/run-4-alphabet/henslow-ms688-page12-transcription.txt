My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge putt thereto a handefull of violett leaues
lang de beefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knitt
togeather of euerie one a like quantitye. . . . . . . .


. . . . . . . . For the pyles . . . . .

Take one handefull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the [Imbers?] untill they bee well rosted
then take them, and putt awaye the Colewort*e* leafe, and [grunde?]
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them together
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a [trusser?], and vse this
          morninge and euenynge. . . . . . . . .


. . . . . . . . . For the flixe . . . . . . . .

Take horsedounge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and putt it in a Clothe and laye itt to the fundament as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and sitt vppen untill yow [haue?] ease.

. . . . . . . . . . . For Defnes . . . . . .

Take blacke woole whiche growes vpon the flanke
     of a sheepe and [tarre?], and mingell them togeather
     and putt them into the eare.
