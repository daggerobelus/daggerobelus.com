My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge put thereto a handfull of violett leaues
and a gresse leaues Borrages leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit-
tegeathor of euerie one athe quantitye . . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handefull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the Imbres vntill they bee well rosted
then take them, and put awaye the Colwortte leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and put therto oyle of roses, and drinke them [togeather?]
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a trusser, and vse this
mornynge and euenynge . . . . . . .

. . . . . ffor the fflixe . . . . . . .

Take horsedonge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and put it in a Clothe and laye itt to the fundament as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and sitt vppen vntill yow haue ease

. . . . . . . . ffor Deafnes . . . . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche growes vpon the flanke
of a sheepe and Tarre and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare
