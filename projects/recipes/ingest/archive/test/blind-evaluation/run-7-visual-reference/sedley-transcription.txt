A Salue for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle oliue of the best Tr of [palm?]
lead very well [cerused?] with dust 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pot not glased & when the [ceruse?] is incor=
porated that the softe comes abouermost & put it vpon
a small fyre of coales contynuinge the said fyre an
houres & a halfe still stirringe it on a bolt of [boxe?]
fastned to a sticke of wood, ye make ye fire som=
what eager, till the rednys be turned into a graye
colour but ye must not leaue stirringes till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyle some=
darker, then drop it on a wooden trencher, & if it
nether cleue to ye fingers nor the trencher [feasibylly?]
enoughe, then bype floaters in that, the duracion of
thereon dipped will last 20 yeares, p.y glary ye [hue?]
this plaister laied to the stomack [proukes?] appetite and
takes awaye the paine of the stomack laied to the
bellie, tis a remedy for the [Collick?] laid to the
raines of the back it stops the bloody fluxe

[RIGHT PAGE - RECTO]

Openinge of the raines bloat of the Kidneys, [resolues?]
of the backe, it heales all swellinges, Aches & [bruises?], it
[frankes?] ffellons, Impostumes, pushes & [fyles?] them in-
ioynges applyed to the fundament it heales all diseases
thereon laid to the head, it is good for the [memorie?]

A Plod Cordiall for A woman in Childbed
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & [as manie?]
Syrup of Cloue gilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinnamon
water, &c a Spoonefull of [Gascon wine?] & [Mirouns?]
[Drauk?] mingle all these together and it is a souerein
good cordiall for any, especially for one that lyes
in Childbed

After A Totter

Take one Handfull of Sallanders & as much bay
salt as can take up & betwene ye finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp yt together in a
Morter & straines the iuice out & smalt the place [with?]
it put some of the hearbs vpon it, vse this goe 4
or 5 dayes together dressinge it 3 or 4 times in a Day
together &c
