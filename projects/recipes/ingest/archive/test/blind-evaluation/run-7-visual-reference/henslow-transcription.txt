My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge put thereto a handfull of violett leaues
and a gresse leaues Borrages leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knit-
tegeathor of euerie one athe quantitye . . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handefull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Colewort
leafe, and rake them in the Imbres vntill they bee well rosted
then take them, and put awaye the Colwortte leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and put therto oyle of roses, and grinke them [togeather?]
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a trusser, and vse this
mornynge and euenynge . . . . . . .

. . . . . ffor the fflixe . . . . . . .

Take horsedonge new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and put it in a Clothe and laye itt to the [fundament?] as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and sitt vppen vntill yow haue ease

. . . . . . . . ffor Deftues . . . . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche growes vpon the flanke
of a sheepe and [Farre?] and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare

Confidence Notes
================
- "a gresse leaues": The word before "leaues" in line 3 is partially unclear; it appears to read "gresse" but the letterforms are somewhat ambiguous. Could be "agresse" or "egresse".
- "knittegeathor": This appears to be a compound of "knit together" written as one word with variant spelling.
- "athe": Appears to read "athe" - possibly "a the" run together, meaning "of the".
- "mollett": The letterforms appear to be m-o-l-l-e-t-t; this may be "mallet" in variant spelling.
- [togeather?]: The final word of the long line in the pyles recipe is partially obscured by the page edge.
- [fundament?]: The word appears to read "fundament" but the latter portion is slightly crowded.
- [Farre?]: The word after "and" in the penultimate line is partially unclear; the letterforms suggest "Farre" or "farre" but this reading is uncertain.
- "Deftues": The recipe title appears to read "Deftues" - possibly "Deafness" in variant spelling.
- "grinke": This appears as written; may be "drinke" with the d resembling g, or may be a variant form.
- "Imbres": Appears to read "Imbres" (embers).
- "rake": May be "bake" - the initial letter could be either r or b in this hand.
