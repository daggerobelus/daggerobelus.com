HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Bulkeley MS 169, Page 17
================================================================

This is a flowing, somewhat rapid secretary hand. The text is dense with
relatively little spacing between lines. The page is numbered '7' in the
upper right corner. The text discusses lavender — its temperament, virtues,
and medical uses. This is a single continuous passage rather than separate
recipes. The hand is consistent throughout, suggesting a single scribe.

================================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS (a–z)
================================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment, formed with a curved stroke on left and
  short right-side stroke. Often quite round, approaching 'o' shape.
  Example words: "Lavander" (line 1, 2), "and" (throughout), "that" (line 2)
  Confusion risks: High confusion with 'o' when loosely formed
  Variants: Generally consistent

b:
  Letterform: Ascender with rounded bowl. The ascender curves slightly right.
  Example words: "be" (line 5), "blood" (line 14), "before" (line 21)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'h' or 'l'
  Variants: Consistent

c:
  Letterform: Small curved open stroke to the right.
  Example words: "comperaturs" (line 1), "cold" (line 14), "cause" (line 9)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'e' or 't'
  Variants: Capital C is larger

d:
  Letterform: Secretary-style with looped ascender curving back to the left.
  The bowl is round.
  Example words: "distilled" (line 8), "dead" (line 10), "disorders" (line 16)
  Confusion risks: The looped ascender can look like 'cl' to modern eyes
  Variants: Consistent secretary d

e:
  Letterform: Small reversed or backward-facing loop. In this hand, it can
  be quite angular at times, forming almost a pointed shape.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Lavender" (line 2), "degree" (line 2)
  Confusion risks: HIGHEST confusion with 'o' — both are small closed loops
  Variants: Sometimes more open, sometimes very tight

f:
  Letterform: Tall letter with ascender and descender, clear crossbar through
  the stem.
  Example words: "for" (throughout), "first" (line 3), "falling" (line 10)
  Confusion risks: Confused with long-s (ſ) — must check for crossbar
  Variants: Double-ff not prominent

g:
  Letterform: Round bowl with descending loop below baseline curving left.
  Example words: "good" (line 4), "griefe" (line 6), "generally" (line 24)
  Confusion risks: Descender could be confused with 'y' descender
  Variants: Consistent

h:
  Letterform: Ascender on left with curved stroke descending right. The
  right stroke sometimes extends below the baseline.
  Example words: "hott" (line 2), "head" (line 6), "her" (line 1)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'b' if right stroke is short
  Variants: Consistent

i:
  Letterform: Single minim stroke, sometimes dotted.
  Example words: "in" (throughout), "is" (throughout), "it" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Confused with first stroke of 'm', 'n', 'u'
  Variants: Consistent

j:
  Letterform: Not distinctly separate from 'i' on this page.
  Example words: N/A
  Confusion risks: Interchangeable with 'i'
  Variants: N/A

k:
  Letterform: Ascender with angular strokes meeting at midpoint.
  Example words: "kindes" (line 16)
  Confusion risks: Distinctive
  Variants: Rare on this page

l:
  Letterform: Tall simple ascender, slightly curved.
  Example words: "Lavander" (throughout), "let" (line 11), "like" (line 17)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with unlooped 'b' or 'h'
  Variants: Consistent

m:
  Letterform: Three minim strokes connected at top with arches.
  Example words: "migram" (line 9), "much" (line 15), "make" (line 13)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'in' or 'ni' sequences
  Variants: Capital M not present on this page

n:
  Letterform: Two minim strokes with arch at top.
  Example words: "not" (throughout), "nor" (line 13), "never" (line 21)
  Confusion risks: 'n' vs 'u' — arch at top (n) vs curve at bottom (u)
  Variants: Consistent

o:
  Letterform: Round closed loop at x-height.
  Example words: "of" (throughout), "on" (line 6), "or" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'e' — highest risk pair
  Variants: Generally round

p:
  Letterform: Descender below baseline with bowl at upper right.
  Example words: "palsie" (line 10), "prescribed" (line 16), "put" (line 20)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'q'
  Variants: May have special p form for "pre-", "per-"

q:
  Letterform: Round bowl with rightward descender.
  Example words: "qualitie" (line 7)
  Confusion risks: Distinguished from 'p' by descender direction
  Variants: Rare

r:
  Letterform: Two forms — (1) short stroke with rightward shoulder,
  (2) 2-shaped form after rounded letters.
  Example words: "rather" (line 7), "remedy" (line 6), "regard" (line 15)
  Confusion risks: 2-shaped r can look like 'z'
  Variants: Both forms present

s:
  Letterform: Long-s (ſ) initially/medially — tall like 'f' without clear
  crossbar. Round-s at word end.
  Example words: long-s in "stiffninges" (line 3); round-s in "comperaturs" (line 1)
  Confusion risks: Long-s confused with 'f' — this hand's biggest challenge
  as the crossbar distinction is sometimes subtle
  Variants: Two positional forms

t:
  Letterform: Short upstroke with crossbar near top.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "that" (throughout), "to" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'c' if crossbar faint
  Variants: Consistent

u:
  Letterform: Two minims connected at bottom.
  Example words: "unto" (line 6), "up" (line 4), "use" (line 15)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'n'
  Variants: Used medially; 'v' at word start

v:
  Letterform: Two strokes meeting at point below.
  Example words: "very" (line 6), "vertues" (line 8)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'u' or 'r'
  Variants: Used word-initially

w:
  Letterform: Broad double-v form.
  Example words: "water" (line 8), "which" (line 16), "was" (line 4)
  Confusion risks: Distinctive
  Variants: Consistent

y:

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Very frequent. Formed as separate letters. Thorn (þ) may appear as 'y'
    in common words.

-es: Terminal -es graph (looped downstroke) appears at word ends.

-er: The -er graph (hook upstroke) appears in several words.

-tion/-sion: May appear with abbreviation marks (tilde over vowel).

-ll: Double-l: "distilled", "all", "fill".

-tt: Double-t: "hott", "putt", "gett".

-ss: Double-s: possible in "griefes", "stiffninges".

DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Dense text with relatively close line spacing.
2. Flowing, somewhat rapid hand — not as careful as a formal document.
3. Consistent use of secretary 'd' with looped ascender throughout.
4. Long-s (ſ) and 'f' are sometimes very similar — careful distinction needed.
5. The page discusses a single topic (lavender) in extended prose.
6. Catchword "that" at bottom right of page indicates continuation.
7. The hand maintains legibility despite speed.
8. Title line in slightly larger hand: "of Lavander, her comperaturs,"

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. e / o — HIGHEST RISK
   Why: Both are small round/looped forms at x-height. This hand writes
   both quite similarly. Must look for slight angular quality in 'e' vs.
   full round loop of 'o'. Context may be needed for disambiguation.

2. long-s (ſ) / f — HIGH RISK
   Why: This hand's long-s and 'f' are very similar. Both tall with
   ascender/descender. The crossbar in 'f' is sometimes subtle. Must
   examine carefully each time. This is particularly important because
   this page discusses medical remedies with many words containing both
   letters.

3. n / u
   Why: Both two-minim forms. In this flowing hand, the distinction
   between arch-at-top (n) and curve-at-bottom (u) can blur. Critical
   for reading words like "unto", "upon", "never", "under".

4. m / in / ni
   Why: Three minims — 'm' or 'i+n'/'n+i'. Count carefully.

5. c / t
   Why: Both short forms. 'c' open curve; 't' has crossbar. If crossbar
   is light, they merge.

6. d / cl
   Why: Secretary 'd' has looped ascender that can look like 'c' + 'l'
   to readers unfamiliar with the form. In this hand, the loop is
   distinct enough to identify, but it is a risk.

7. r / v
   Why: Standard 'r' form with rightward shoulder can resemble 'v'
   in some formations.
