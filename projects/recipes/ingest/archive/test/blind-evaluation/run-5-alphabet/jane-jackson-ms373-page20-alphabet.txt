HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Jane Jackson MS 373, Page 20
================================================================

This image shows a two-page opening. The hand is a relatively clear italic/
secretary hybrid with good spacing. However, significant water staining and
damage affects the lower third of both pages, making portions illegible.
The right page is numbered (appears to be page 9 or similar in upper right).
Multiple recipes appear on each page. The hand is practiced and fairly
consistent, with good word spacing.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS (a–z)
================================================================

a:
  Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with curved left stroke. Generally
  clear and round in this hand.
  Example words: "and" (throughout), "a" (throughout), "Take" (recipe starts)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'o' when loosely formed
  Variants: Consistent

b:
  Letterform: Ascender with rounded bowl on right.
  Example words: "be" (various), "black" (left page), "butter" (various)
  Confusion risks: Can look like 'l' or 'h'
  Variants: Consistent

c:
  Letterform: Small open curve to the right.
  Example words: "cold" (various), "cure" (various)
  Confusion risks: Can be confused with 'e' or 't'
  Variants: Consistent

d:
  Letterform: Secretary 'd' with looped ascender curving back left.
  Example words: "dried" (left page), "and" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Looped ascender looks like 'cl'
  Variants: Consistent secretary d

e:
  Letterform: Small rounded or slightly angular form. In this hand, 'e'
  tends to be slightly more angular than 'o'.
  Example words: "the" (throughout), "Take" (throughout)
  Confusion risks: Confused with 'o' — highest risk
  Variants: Sometimes more open at the right side

f:
  Letterform: Tall with ascender/descender and crossbar.
  Example words: "for" (throughout), "from" (various)
  Confusion risks: Confused with long-s (ſ)
  Variants: Consistent

g:
  Letterform: Round bowl with descending loop below baseline.
  Example words: "good" (various), "green" (various)
  Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'y'
  Variants: Consistent

h:

i:

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. e / o — HIGHEST RISK
   Why: Both small rounded forms. In this hand, 'e' tends to be slightly
   more angular, but in rapid writing they merge. Must look for the
   angular quality in 'e'.

2. long-s (ſ) / f
   Why: Both tall with ascender/descender. Need to check crossbar carefully.

3. n / u
   Why: Both two-minim forms. Must check arch direction.

4. m / in / ni
   Why: Three minims need careful counting.

5. c / t
   Why: Small forms; crossbar in 't' sometimes light.

6. h / b
   Why: Both have ascenders; bowl vs. curve distinguishes them.

7. d / cl
   Why: Secretary d's loop can look like c+l.

8. WATER DAMAGE — SPECIAL RISK
   This is not a letterform confusion but a major factor on this page.
   The water staining makes text in the lower portions completely
   illegible in many areas. Where partial letterforms are visible
   through staining, readings are especially uncertain.
