HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Jane Jackson MS 373, Page 20
=====================================================

This is a two-page opening (verso and recto) with significant water/stain
damage on the lower portions of both pages. The hand is a relatively neat
italic-secretary hybrid, more toward the italic end than the other manuscripts.
The letterforms are generally well-spaced and moderately sized. The page
number appears to be "9" or similar at top right.

The left page has multiple recipe entries. The right page also has
multiple entries. Both pages show significant water damage/staining in
the lower third, making those sections partially or fully illegible.

INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
======================

a - Open-topped, moderate size, italic-influenced. More modern-looking
    than pure secretary forms.
    Example words: and, a, are, also, after
    Confusion risks: o, u (less so than in other MSS due to italic clarity)
    Variants: One main form

b - Standard form with ascender and rounded bowl.
    Example words: be, both, black, boyle
    Confusion risks: h, l
    Variants: One main form

c - Open curved form.
    Example words: cold, cure, compound
    Confusion risks: e (less confusion than in secretary hands)
    Variants: One form

d - Italic d with straight or slightly curved ascender (not looped like
    pure secretary). More modern appearance.
    Example words: day, drink, dose
    Confusion risks: Minimal (italic d is quite distinct)
    Variants: One form

e - More standard/italic form than secretary reversed loop. Easier to
    distinguish from o.
    Example words: the, these, every
    Confusion risks: Still some risk with o, but less than in other MSS
    Variants: One form

f - Standard form with crossbar.
    Example words: for, from, first, faire
    Confusion risks: Long-s (less common in this more italic hand)
    Variants: One form

g - Bowl with descender curving left.
    Example words: good, great, ginger, ground
    Confusion risks: y (less so than secretary)
    Variants: One form

h - Standard form, ascender and body.
    Example words: have, halfe, hott, hold
    Confusion risks: b, ln
    Variants: One form

i - Simple minim with dot.
    Example words: it, in, is
    Confusion risks: n, u minims (less severe in italic)
    Variants: One form

k - Less elaborate than pure secretary k.
    Example words: know, keep
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

l - Vertical stroke, clear ascender.
    Example words: let, like, last, lay
    Confusion risks: h, b (less so in italic)
    Variants: One form

m - Three minims, generally clear.
    Example words: make, much, more
    Confusion risks: in, ni
    Variants: One form

n - Two minims.
    Example words: not, new, nor, next
    Confusion risks: u
    Variants: One form

o - Round form, clearer than in secretary.
    Example words: of, or, one, on
    Confusion risks: e (reduced), a
    Variants: One form

p - Standard descender with bowl.
    Example words: put, pound, pint
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

q - Standard form.
    Example words: quarter, quart
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

r - Standard forms.
    Example words: receipt, roses
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One or two forms

s - Primarily round s (italic influence); some long-s.
    Example words: said, sugar
    Confusion risks: f (less common confusion in this hand)
    Variants: Round s predominates

t - Standard crossbar form.
    Example words: take, the, that
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

u/v - Standard forms.
    Example words: upon/vpon, use
    Confusion risks: n (for u)
    Variants: v initial, u medial

w - Double-v form.
    Example words: with, water, well
    Confusion risks: Minimal
    Variants: One form

y - Standard form with descender.
    Example words: you, your, yet
    Confusion risks: g, thorn (less common)
    Variants: One form

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
===================================

th - Common
st - Sometimes joined
& - Ampersand used
ff = F (limited)

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (highest to lowest)
===================================================

1. n / u - Still the primary minim confusion, even in this italic hand
2. e / o - Reduced but still present
3. m / in / ni - Minim confusion
4. Long-s / f - When long-s appears
5. h / b / l - Ascender confusion
6. c / r - In some positions
7. y / g - Descender confusion
8. WATER DAMAGE: The lower portions of both pages are heavily stained,
   making letterforms completely invisible in those areas. This is the
   dominant factor affecting readability, more than any letterform confusion.
