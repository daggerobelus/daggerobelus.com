HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Henslow MS 688, Page 12
=================================================

This is a clear, practiced secretary hand. The scribe writes with consistent
letterforms and good spacing between words. The hand is relatively upright
with moderate use of abbreviations.

LOWERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

a - Round, open bowl. Two forms: (1) a simple single-stroke a with an open top,
    (2) occasionally a more rounded form. Clearly formed in most instances.
    Example words: handsfull, Take, and, quantitye
    Confusion risks: o (when hastily written)

b - Upright ascender with a round bowl at the baseline. The ascender is straight
    or slightly curved to the right.
    Example words: Brothe, before, Borrage, blacke
    Confusion risks: l (the ascender), h (when bowl is open)

c - Small rounded letter, open to the right. Formed with a single curved stroke.
    Example words: Caunteberries, Consumptione, Coleworte
    Confusion risks: e (when tightly formed), r (in some positions)

d - Two forms: (1) a secretary d with the ascender looping back to the left,
    forming a characteristic curved top. (2) Occasionally a simpler form.
    Example words: handsfull, and, morninge
    Confusion risks: e (the loop can look like e), cl combination

e - A reversed loop form typical of secretary hand. The scribe makes a
    backward-facing oval. Quite consistent throughout.
    Example words: the, leaues, before, one
    Confusion risks: o (especially when small), c

f - Long descender below the line with a crossbar. The crossbar distinguishes
    it from long-s.
    Example words: for, fflixe, fundamente, flanke
    Confusion risks: long-s (when crossbar is faint)

g - A round bowl sitting on the line with a descender that curves left then
    loops back. Distinctive shape.
    Example words: Borrage, grinde, groues
    Confusion risks: y (descender shape)

h - Tall ascender with the lower part curving right and sometimes dipping
    below the baseline. The descender loop is characteristic.
    Example words: handsfull, the, them, horselounge
    Confusion risks: b (similar ascender), ln combination

i - A simple minim with a dot above. Dot placement is fairly consistent.
    Example words: in, it, violett
    Confusion risks: first stroke of m, n, u; l (when no dot visible)

k - An elaborate form with a looped ascender and a kick at the baseline.
    Example words: Take, blacke
    Confusion risks: Distinctive enough to avoid confusion

l - A simple tall stroke, ascending above the midline. Sometimes has a
    slight curve at the top.
    Example words: leaues, laye, Coleworte
    Confusion risks: b (if a stray mark looks like a bowl), tall s

m - Three minims joined. The scribe forms them evenly.
    Example words: them, morninge, morter
    Confusion risks: ni, in, mi (minim confusion)

n - Two joined minims. Similar to m but with one fewer stroke.
    Example words: one, and, in, new
    Confusion risks: u (inverted n), m (extra minim)

o - A round letter, closed or nearly closed. Formed with a single
    clockwise stroke.
    Example words: one, of, Coleworte, oyle
    Confusion risks: a (when open at top), e (when small)

p - A descender below the line with a bowl at the midline. Sometimes the
    descender has a slight curve.
    Example words: pyles, putt, put
    Confusion risks: Fairly distinctive

q - Round bowl with a descender, similar to p but mirrored. The tail goes
    right.
    Example words: quantitye
    Confusion risks: p (mirror image)

r - Multiple forms: (1) a short stroke with a shoulder, like a modern r.
    (2) a 2-shaped form in some positions. Both are used.
    Example words: Brothe, roses, morter, rested
    Confusion risks: v (the 2-shaped form), c, n

s - Two forms: (1) long-s in initial and medial positions, looking like f
    but without a full crossbar. (2) round s at end of words.
    Example words: leaues (round final s), Strawberrie (long medial s)
    Confusion risks: f (long-s vs f is the main challenge)

t - A short stroke with a crossbar. The crossbar is sometimes connected to
    the next letter.
    Example words: Take, the, to, them
    Confusion risks: c (when crossbar is faint)

u/v - Used interchangeably as per period convention. v at word start, u in
    middle. Formed as a rounded trough (u) or a pointed stroke (v).
    Example words: vpon (upon), euerie (every), violett, leaues
    Confusion risks: n (minim confusion with u)

w - A broad letter formed with multiple strokes, often with loops.
    Example words: wrappe, well, Coleworte (the w in worte)
    Confusion risks: Distinctive enough

x - Crossed strokes forming an X shape.
    Example words: fflixe
    Confusion risks: Rarely seen, distinctive

y - A letter with a descender curving left below the baseline. Has a dot
    above in some instances.
    Example words: you, your, pyles, oyle
    Confusion risks: thorn (y-shaped thorn means "th")

z - Not clearly visible on this page.
    Confusion risks: N/A

UPPERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

B - Large, decorative form with double bowls. Seen in "Brothe," "Borrage."
C - Large curved initial stroke. Seen in "Caunteberries," "Consumptione," "Coleworte."
F - Rendered as ff (double-f = capital F). Seen in "ffor," "fflixe."
M - Large form seen in "My."
S - Large sweeping form seen in "Strawberrie."
T - Large decorated form seen in "Take" (multiple instances).

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
----------------------------------

th - Very common. The t and h are usually connected. The scribe does not
     appear to use thorn (y = th) on this page; "the" is spelled out.
sh - Seen in "sheepe." Long-s followed by h.
ff - Double-f represents capital F: "ffor," "fflixe"
-es graph - The scribe sometimes uses a terminal -es graph (a looped
           downstroke) at the ends of words like "leaues," "roses."
-er graph - A hook at end of words, visible in some instances.
ll - Double l clearly formed as two tall strokes.

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)
---------------------------------------------------

1. f / long-s (f vs s) -- The most dangerous confusion. Both have tall
   ascenders; f has a crossbar, long-s does not. When ink is heavy the
   crossbar can be hard to see, or a stray mark can look like one.

2. Minim confusion (m/n/u/i) -- Classic secretary hand problem. Three
   minims = m, two = n or u, one = i. Must count carefully.

3. e / o -- The reversed-loop e can resemble o when small or hastily formed.

4. c / r / t -- These small letters can look similar, especially c and r
   in certain positions.

5. a / o -- When a is written with a closed bowl it can look like o.

6. b / h -- Similar ascenders; the bowl of b vs the curve of h can be
   ambiguous.

7. y / thorn -- If the scribe uses thorn (y = th), then y must be
   distinguished from thorn by context. On this page, this does not appear
   to be a significant issue.
