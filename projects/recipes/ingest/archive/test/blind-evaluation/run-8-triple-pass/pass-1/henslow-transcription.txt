My lorde of Caunterberries
. . . . Brothe for a Consumptione . . . . . .

Take like as before sauinge putt thereto a handsfull of violett leaues
Langdebeefe leaues Borrage leaues and Strawberrie leaues, knitt
togeather of euerie one alike quantitye . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the pyles . . . .

Take one handsfull of mollett leaues, and wrappe them in a Coleworte
leafe, and rake them in the [imbers?] untill they bee well [rosted?]
then take them, and putt awaye the Coleworte leafe, and grinde
them in a morter, and putt to yt oyle of roses, and grinde them together
then take it and laye itt to the pyles withe a trusser, and vse this
morninge and euenynge . . . . . .

. . . . ffor the fflixe . . . . . .

Take [horsedounge?] new made and frie itt withe sweete butter
and putt it in a clothe and laye itt to the fundamente as hott
as you maye suffer ytt, and sitt vppon untill you haue ease

. . . . . . ffor Deafnes . . . . .

Take blacke wolle whiche groues vppon the flanke
of a sheepe and [Tarre?] and mingell them togeather
and putt them into the eare

Confidence Notes
================

1. "imbers" (line 8): The word begins with what looks like a minim-based
   letter. It could be "imbers" (embers) or possibly "timbers" though there
   does not appear to be a t before it. The reading is uncertain because
   the initial letterform is partially obscured. Flagged as [imbers?].

2. "Langdebeefe" (line 3): On closer inspection, the word before "leaues"
   appears to read "Langdebeefe" (lang-de-boeuf, literally "ox-tongue," a
   plant also known as bugloss). The letterforms show L-a-n-g-d-e-b-e-e-f-e.
   This is a recognized early modern plant name. The initial capital L and
   the tall d are consistent with this reading.

3. "Tarre" (penultimate line): The word appears to start with a capital T
   and reads as "Tarre" (tar), which makes sense in context (black wool
   and tar for an ear remedy), but the letterforms are slightly smudged.
   Flagged as [Tarre?].

4. "mollett" (line 7): The letterforms clearly show m-o-l-l-e-t-t. This
   appears to be "mallet" or "mollet" — a variant spelling. Transcribed as seen.

5. "Deafnes" (line 15): On closer re-examination, the heading reads "Deafnes"
   (deafness), not "Ditties." The recipe that follows (putting black wool
   and tar into the ear) is clearly a remedy for deafness. The letterforms
   show D-e-a-f-n-e-s. The initial reading was incorrect because I misread
   the secretary e-a as i-t-t in the decorative heading script.

6. "rosted" (line 8): Changed from "rested" to "rosted" (roasted). Cooking
   leaves in embers until "rosted" is more consistent with the recipe
   process. The first vowel letterform is ambiguous between e and o in
   this hand, but the o reading is more likely. Flagged as [rosted?].

7. "trusser" (line 11): The letterforms clearly show this word. It appears
   to be a medical implement or cloth for applying a poultice.

8. "horsedounge" (line 13): Initially read as "horselounge." On review, the
   middle letterforms could show d-o-u-n-g (horse dung, a common poultice
   ingredient) rather than l-o-u-n-g. The d/l distinction is ambiguous in
   this hand. Flagged as [horsedounge?].
