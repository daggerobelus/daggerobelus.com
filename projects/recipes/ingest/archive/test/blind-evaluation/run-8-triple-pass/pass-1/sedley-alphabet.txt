HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Sedley MS 534, Page 13
=================================================

This page shows TWO different hands. The left page (Recipe XXVII, "A Salve
for sundry sores") appears to be in one hand — a fairly neat, slightly
forward-leaning secretary/italic mix. The right page (Recipes XXVIII and
XXIX) appears to be in a different, somewhat more italic hand. I will
document the primary hand from the left page and note differences in the
right-page hand.

LEFT PAGE HAND (Recipe XXVII)
-----------------------------

LOWERCASE LETTERS

a - Round, open bowl form. Consistent and clearly formed.
    Example words: Salve, and, Spanish, all
    Confusion risks: o (when closed), u

b - Upright ascender with round bowl. Clear and well-formed.
    Example words: best, but
    Confusion risks: l (ascender alone)

c - Small curved letter open to right.
    Example words: cerus, colour, course
    Confusion risks: e, r

d - Secretary d with looped ascender curving left, but also some italic d
    forms with straight ascender leaning right.
    Example words: dust, drop, dried
    Confusion risks: cl combination (the loop)

e - Mostly italic e form (not fully reversed loop). A small rounded letter.
    Example words: the, sores, leade
    Confusion risks: o (when small), i (when cramped)

f - Descending stroke with crossbar. Clear.
    Example words: for, faire, fast
    Confusion risks: long-s (without crossbar)

g - Round bowl with descending tail that curves left.
    Example words: good, great
    Confusion risks: y (descender)

h - Ascender with curved right stroke. Sometimes the right stroke
    descends below the baseline.
    Example words: houres, halfe
    Confusion risks: b, ln

i - Single minim with dot.
    Example words: it, in, incorporate
    Confusion risks: minim confusion (m, n, u)

k - Looped form with ascender.
    Example words: make
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

l - Tall single stroke.
    Example words: leade, last, all
    Confusion risks: b, tall-s

m - Three minims.
    Example words: make, mould, must
    Confusion risks: ni, in (minim)

n - Two minims.
    Example words: not, new, nor
    Confusion risks: u, m

o - Round closed letter.
    Example words: of, ounces, oyle
    Confusion risks: a, e

p - Descender with bowl at midline.
    Example words: put, plaster, pare
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

r - Short stroke with shoulder. Also 2-shaped form in some positions.
    Example words: red, roses, remedy
    Confusion risks: v (2-shaped form), n

s - Long-s (medial/initial) and round s (final).
    Example words: sores (round final s), stirring (long-s)
    Confusion risks: f (long-s)

t - Short upright stroke with crossbar, sometimes connected to following letter.
    Example words: the, to, that, turned
    Confusion risks: c

u/v - v at word start, u in middle. Standard convention.
    Example words: vppon, untill, verie
    Confusion risks: n (minims)

w - Broad, multi-stroke letter.
    Example words: will, with, well
    Confusion risks: Distinctive

y - Descending stroke with curve, sometimes dotted.
    Example words: you, your, yett
    Confusion risks: thorn

RIGHT PAGE HAND (Recipes XXVIII, XXIX)
---------------------------------------

This hand is more italic in character. Letters are generally more upright
and slightly rounder than the left-page hand. Key differences:

- d tends to be more italic (straight ascender) rather than secretary
- e is more clearly formed as a small rounded letter
- The hand is slightly less cramped with better word spacing
- Capital letters are more decorative

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
----------------------------------

th - Commonly written, t and h connected. No thorn usage visible.
sh - long-s + h
-es graph - Some terminal -es graphs visible
st - s and t connected
ct - c and t sometimes ligatured

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)
---------------------------------------------------

1. f / long-s -- As always, the primary confusion risk.
2. Minim confusion (m/n/u/i) -- Especially in cramped passages on the
   left page.
3. e / o -- Small letterforms can be ambiguous.
4. c / r / t -- Similar small letterforms.
5. Right-margin text -- The right margin of the left page has smaller,
   more cramped text that is harder to read.
6. Two-hand confusion -- Must track which hand is writing where.
