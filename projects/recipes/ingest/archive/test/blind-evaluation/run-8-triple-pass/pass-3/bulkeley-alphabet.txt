HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Bulkeley MS 169, Page 17
=================================================

General Observations:
- Dense, continuous prose text on a single page
- Secretary hand written at speed — more cursive than Henslow
- Some italic influence in certain letter forms
- Text concerns lavender and its medical properties/virtues
- Ink is fairly dark throughout but some areas show lighter strokes
- The hand is fairly consistent but somewhat hurried in places
- Page number "7" visible at top right

LOWERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

a: Open-topped, rounded bowl. Written quickly, sometimes looks angular.
   Examples: "Lauander", "and", "that"
   Confusion risks: Can resemble u when hastily formed
   Variants: Generally consistent

b: Ascender with bowl. Written fairly clearly.
   Examples: "be", "but", "blood"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with l or h
   Variants: One main form

c: Small open curve. Sometimes angular in this rapid hand.
   Examples: "composition", "called", "cause"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with r or t
   Variants: One form

d: Secretary d with looped ascender curving left.
   Examples: "and", "distilled", "degree"
   Confusion risks: Standard cl confusion with looped d
   Variants: One form

e: Secretary reversed-loop e. In this hand, sometimes barely distinguished from o.
   Examples: "the", "be", "temperature"
   Confusion risks: HIGH confusion with o — this scribe's e and o are very similar
   Variants: Generally consistent but ranges from open to closed loop

f: Long descender with crossbar.
   Examples: "for", "flowers", "first"
   Confusion risks: Long-s
   Variants: One form

g: Small bowl with descending tail.
   Examples: "good", "great", "given"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with y
   Variants: One form

h: Ascender with curved descending stroke.
   Examples: "the", "that", "have"
   Confusion risks: Could resemble b
   Variants: One form

i: Short minim, sometimes dotted.
   Examples: "in", "is", "it"
   Confusion risks: Minim confusion
   Variants: One form

k: Elaborate form with loops.
   Examples: "kindes"
   Confusion risks: Could resemble h
   Variants: One form

l: Tall straight ascender.
   Examples: "Lauander", "light", "like"
   Confusion risks: Could be confused with b
   Variants: One form

m: Three minims.
   Examples: "many", "much", "made"
   Confusion risks: Minim sequence confusion
   Variants: One form

n: Two minims.
   Examples: "not", "nor", "new"
   Confusion risks: u confusion
   Variants: One form

o: Round bowl, closed.
   Examples: "of", "or", "one"
   Confusion risks: Secretary e
   Variants: One form

p: Descender with bowl.
   Examples: "put", "parts"
   Confusion risks: Special p for pre-/pro-/per-
   Variants: Standard and possibly abbreviated

q: Bowl with descending rightward tail.
   Examples: "qualitie"
   Confusion risks: Could resemble g
   Variants: One form

r: Multiple forms visible — standard and 2-shaped.
   Examples: "rather", "remedy"
   Confusion risks: v confusion with 2-shaped r
   Variants: Two forms

s: Long-s and round-s.
   Examples: long-s in various positions; round s finally
   Confusion risks: f confusion
   Variants: Two positional forms

t: Short stroke with crossbar.
   Examples: "the", "that", "to"
   Confusion risks: c confusion
   Variants: One form

u/v: Standard positional usage.
   Examples: "vpon", "vnder", "humours"
   Confusion risks: u/n minim; v/r
   Variants: Positional

w: Wide double-v.
   Examples: "water", "with", "which"
   Confusion risks: Minimal
   Variants: One form

y: Descender below line.
   Examples: "you", "yet"
   Confusion risks: Thorn
   Variants: One form

UPPERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

L: Large, elaborate form in "Lauander"
T: Large form in "The"
B: Large form in "But"

COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
----------------------------------

th: Very common, connected
-es: Terminal graph
-er: Hook upstroke
-tion: With tilde or superscript
wth: Abbreviation for "with"
&: Ampersand present throughout

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)
---------------------------------------------------

1. e / o — Extremely high risk in this rapid hand; the scribe's e and o are nearly identical at times
2. Minim sequences (m/n/u/i) — Dense, rapid writing makes minims very difficult
3. long-s / f — Standard high risk
4. c / r / t — Small angular forms overlap
5. d / cl — Looped secretary d
6. a / u — Open-topped a looks like u
7. h / b — Similar ascenders
8. y / thorn
9. r / v
