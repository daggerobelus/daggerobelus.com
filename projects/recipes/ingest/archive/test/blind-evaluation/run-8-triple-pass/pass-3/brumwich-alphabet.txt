HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET: Brumwich MS 160, Page 10
=================================================

General Observations:
- Two-page spread with dense, small writing
- Very rapid, cramped secretary hand — among the most difficult to read
- Multiple recipes on each page
- Ink varies — some parts darker, some faded
- The hand is somewhat irregular, suggesting speed or less formal context
- Page numbers visible: 6 (left), 7 (right)
- Some recipes have titles/headings that are slightly larger or underlined
- Overall legibility is LOW compared to Henslow

LOWERCASE LETTERS
-----------------

a: Hastily formed, often open-topped. Very angular in places.
   Examples: "and", "Take", "water"
   Confusion risks: HIGH confusion with u and o
   Variants: Ranges from rounded to angular

b: Ascender with small bowl. Written quickly.
   Examples: "be", "blood", "boile"
   Confusion risks: l, h
   Variants: One form

c: Small curve, often barely distinguishable.
   Examples: "cold", "cure"
   Confusion risks: e, r, t — very high confusion in this hand
   Variants: One form

d: Looped secretary d, written quickly.
   Examples: "and", "good"
   Confusion risks: cl
   Variants: One form

e: Secretary e, very small and often closed.
   Examples: "the", "Take"
   Confusion risks: VERY HIGH confusion with o
   Variants: Ranges from open to completely closed

f: Descender with crossbar.
   Examples: "for", "from"
   Confusion risks: long-s
   Variants: One form

g: Small bowl with descending tail.
   Examples: "good", "great"
   Confusion risks: y, q
   Variants: One form

h: Ascender with curved descender.
   Examples: "the", "that"
   Confusion risks: b
   Variants: One form

i: Minim, rarely dotted in this hand.
   Examples: "in", "it"
   Confusion risks: Extreme minim confusion
   Variants: One form

k: Angular strokes off ascender.
   Examples: "Take"
   Confusion risks: h
   Variants: One form

l: Straight ascender.
   Examples: "like", "let"
   Confusion risks: b, h
   Variants: One form

m: Three minims — often barely distinguished.
   Examples: "make", "much"
   Confusion risks: Extreme — minims run together
   Variants: One form

n: Two minims.
   Examples: "not", "and"
   Confusion risks: u — nearly identical
   Variants: One form

o: Small round bowl.
   Examples: "of", "one"
   Confusion risks: e
   Variants: One form

p: Descender with bowl.
   Examples: "put", "pint"
   Confusion risks: Special p
   Variants: Standard

r: Multiple forms, often 2-shaped.
   Examples: "red", "roses"
   Confusion risks: v
   Variants: Two forms

s: Long-s and round-s.
   Examples: standard distribution
   Confusion risks: f
   Variants: Two forms

t: Short crossbar.
   Examples: "the", "to"
   Confusion risks: c
   Variants: One form

u/v: Standard positional.
   Confusion risks: n nearly identical to u
   Variants: Positional

w: Double-v form.
   Examples: "water", "with"
   Variants: One form

y: Descending tail.
   Examples: "you", "your"
   Confusion risks: thorn, g
   Variants: One form

RANKED CONFUSION RISK SUMMARY (Highest to Lowest)
---------------------------------------------------

1. e / o — Nearly indistinguishable in many instances
2. u / n / i / m minims — Extreme confusion; this is the hardest aspect of this hand
3. long-s / f
4. c / r / t / e — Multiple small letters overlap
5. a / u / o — Open a, u, and o can all look similar
6. d / cl
7. h / b / l — Ascender confusion
8. y / thorn / g
9. r / v
