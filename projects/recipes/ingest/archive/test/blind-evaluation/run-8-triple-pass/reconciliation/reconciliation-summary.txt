RECONCILIATION SUMMARY
Blind Transcription Test — Run 8
===================================

METHODOLOGY:
Three independent agents each transcribed the same five manuscript pages
without seeing each other's work. This reconciliation compared all three
passes line by line, word by word, using majority rule to produce a
consensus transcription. After all five manuscripts were reconciled,
the vocabulary reference (~19,000 attested early modern recipe words) was
used to verify uncertain readings.

==========================================================================

MANUSCRIPT 1: HENSLOW MS 688, Page 12
--------------------------------------
3-way agreements:   128 words
2-way agreements:    23 words
3-way disagreements:  6 words
Flagged for review:   3 words

Inter-pass consistency: HIGH
This manuscript produced the strongest agreement across all three passes.
The hand is relatively clear and the recipe structure is straightforward.
Disagreements were mostly minor spelling variations (euenynge/eueninge/
evenynge; horsedounge/horsedeunge/horsedonge). Only one substantive
3-way disagreement required flagging: "sitt/lay/soe vppon" — resolved
to "sitt" via vocabulary verification.

Vocab verification changes: 1
- "[sitt/lay/soe?]" resolved to "sitt" (vocab confirmed)

OBSERVATIONS:
=============

1. Manuscript difficulty is the dominant factor in inter-pass agreement.
   The three passes agree readily on well-written, clearly laid out text
   (Henslow) but diverge dramatically on small, cramped, or damaged text
   (Brumwich, Jane Jackson).

2. The vocabulary reference was most useful for resolving specific 3-way
   disagreements where one pass's reading could be confirmed by the word
   list (e.g., "calcined," "Dramm," "Mirre," "Mastick"). It was less
   useful for the most damaged passages, which had too little agreement
   to work with.

3. Recipe structural words ("Take," "of," "and," "&," "halfe," "ounces")
   and well-known ingredient names (violetts, Cowslipps, Treacle, sugar,
   Cinnamon, pepper) are the most robust readings across all manuscripts,
   even in very difficult hands.

4. The 3-pass approach with majority rule works well for moderately difficult
   manuscripts (Henslow, Sedley, upper Bulkeley) but reaches its limits
   with extremely challenging material. For Brumwich and Jane Jackson,
   the original manuscript images would be essential for further progress.
