HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Jane Jackson MS 373, Page 20
================================================================

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS:
This is a two-page opening from a recipe book in a late 16th or early 17th
century mixed hand. The writing is relatively small and compact compared
to the Henslow manuscript. The hand mixes secretary and italic features.
The scribe writes neatly but with closely-spaced lines. There is significant
water/stain damage at the bottom third of both pages, rendering those
portions largely illegible.

The right page is numbered "9" at the top right. Multiple recipes are
visible on each page, with recipe headings set off from the body text.

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Very common; 't' and 'h' closely joined. Thorn may appear.

-ing/-inge: Common word ending.

ff: Double-f for capital F appears (e.g., "ffor").

-es: Terminal '-es' generally spelled out.

================================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Compact mixed hand, smaller than the Henslow manuscript.
2. Secretary 'd', 'e', and 's' combined with italic features.
3. Close line spacing means ascenders and descenders from adjacent lines
   can interfere.
4. Significant water/stain damage at the bottom third of both pages.
5. Recipe headings are slightly larger or underlined.
6. The scribe uses "ffor" (double-f capital F) for "For."
7. Multiple hands may appear on the page (common in recipe books where
   multiple family members contributed).

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. MINIMS (i/n/m/u) — HIGH RISK
   Why: The compact writing and close spacing make minim strokes
   particularly difficult to distinguish.
   Distinguishing features: Dots over 'i' are inconsistent; context
   and word shape help.

2. e/o — HIGH RISK
   Why: Secretary 'e' and 'o' are similar in this hand, especially
   in words where both are plausible.
   Distinguishing features: 'e' tends to have a slight angle on the left.

3. long-s/f — MODERATE-HIGH RISK
   Why: The crossbar on 'f' can be subtle in this smaller hand.
   Distinguishing features: Look carefully for crossbar.

4. c/t — MODERATE RISK
   Why: Similar forms when crossbar is faint.
   Distinguishing features: Crossbar on 't'.

5. Secretary r/v — MODERATE RISK
   Why: The '2'-shaped 'r' can resemble 'v'.
   Distinguishing features: Size and context.

6. a/u — MODERATE RISK
   Why: In rapid writing, 'a' can open and look like 'u'.
   Distinguishing features: Closing stroke on 'a'.

7. d loop/cl — LOW-MODERATE RISK
   Why: Secretary 'd' loop could be read as 'cl'.
   Distinguishing features: Single stroke vs. two strokes.

8. g/y — LOW RISK
   Why: Both descend but 'g' has a clear bowl.
   Distinguishing features: Bowl of 'g'.
