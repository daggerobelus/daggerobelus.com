HAND-SPECIFIC ALPHABET CHART
Manuscript: Henslow MS 688, Page 12
================================================================

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS:
This is a large, decorative secretary hand, likely from the mid-to-late
16th century. The writing is very well-formed and highly legible. The
scribe uses large decorative capitals at the start of recipe headings
and the word "Take." The hand is bold with thick downstrokes, and the
letter spacing is generous. Ink is dark brown/black. This is one of the
most legible manuscripts in this set.

The page contains four recipes:
1. "My lorde of Caunterberries Brothe for a Consumptione"
2. "For the pyles"
3. "For the flixe"
4. "For Deafnes"

Decorative dot patterns (".......") are used as line fillers and
separators between recipes.

================================================================
INDIVIDUAL LETTERFORMS
================================================================

a:
- Letterform: Single-compartment 'a' with a rounded bowl. Very clearly
  formed. The closing stroke at top right is consistently visible.
- Example words: "Caunterberries" (line 1), "handfull" (line 3),
  "and" (throughout), "Take" (multiple)
- Confusion risks: Very low; clearly differentiated from other letters
- Variants: Single consistent form

b:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with a clear rounded bowl at the baseline.
  Well-formed and distinctive.
- Example words: "Brothe" (line 2), "Borrage" (line 4), "bee" (line 8)
- Confusion risks: Minimal; clearly differentiated from 'l' and 'h'
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital 'B' in "Brothe" and "Borrage"

c:
- Letterform: Open curve, clearly formed. Slightly angular at the top.
- Example words: "Caunterberries" (line 1), "Consumptione" (line 2),
  "Coleworte" (line 7)
- Confusion risks: Could be confused with 't' or 'r' in rapid writing,
  but this scribe is careful
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital 'C'

d:
- Letterform: Secretary 'd' with a looped ascender curving to the left.
  Very consistently formed in this hand.
- Example words: "handfull" (line 3), "and" (throughout), "Deafnes" (line 16)
- Confusion risks: The loop is very clear, low confusion risk
- Variants: Decorative capital 'D' in "Deafnes"

e:
- Letterform: Secretary 'e' — backward-facing loop. Well-formed and
  consistently distinguishable from 'o'.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "leaues" (lines 3-4), "euerie" (line 5),
  "embers" (line 8)
- Confusion risks: Low; this scribe keeps 'e' and 'o' quite distinct.
  The 'e' has a clear angular left side.
- Variants: Single form

f:
- Letterform: Long descender with clear crossbar. Well-differentiated
  from long-s.
- Example words: "for" (multiple headings), "of" (throughout),
  "handfull" (line 3), "flixe" (line 12)
- Confusion risks: Long-s/f distinction is clear in this hand because
  the crossbar is prominent
- Variants: Decorative capital 'F' in "For"

g:
- Letterform: Round bowl with a clear descending loop curving left.
- Example words: "sauinge" (line 3), "Borrage" (line 4), "grinde" (line 9)
- Confusion risks: Low; the bowl above the line is distinctive
- Variants: Single form

h:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with a curved stroke at the baseline. The
  descender sometimes dips slightly below the line.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "thereto" (line 3), "handfull" (line 3),
  "them" (line 8)
- Confusion risks: Low in this hand; 'h' is well-differentiated
- Variants: Single form

i:
- Letterform: Simple minim stroke, sometimes with a clear dot above.
  This scribe tends to dot the 'i' more consistently than many.
- Example words: "in" (lines 7, 9), "it" (line 11), "itt" (line 11)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion is present but mitigated by spacing
- Variants: Used interchangeably with 'j'

j:
- Letterform: Extended 'i' form; not clearly present on this page.
- Example words: None clearly identified
- Confusion risks: See 'i'
- Variants: See 'i'

k:
- Letterform: Tall ascender with looped angular strokes. Decorative form
  in "Take."
- Example words: "Take" (multiple), "like" (line 3), "knitt" (line 4)
- Confusion risks: The decorative form in "Take" is highly distinctive
- Variants: Ornamental capital form in "Take"

l:
- Letterform: Tall ascender, straight with a slight curve. Clear and
  consistent.
- Example words: "lorde" (line 1), "leaues" (lines 3-4), "laye" (line 11)
- Confusion risks: Could be confused with 'b' but this scribe's 'b'
  has a clear bowl
- Variants: Single form

m:
- Letterform: Three clear minim strokes, fairly wide.
- Example words: "My" (line 1), "them" (line 8), "morter" (line 9),
  "morninge" (line 12)
- Confusion risks: Moderate minim risk, but spacing helps
- Variants: Decorative capital 'M' in "My"

n:
- Letterform: Two minim strokes.
- Example words: "and" (throughout), "one" (line 6), "new" (line 14)
- Confusion risks: Minim confusion with 'u'
- Variants: Single form

o:
- Letterform: Round, fully closed loop. Clearly differentiated from 'e'.
- Example words: "of" (throughout), "one" (line 6), "oyle" (line 9)
- Confusion risks: Low; 'o' is consistently round in this hand
- Variants: Single form

p:
- Letterform: Descender with a round bowl at the baseline. Clear.
- Example words: "putt" (lines 3, 9), "pyles" (line 6)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

q:
- Letterform: Round bowl with rightward-curving descender.
- Example words: "quantitye" (line 5)
- Confusion risks: Low
- Variants: Single form

r:
- Letterform: Secretary 'r' — appears as a short upstroke, sometimes
  '2'-shaped. Also italic 'r' in some positions.
- Example words: "Brothe" (line 2), "Borrage" (line 4), "roses" (line 9)
- Confusion risks: Secretary 'r' can look like 'v' but this scribe is
  generally clear
- Variants: Both secretary and italic forms

s:
- Letterform: Two forms: long-s (tall, at word-start and middle) and
  round-s (at word-end). Very clearly differentiated from 'f' by the
  absence of a crossbar on long-s.
- Example words: "sauinge" (long-s, line 3), "leaues" (round-s, line 3),
  "Strawberrie" (long-s in middle, line 4)
- Confusion risks: Long-s/f distinction is relatively clear in this hand
- Variants: Long-s and round-s

t:
- Letterform: Short stroke with clear crossbar. Well-formed.
- Example words: "the" (throughout), "thereto" (line 3), "Take" (multiple)
- Confusion risks: Low; crossbar is clear
- Variants: Decorative capital 'T' in "Take"

u/v:
- Letterform: 'v' at word-start, 'u' in the middle. Both clear.
- Example words: "vntill" (line 8), "violett" (line 3), "leaues" (u, line 3)
- Confusion risks: Standard u/v confusion; 'u' can look like 'n' in
  minim sequences
- Variants: Positional variants

w:
- Letterform: Double-v, broad and clear. Sometimes decorative.
- Example words: "wrappe" (line 7), "well" (line 8), "withe" (line 11)
- Confusion risks: Very low; distinctive width
- Variants: Single form

x:
- Letterform: Two crossing strokes. Appears in "flixe" (line 12).
- Example words: "flixe" (line 12)
- Confusion risks: Minimal
- Variants: Single form

y:
- Letterform: Descending stroke with a dot or short upstroke.
- Example words: "My" (line 1), "oyle" (line 9), "they" (line 8)
- Confusion risks: Could resemble thorn; low risk in this hand
- Variants: Single form; decorative capital in "My"

z:
- Letterform: Not clearly present on this page.
- Example words: None
- Confusion risks: N/A
- Variants: N/A

================================================================
COMMON COMBINATIONS AND LIGATURES
================================================================

th: Common; 't' and 'h' written closely but usually as separate strokes.
    No thorn usage observed on this page.

-tt: Doubled consonants are common: "putt", "itt", "knitt"

-inge/-ynge: Common word ending. Very clearly formed.

ff: Double-f for capital F not observed on this page; the scribe uses
    decorative capitals instead.

-es: Terminal '-es' is spelled out.

& : Ampersand not prominently used on this page; "and" is spelled out.

================================================================
DISTINCTIVE FEATURES OF THIS HAND
================================================================

1. Large, bold secretary hand — one of the most legible in this set.
2. Highly decorative capital letters, especially T (in "Take"), M, F, C, B, D.
3. Thick downstrokes and thin upstrokes typical of a well-cut quill pen.
4. Generous line spacing and letter spacing.
5. Decorative dot patterns (".......") used as recipe separators and line fillers.
6. Recipe headings are centered and slightly larger than body text.
7. Consistent letterforms throughout — the scribe does not show fatigue
   or rushing.
8. Doubled consonants are carefully written (e.g., "putt", "itt", "knitt").
9. Dark brown/black ink, well-preserved.

================================================================
CONFUSION RISK RANKING (highest to lowest)
================================================================

1. MINIMS (i/n/m/u) — MODERATE RISK (highest for this hand)
   Why: Even in this clear hand, minim strokes are the most similar
   letters. The generous spacing reduces but does not eliminate this risk.
   Distinguishing features: Dots over 'i' are more consistent than in
   most hands; spacing helps.

2. e/o — LOW-MODERATE RISK
   Why: The secretary 'e' has a backward loop that could in theory
   resemble 'o', but this scribe keeps them quite distinct.
   Distinguishing features: 'e' has an angular left side; 'o' is
   uniformly round.

3. long-s/f — LOW RISK
   Why: The crossbar on 'f' is prominent in this hand, making
   distinction relatively easy.
   Distinguishing features: Clear crossbar on 'f'.

4. c/t — LOW RISK
   Why: The crossbar on 't' is clear and consistent.
   Distinguishing features: Crossbar on 't'.

5. Secretary r/v — LOW RISK
   Why: This scribe's 'r' and 'v' are generally well-differentiated.
   Distinguishing features: 'v' is broader with a clear V-shape.

6. l/b — LOW RISK
   Why: The bowl on 'b' is clear.
   Distinguishing features: 'b' has a visible bowl.
