Of Lavander spike, the temperature,
Lavander is hott & drye, & that in the third degree.
& is of a thin substance, consisting of many airie
& spirituall partes. therefore it is good to bee giuen
any way against the diseases of the head, &
especially those which cause them of originall or
by wyndes not of a abundance of humors, but
therefore of a qualitie onely.
The Vertues
The distilled water of Lavander smelt vnto
or the temples and fore=head bathed therewith
is a refreshinge to them that haue the cataplasie
a light migram, & to them that haue the fallinge
sicknes, & that lose to some much. But where
there is abundance of humors, especially mixt
w*i*th bloud, it is not then to bee so safe, neyther
is the composition to bee taken, nether made of

General: This page is relatively legible compared to other manuscripts in

Gerard's Herball, 1597) about lavender spike, its temperature and virtues.

Line-by-line notes:

- "giuen" (line 4): Early modern spelling of "given" with u/v convention;
  confirmed in vocab reference.

- "cataplasie" (line 12): This word is difficult. The letterforms clearly
  show 'c-a-t-a-p-l-a-s-i-e'. Not found in vocab reference. Could be a
  variant of "catalepsie" (catalepsy) or "cataplasme" (cataplasm). The
  letterforms favor "cataplasie" but this may be a scribal variant.

  of "migraine."

- "whith" (line 17): This appears to read "whith" rather than "which" —

- "nipt" (line 18): Not found in vocab reference. The letterforms clearly
  show n-i-p-t. This likely means "nipped" (as in bruised/crushed herbs).

- "rashly" (line 19): Not found in vocab reference but is a standard
  English word. The letterforms clearly support this reading.
