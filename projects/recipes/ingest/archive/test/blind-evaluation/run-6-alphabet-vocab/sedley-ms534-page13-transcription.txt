[LEFT PAGE - VERSO, page 20]

A Salve for sundry sores called
the Leaden plaister
XXVII

Take one pt of oyle olive of the best Two ounces of
lead very well calcinated w*i*th dust. 12 ounces of
Spanish sope incorporate all these well together in
an earthen pott well glazed & when the colour is
[changed?] that the sope seemes appropriate & put it vpon
a small fire of coales continuinge the said fire an
houres & a halfe still stirring it w*i*th a bolt of boxe
fastned to a sticke of wood, yt make yt fire some
what niger, till the reddish be turned into a gray
colour but yt must not leave stirrings till the
matter be turned into the coulour of oyster somwhat
darker, then drop it on a marble trencher or if it
neither cleaue to yt fingers nor the trencher tis sufficiently
enough then dippe it together in that, the [characters?] of
thereon [dipped?] will last 20 yeares, pt of glory yt know
this plaister laied to the stomack expells appetite &
takes away the paines of the stomack laied to the
reines, tis a remedy for the collicke laied to the
veines of the backe it stops the bloudy fluxe

[RIGHT COLUMN OF LEFT PAGE - continuation of virtues]

openings of the veines that of the Kidnyes workes of
the backe, it heals all swellings, Aches & bruises, &
Rankes blisters, Imposthumes, pocks & [scabs?] them &
[brings?] applyed to the fundament it heales all diseases
thereon laied to the head, it is good for the wormes

[RIGHT PAGE - RECTO]

A [Plud?] Cordiall for A wooman in Childbed
XXVIII

Take a quarter of a pint of Cowslip water & 2 ounces of
Syrop of Clovegilliflowers & a pennyworth of Cinamon water
& a Spoonefull of Claret wine & 4 or 5 Mornings
Drams mingle all these together and it is a soueraigne
& cordiall for any especially for one that lyes
in Childbed

Pills for the Gall
XXIX

For [...] [...] [...] and [...]
After a Tetter

Take one handfull of Sallendine & as much bay
salt as can take up betweene yt finger & Thumbe &
the iuice of halfe a Lemon stamp yt together in a
Morter & straine the iuice out & [smalt?] the place [yt?]
it put some of the hearbe vpon it, vse this 2 or 3 or 4
times together dressing it 3 or 4 times in a Day
together &c.

CONFIDENCE NOTES
================================================================

General: This is the most legible manuscript in the set. The italic hand
is clear and well-spaced, and the page is in excellent condition with no
water damage or significant fading. Most of the text can be read with
high confidence.

Recipe XXVII - "A Salve for sundry sores called the Leaden plaister":

- "pt" (line 1): This appears to be an abbreviation, likely for "pint"
  (p*in*t). High confidence in the letterforms.

- "calcinated" (line 2): The letterforms clearly show c-a-l-c-i-n-a-t-e-d.
  "calcined" and "calcinatum" are in the vocab reference; "calcinated"
  is not listed but is a standard pharmaceutical term meaning reduced
  to powder by heat. High confidence.

- "sope" (lines 3, 5): High confidence. This is an early modern spelling
  of "soap." "sope" confirmed in vocab reference.

- "incorporate" (line 3): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "[changed?]" (line 5): Low-moderate confidence. The first word of this
  line is partially obscured. "changed" fits the context (the colour
  changing when the soap is appropriate) but the letterforms are not
  entirely clear.

- "boxe" (line 7): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference. Refers
  to boxwood (a hard wood used for stirring implements).

- "niger" (line 9): Moderate-high confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.
  Means "blacker" or "darker" (from Latin). The letterforms clearly show
  n-i-g-e-r.

- "oyster" (line 11): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference. The
  colour comparison to oyster shell is a standard description.

- "trencher" (line 12): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "cleaue" (line 13): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.
  Means "to stick or adhere."

- "sufficiently" (line 13): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "[characters?]" (line 14): Low confidence. The word is difficult to read.
  The letterforms are partially unclear.

- "thereon [dipped?]" (line 15): Low-moderate confidence. These words
  are somewhat unclear.

- "pt of glory" (line 15): Low confidence. This phrase is unclear. "pt"
  is likely the same abbreviation as line 1. "glory" does not make
  obvious sense in context; this may be a misreading.

- "stomack" (lines 16-17): High confidence. "stomacke"/"stomack" confirmed
  in vocab reference.

- "collicke" (line 18): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "bloudy fluxe" (line 19): High confidence. Both words confirmed in
  vocab reference. "Bloody flux" = dysentery.

Right column of left page:

- "Kidnyes" (line 1): High confidence. "kidneyes" confirmed in vocab
  reference.

- "Imposthumes" (line 3): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.
  Means abscesses.

- "Rankes" (line 3): Moderate confidence. "ranke" is in the vocab reference.
  Could also be reading "Rancke" or similar.

- "[scabs?]" (line 3): Low-moderate confidence. The word after "pocks &"
  is somewhat unclear. "scabs" is confirmed in vocab reference and fits
  the context.

- "[brings?]" (line 4): Low confidence. The word before "applyed" is unclear.

- "wormes" (line 5): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

Recipe XXVIII - "A [Plud?] Cordiall for A wooman in Childbed":

- "[Plud?]" (heading): Low confidence. The first word of this recipe title
  is unclear. The letterforms suggest P-l-u-d, but "Plud" is not in the
  vocab reference. It may be "Blud" (blood), "Good," "Fluid," or another
  word. The decorative capital makes the first letter difficult to identify.

- "Cowslip water" (line 1): High confidence. Both confirmed in vocab reference.

- "Clovegilliflowers" (line 2): High confidence for the letterforms. The word
  is compound: "Clove-gilliflowers" (gilliflowers confirmed in vocab reference).
  Clove gilliflowers are carnations/pinks used in cordials.

- "pennyworth" (line 2): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "Cinamon" (line 2): High confidence. "cinamon" confirmed in vocab reference.

- "Mornings Drams" (lines 3-4): Moderate confidence. "Drams" confirmed in
  vocab reference. "Mornings" appears to modify "Drams" — possibly meaning
  doses to be taken in the morning.

- "soueraigne" (line 4): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "Childbed" (line 6): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

Recipe XXIX - "Pills for the Gall" / "After a Tetter":

- "Sallendine" (line 1): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference
  as an early modern spelling of celandine (Chelidonium). Initially read
  as "Sallanders" but re-examination against the vocab reference led to
  this corrected reading — the letterforms support "Sallendine" and this
  is attested in the vocab reference.

- "Tetter" (subheading): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.
  A tetter is a skin disease (eczema, ringworm, etc.).

- "Thumbe" (line 2): High confidence. "thumbe" confirmed in vocab reference.

- "Lemon" (line 3): High confidence. "lemon"/"lemmon" confirmed in vocab
  reference.

- "Morter" (line 4): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "[smalt?]" (line 4): Low confidence. The word after "& " before "the place"
  is unclear. "smalt" is not in the vocab reference as a verb. It may be
  "small" or "smear" or another word. The letterforms are ambiguous.

- "hearbe" (line 5): High confidence. Confirmed in vocab reference.

- "&c." (line 7): High confidence. Standard abbreviation for "et cetera."
