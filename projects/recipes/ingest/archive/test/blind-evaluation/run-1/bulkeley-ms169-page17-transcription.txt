General observations:
- This is a single page of continuous prose about the medicinal
  properties of lavender, not a recipe per se but a herbal
  description of the plant's "temperature" (in humoral medicine)
  and its "vertues" (medicinal uses)
- The hand is a flowing secretary hand with reasonably clear
  letterforms, though some words are compressed
- The page ends mid-sentence with "that" as a catchword, indicating
  the text continues on the next page
- The humoral medical framework is evident: lavender is "hott & drye"
  in the "third degree" — standard Galenic classification

- "Catalepsie" — this reading is fairly confident; catalepsy was a
  recognized medical condition in the period
- "comp*os*ition" — expanded abbreviation; the middle portion is
  abbreviated
- "meassuge" — uncertain; may be "measure" or "message"
- "Apothecaries" — fairly confident reading but the word is slightly
  compressed
- "compotions" — uncertain; likely "compositions" or "compotions"
  (compounds/mixtures)
- "omdant" — uncertain; likely "abundance" or "redundance" but the
  letterforms don't clearly match either

The text discusses warnings about the misuse of lavender preparations,
particularly in cases where the patient has an abundance of humors
mixed with blood. The author cautions against giving distilled waters
and herbal compositions without proper bloodletting or purging first.
This is consistent with early modern medical practice.
